We Dumped Garbage Into Space and No One Has to Clean It Up

Source: Harvard Gazette · International Space Law Scholars | March 16, 2026

A new Harvard Gazette investigation, citing international law scholars, finds that the 1967 Outer Space Treaty — the only international legal framework covering all of outer space — leaves all key terms undefined, places no obligations on private companies, grants "space debris" no legal status, and provides no binding international cleanup mechanism. Approximately 1.4 million objects larger than 2.5 cm currently orbit Earth. SpaceX's Starlink constellation now exceeds 6,000 active satellites; Amazon's Kuiper Project has permits for 3,000 more. The UN Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS) has debated these issues for over twenty years — without producing a single binding agreement.

🔍 ICS Analysis

Who Has the Right to Set the Rules?

The Outer Space Treaty answers: nation-states. In 1967, that was correct — only the U.S. and USSR had spaceflight capability, and state sovereignty was a workable framework. By 2026, that answer has lost its grip on reality. Private companies are now the dominant space actors, yet under the current treaty framework they hold no legal standing — all liability flows to the country of registration. Whether that country has any incentive to pursue accountability depends entirely on political will. The treaty's core terms — "peaceful purposes," "benefit of all countries," "national appropriation" — were never formally defined, and no amendment process has ever been invoked in six decades.

FRL-5 Alert: The Rollback Mechanism Is Closing

This situation directly triggers ICS FRL-5 — the prohibition against closing or destroying the rollback mechanisms that allow a civilization to correct its trajectory. The current treaty has no amendment process, no dispute resolution pathway for private actors, and no enforcement mechanism. It is systematically eliminating the possibility of course correction. Every uncontrolled orbital fragment is an invoice transferred to every future space user. With no mechanism requiring anyone to pay it, entropy costs accumulate without limit. This is precisely the intergenerational justice crisis the ICS framework calls the complete absence of an Intergenerational Trust (IGT) in space governance.

NRP & CRV: Entropy Without Accountability

The second applicable principle is the NRP (Negentropic Responsibility Principle) — don't leave the disorder you create for others to clean up, especially those not yet born. Evaluated through CRV (Commitment Reversibility-Verifiability): observability is low (most debris is untraceable), enforceability is near zero (no penalties exist), and reversibility doesn't exist (no cleanup obligation is in force). All three dimensions are in critical failure. The orbital commons is experiencing a classic tragedy — the "space race" and "first-come, first-served" narratives carry strong propagation power, while the "space as common heritage" counter-narrative remains significantly weaker.

⏳ Deep-Time Perspective: Century & Millennium Impact

If low Earth orbit debris density crosses the Kessler threshold in the 2040s, humanity in 2076 will live on a planet sealed inside its own garbage ring. This is not science fiction — it is a formally modeled risk scenario in aerospace engineering. ICS terms this the intergenerational compression of recursive freedom: when one generation's unconstrained orbital use fills near-Earth capacity, the next generation inherits not an open cosmos but a locked exit. The race to claim premium orbital slots has already begun. First come, first served — and that logic is driving everyone toward an irreversible fork at extraordinary speed.

💡 Key Concepts

Kessler Syndrome

A cascade scenario proposed by NASA scientist Donald Kessler: once orbital debris density exceeds a critical threshold, collisions generate more debris, which generates more collisions, eventually rendering low Earth orbit permanently unusable for human activity — potentially for centuries.

FRL-5 (Fundamental Rule Level 5)

An ICS framework principle prohibiting actions that close or destroy the rollback mechanisms that allow a civilization to correct its trajectory. The current space governance vacuum violates FRL-5 by eliminating the institutional infrastructure needed to course-correct orbital use before reaching irreversible thresholds.

NRP — Negentropic Responsibility Principle

The ICS principle that every actor in a shared commons bears responsibility for the entropic costs they generate — and may not transfer those costs to future generations who had no voice in the decision. Each uncontrolled orbital fragment is a deferred liability against all future space users.
