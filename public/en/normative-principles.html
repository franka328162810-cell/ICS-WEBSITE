Cosmic Responsibility Ethics: Normative Principles Framework

Cosmic Responsibility Ethics is the normative framework of Interstellar Civilization Studies, consisting of three core principles and a set of forbidden red lines. These principles are not arbitrary moral intuitions, but normative guidance rigorously derived from the New Three Views, providing an ethical foundation for cosmic-scale action.

From New Three Views to Normative Principles

Bridge Argument

Moving from the New Three Views (descriptive propositions) to normative principles (normative propositions) requires a bridge argument. The core argumentative structure is as follows:

Core Arguments

From New Cosmology to Relational Responsibility:

  • Premise 1: Existence is relational (NC-1)
  • Premise 2: We are participants in cosmic processes (NC-3)
  • Conclusion: We have relational responsibility to other beings in the cosmos

From New Life View to Negentropy Protection:

  • Premise 1: The essence of life is the maintenance and growth of local negentropy (NL-2)
  • Premise 2: Negentropy is scarce and valuable in the universe
  • Conclusion: We have responsibility to protect and promote negentropy in the cosmos

From New Cognition to Reversibility Requirement:

  • Premise 1: We face deep uncertainty (NK-3)
  • Premise 2: Reasonable action under deep uncertainty should preserve error-correction possibilities
  • Conclusion: Our actions should maintain reversibility as much as possible

Four Normative Components

Cosmic Responsibility Ethics consists of the following four components:

Component English Name Abbreviation Core Claim
Recursive Freedom Recursive Freedom Principle RFP Exercise of freedom must not systematically undermine conditions for others' freedom
Negentropy Responsibility Negentropy Responsibility Principle NRP Responsibility to protect and enhance cosmic negentropy stock
Reversibility Reversibility Principle REV Prioritize reversible actions under deep uncertainty
Forbidden Red Lines Forbidden Red Lines FRL Absolute prohibitions that must not be crossed under any circumstances

Relationships Among Principles

There exists a hierarchical relationship among the three principles and forbidden red lines:

Forbidden Red Lines (FRL)
Absolute prohibitions, non-negotiable
Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP)
Core principle, priority application
Negentropy Responsibility (NRP) │ Reversibility (REV)
Derived principles, mutually complementary

Hierarchy Explanation

  • Forbidden Red Lines: Absolute priority, must not be crossed under any circumstances
  • Recursive Freedom Principle: Core principle, foundation for other principles
  • Negentropy Responsibility Principle and Reversibility Principle: Derived from Recursive Freedom Principle, guide action in specific contexts

Core Maxim

"Freedom is the guardianship of possibility"

This maxim is the quintessential expression of the Recursive Freedom Principle:

  • Freedom is not arbitrary action, but responsible action
  • Possibility is not just current options, but includes the possibility space of future agents
  • Guardianship means protecting, maintaining, and expanding possibilities, not consuming them

Relationship with Traditional Ethics

Cosmic Responsibility Ethics absorbs insights from multiple ethical traditions, but has unique integration:

Tradition Absorbed Insights Unique Development
Kantian Deontology Universalization principle, respect for autonomy Extension to cross-substrate, cross-temporal agents
Consequentialism Focus on outcomes, long-term impacts Revision under deep uncertainty
Virtue Ethics Character and practical wisdom Cultivation of cosmic-scale virtues
Care Ethics Relationality, responsibility Extension to cosmic relationality
Environmental Ethics Care for non-human beings Extension to cosmic negentropy

Application Domains

Cosmic Responsibility Ethics provides normative guidance for the following domains:

  • AI Governance: How to develop AI to enhance rather than undermine recursive freedom?
  • Space Governance: How to conduct space exploration while protecting cosmic negentropy?
  • Existential Risk Management: How to address risks that could terminate human civilization?
  • Intergenerational Governance: How to be responsible to future generations?
