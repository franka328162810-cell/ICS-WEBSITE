II. The Core Thesis of This Chapter

Core Thesis (Central Thesis):

The four core features of the New View of Cognition (Participatory Cognition, the Sociality of Knowledge, deep uncertainty, and Revisability of Knowledge) can be integrated into a consistent formal system NK (New Knowledge), which:

1. Consistency: the four subsystems PC, SK, DU, and RK are mutually consistent, with no contradictions

2. Non-redundancy: the four subsystems are mutually independent and cannot be reduced to one another

3. Emergence: the integrated system NK has new properties that no single subsystem possesses

4. Completeness: NK completely characterizes the New View of Cognition proposed in Chapter 3 of Volume One

5. Bridge character: NK provides a formal basis for the derivation from epistemology to normativity

§16.1 The Necessity and Strategy of Integration

§16.1.1 Why Integration Is Needed

Chapters 12 through 15 constructed four independent formal systems respectively:

SystemCore ContentNumber of Main Theorems
PCParticipatory Cognition7
SKthe Sociality of Knowledge8

These four systems are each independent, but together they constitute the New View of Cognition. However, independent construction brings three problems:

Problem One: the consistency problem

Are the four systems mutually consistent? For example:

  • PC asserts the participatory character of cognition (the cognizer constitutes the world)
  • SK asserts the sociality of knowledge (knowledge is negotiated by a community)
  • Are these two compatible? Is participation individual or collective?

Problem Two: the redundancy problem

Do the four systems contain redundancy? For example:

  • Is the revisability of knowledge in RK already implied by the deep uncertainty of DU?
  • Is the sociality of SK already implied by the participation of PC?

Problem Three: the completeness problem

Do the four systems completely characterize the New View of Cognition? Have some important features been omitted?

The necessity of integration:

Only through systematic integration can we:

1. Verify the consistency of the four systems

2. Eliminate possible redundancy

3. Test the complete characterization of the New View of Cognition

4. Reveal new properties of the integrated system

§16.1.2 The Strategy of Integration

We adopt a Layered Integration Strategy:

Layer One: linguistic integration

Construct a unified formal language that contains all the symbols of the four subsystems.

Layer Two: semantic integration

Construct a unified semantic structure such that the semantics of the four subsystems are mutually compatible.

Layer Three: axiomatic integration

Construct a unified axiom system that contains all the axioms of the four subsystems and adds Bridge Axioms to connect the different subsystems.

Layer Four: theorem integration

Derive Emergent Theorems specific to the integrated system; these theorems require the cooperation of multiple subsystems in order to be proved.

§16.1.3 The Challenges of Integration

The integration process faces three main challenges:

Challenge One: symbol conflict

Different subsystems may use the same symbol to denote different concepts. For example:

Challenge Two: semantic incompatibility
