Volume I: Philosophical Foundations
Establishing the Ontological, Epistemological and Normative Foundations

Volume I of The Normative Reconstruction of Interstellar Civilization lays the philosophical cornerstone of the entire six-volume work. It establishes the “New Three Views” (New Cosmology, New Biology, New Epistemology) as the ontological–epistemological foundation, from which three core normative principles are derived, followed by a systematic dialogue with the principal objections raised against the first edition.

Core Challenges

This volume opens by confronting the fundamental challenge facing human civilization: once our technological reach extends to interstellar scales, once artificial intelligence may surpass human intelligence within this century, and once our decisions may carry consequences lasting millennia, how do we construct a normative framework fit for such vast spatiotemporal scales?

Traditional ethics — grounded in anthropocentrism, terrestrial scale and short-term horizons — proves inadequate when confronting AGI governance, extraterrestrial contact and intergenerational justice. The first edition left a gap between “survival” and “value”; the second edition repairs it with an explicit argumentative chain.

Three core puzzles: What is the cosmic foundation of normativity? How is rationality sustained under deep uncertainty? How is long-range responsibility attributed?

Content Structure

Part I · Problems and Methods (Chapter 0 + Introduction)

States the three core puzzles that constitute the starting point of the work. The author candidly declares methodological commitments and boundaries, adopting an “engineering narrative” that fuses philosophical argumentation with formalized expression.

Part II · Independent Foundations of the New Three Views (Chapters 1–5)

Each of the three views now receives an independent entry point, resolving the “circular co-construction” weakness of the first edition:

  • New Cosmology (NC) — Grounded in process philosophy and quantum mechanics: the basic pattern of the universe is relation rather than substance, existence is becoming rather than static being, and observers are participants rather than spectators.
  • New Biology (NL) — Breaks the limits of carbon-based life, proposes substrate-independent life, defines life as negentropy organization, and treats consciousness as a continuous spectrum rather than a binary classification.
  • New Epistemology (NK) — Integrates 4E cognitive science (embodied, embedded, extended, enactive), stresses the participatory, social and situated nature of cognition, and acknowledges the ineliminability of deep uncertainty.

Part III · Rigorous Derivation of Normative Principles (Chapters 6–10)

Derives three core principles from the New Three Views and integrates them into a “Cosmic Responsibility Ethics” framework:

  • Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP) — Any agent exercising freedom must preserve the possibility for other agents to exercise equivalent freedom — unifying freedom and responsibility.
  • Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP) — Grounded in the second law of thermodynamics: intelligent agents bear responsibility for maintaining and increasing cosmic negentropy, giving physics an anchor for environmental ethics and intergenerational justice.
  • Reversibility Principle (REV) — Under deep uncertainty, reversible action paths are prioritized so that future choice space is preserved.

Part IV · Dialogue with Major Critiques (Chapters 11–13)

Directly engages the five structural weaknesses identified in the first edition — argumentative leaps, decorative formalism, circular co-construction, weak measurement theory, and technical disconnection — and shows how each is addressed rather than concealed.

Core Features

Surgical Revision

The second edition follows a “scalpel, not demolition” strategy: minimal revision (Principle M1), preserving the recognizable core while repairing precisely targeted weaknesses.

Engineering Narrative

Combines philosophical argumentation with formalized expression — extensive diagrams, formal definitions and argumentation nodes — retaining both rigor and operational feasibility.

Interdisciplinary Integration

Draws on process philosophy, quantum mechanics, 4E cognitive science and thermodynamics to give interstellar-civilization governance a solid scientific footing.

Target Audience

This volume is suitable for researchers and students in the following fields:

  • Philosophy and ethics researchers
  • Policy research and public-governance experts
  • Science and Technology Studies (STS) scholars
  • AI governance and risk-management practitioners
  • Space policy and interstellar law researchers
  • Intergenerational justice and environmental ethics scholars

This volume supplies the conceptual tools and theoretical frameworks for the remaining five volumes, and is essential reading for understanding the whole six-volume work.
