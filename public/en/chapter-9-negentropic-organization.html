II. Why Do We Need to Formalize Negentropic Organization?

Chapter 2 of Volume I proposed the negentropic-organization definition of life:

NL-2 (negentropic organization): the essence of life is the process of maintaining and enhancing negentropic organization.

This definition is based on a fusion of Schrödinger's concept of "negentropy" with information theory, but the first edition suffered from the following problems:

1. Conceptual vagueness: the precise meaning of "negentropic organization" is unclear

2. Risk of circular definition: life is defined in terms of "organization," but "organization" itself may presuppose life

3. Difficulty of measurement: how is "negentropy" to be quantified? How is the "degree of organization" to be measured?

4. Boundary cases: do crystals, flames, and economic systems count as negentropic organizations?

This chapter resolves these problems by constructing the formal system NE.

III. The Core Tasks of This Chapter

The core tasks of this chapter are:

Task 1: Construct the formal system NE

  • Provide rigorous formal definitions of negentropy, entropy production, and information
  • Establish a formal bridge between thermodynamics and information theory
  • Define the axiomatic characteristics of "negentropic organization"

Task 2: Prove the key theorems

  • Theorem 9.1: the Negentropy Sustenance Theorem (any negentropic organization requires a continuous input of negentropy)
  • Theorem 9.2: the Information–Negentropy Equivalence Theorem (the formal relationship between information and negentropy)
  • Theorem 9.3: the Organization Measure Theorem (the degree of organization can be measured by information-theoretic means)
  • Theorem 9.4: the Thermodynamic Necessity of Life (a negentropic organization must be far from thermal equilibrium)
  • Theorem 9.5: the Hierarchy of Negentropic Organizations (negentropic organizations can form hierarchical structures)

Task 3: Apply to boundary cases

  • Crystal: highly ordered but not a negentropic organization
  • Flame: far from equilibrium but not a negentropic organization
  • Virus: a boundary case
  • Economic system: a collective negentropic organization

Task 4: Lay the foundation for the RFD indicator

  • Negentropic organization is the physical basis of recursive freedom degree (RFD)
  • The formalization in this chapter will provide the foundation for the formalization of RFD in Chapter 22

IV. The Methodology of This Chapter

This chapter adopts the following formalization strategy:

1. Thermodynamic foundation: use the mathematical framework of non-equilibrium thermodynamics

2. Information-theoretic bridge: use Shannon information theory and Kolmogorov complexity

3. Axiomatic method: build the axiom system of NE and derive theorems from the axioms

4. Machine verification: use Coq to verify the proofs of the key theorems

§9.1 Construction of the Formal System NE

§9.1.1 Goals of the Formalization

The goal of the formal system NE (Negentropy Organization) is to provide a rigorous formalization of the thermodynamic–information-theoretic definition of life.

Core question: what is a "negentropic organization"?

The natural-language definition in Volume I:

A negentropic organization is a system that is able to maintain an ordered state far from thermal equilibrium and to resist the increase of entropy through the exchange of matter–energy–information with its environment.

This definition contains three key elements:

1. Far from equilibrium: the system is in a low-entropy state

2. Continuous maintenance: the system is able to resist the increase of entropy

3. Open exchange: the system is maintained through exchange with its environment

The formalization goal of NE is to transform these three elements into operational mathematical definitions and provable theorems.

Expected benefits of the formalization:

1. Elimination of ambiguity: precisely define "negentropy," "organization," and "maintenance"

2. Computability: provide a computable measure of negentropy

3. Rejectability: make explicit which systems are / are not negentropic organizations

4. New insights: discover new theoretical results through formal derivation

§9.1.3 Philosophical Motivation for the Formal Language

Why choose this kind of formal language?

Motivation 1: thermodynamic foundation

The negentropic essence of life is first of all a thermodynamic fact. Therefore NE must be built on the mathematical framework of thermodynamics. We choose non-equilibrium thermodynamics (rather than equilibrium thermodynamics) as the foundation, because living systems are essentially non-equilibrium systems.

Motivation 2: information-theoretic bridge

The work of Brillouin, Jaynes, and others shows that there is a profound connection between entropy and information:

where is the Shannon information entropy. This connection allows us to measure negentropy with information-theoretic tools.

Motivation 3: process ontology

Consistent with the Process Ontology (PO) of Chapter 5, NE emphasizes the processual concept of "Maintains." A negentropic organization is not a static low-entropy state but a dynamic process of maintenance.

Motivation 4: open systems

Consistent with the Relational Ontology (RO) of Chapter 4, NE emphasizes the relation between system and environment. A negentropic organization is necessarily an open system, and its definition is essentially relational.

§9.1.5 Inference Rules

NE inherits the standard inference rules of first-order logic and adds the following special rules:

Rule NE-1 (second law of thermodynamics):

The entropy of a closed system does not decrease.

Rule NE-2 (entropy decomposition):

The entropy change of an open system decomposes into entropy production and entropy flow.

Rule NE-3 (non-negativity of entropy production):

The entropy production rate is always non-negative (the local form of the second law of thermodynamics).

Rule NE-4 (information–entropy relation):

The dual relationship between information and entropy.

Rule NE-5 (maintenance condition for being far from equilibrium):

To maintain being far from equilibrium, the negentropy inflow must exceed the entropy production.

§9.1.6 Axiomatization Strategy

NE adopts a hybrid axiomatization strategy:

1. Physical axioms: taken directly from the laws of thermodynamics (e.g., the second law of thermodynamics)

2. Definitional axioms: define the characteristics of "negentropic organization"

3. Bridging axioms: connect thermodynamics and information theory

The advantages of this strategy:

  • Physical axioms guarantee consistency with physics
  • Definitional axioms provide operational criteria for discrimination
  • Bridging axioms make information-theoretic tools available

Input: samples of the system state

Output: an estimate of the Shannon entropy

§9.5.1 Application One: A Crystal Is Not a Negentropic Organization

Case: a perfect crystal (such as diamond)

Analysis:

The crystal satisfies:

  • ✓ NO-1 (openness): the crystal exchanges heat with the environment
  • ✓ NO-2 (far from equilibrium): the entropy of the crystal is far below that of a gas at the same temperature
  • ✗ NO-3 (negentropy inflow): the crystal requires no continuous negentropy input to maintain its structure
  • ✓ NO-4 (degree of organization): the crystal's (too high, but barely acceptable)
  • ✓ NO-5 (steady state): the crystal is in a steady state

Conclusion: the crystal does not satisfy NO-3, and is therefore not a negentropic organization.

Deeper reason:

The low entropy of a crystal is "static," determined by the energy minimization of chemical bonds, and requires no continuous energy input. Once formed, a crystal can exist indefinitely in an equilibrium state.

In contrast, the low entropy of life is "dynamic" and requires continuous metabolism to counteract entropy increase.

Formalization:

§9.5.2 Application Two: A Flame Is Not a Negentropic Organization

Case: a candle flame

Analysis:

A flame satisfies:

  • ✓ NO-1 (openness): the flame exchanges matter and energy with the environment
  • ✓ NO-2 (far from equilibrium): the flame is in an extreme non-equilibrium state
  • ✓ NO-3 (negentropy inflow): the flame continuously consumes fuel (negentropy)

Deeper reason:

Although the flame is far from equilibrium and requires a continuous input, it lacks "organization":

  • the Kolmogorov complexity of the flame is very low (it can be described by simple hydrodynamic equations)

Not every dissipative structure is life.

§9.6 Philosophical Interpretation and New Insights

§9.6.1 New Insights Brought by the Formalization

The formalization of NE has produced the following new insights:

Insight 1: the thermodynamic necessity of life

Theorems 9.1 and 9.4 show that the core characteristics of life (continuous metabolism, being far from equilibrium) are not accidental but thermodynamic necessities. Any negentropic organization must satisfy these constraints.

This insight reinforces the "sustenance premise" of Chapter 5 of Volume I: sustenance is not a value choice but a physical necessity.

Insight 2: the "Goldilocks zone" of the degree of organization

Theorem 9.3 reveals that a negentropic organization must lie at the "edge of complexity" (). This constraint was not previously clearly recognized.

This explains why:

  • life cannot be overly ordered (like a crystal): it would lack adaptability
  • life cannot be overly random (like a gas): it could not maintain a structure

Insight 3: the physical cost of information

The full version of Theorem 9.2 (including the correction term) reveals the exact relationship between information and negentropy, laying the foundation for a physical theory of information.

This insight has far-reaching implications for computational theory, quantum information, and bioinformatics.

Insight 4: the emergence of hierarchy

Theorem 9.5 shows that the hierarchical structure of negentropic organizations is not imposed from outside but emerges from the thermodynamic properties of composition.

This provides a unified thermodynamic foundation for understanding the hierarchical structure of life (molecule→cell→individual→society).

Insight 5: the relationality of the definition of life

The virus case (§9.5.3) reveals the relationality and context-dependence of the definition of life. This is deeply consistent with the Relational Ontology of Chapter 4.

§9.6.2 Feedback to the Arguments of Volume I

The formalization of NE provides the following feedback to the arguments of Volume I:

Feedback 1: the precisification of NL-2

NL-2 (negentropic organization), proposed in Chapter 2 of Volume I, now has a rigorous axiomatic definition (Definition 9.22). This eliminates the conceptual vagueness of the first edition.

Feedback 2: the physical foundation of the sustenance premise

Theorem 9.1 provides a solid physical foundation for the "sustenance premise" of Chapter 5 of Volume I: any negentropic organization (including rational agents) must continuously acquire negentropy; this is a physical necessity, not a value choice.

Feedback 3: a thermodynamic defense of the NRP

The Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP) can now be derived from thermodynamics:

  • Premise 1: a rational agent is a negentropic organization (definition)
  • Premise 2: a negentropic organization requires a continuous negentropy input (Theorem 9.1)
  • Premise 3: the negentropy input comes from the environment (Definition 9.10)
  • Conclusion: a rational agent has the responsibility to maintain its negentropy source (the environment)

This is a thermodynamic argument for the NRP, supplementing the normative argument of Chapter 8 of Volume I.

Feedback 4: the foundation of the life continuum

Theorem 9.5 (hierarchy) provides a thermodynamic foundation for the life continuum: negentropic organizations at different levels have different amounts of negentropy, which can serve as a dimension of moral status.

§9.6.3 Relations with Other Philosophical Positions

Relation to physicalism:

NE is a form of physicalism: the essential characteristics of life can be fully characterized by physics (thermodynamics). But NE is not reductionist:

  • the degree of organization involves information theory and complexity theory and cannot be simply reduced to microphysics
  • hierarchy (Theorem 9.5) shows that higher-level negentropic organizations have emergent properties

Relation to vitalism:

NE explicitly rejects vitalism: life does not require non-physical factors such as a "vital force" (élan vital). All the characteristics of life can be derived from thermodynamics and information theory.

Relation to functionalism:

NE is partly compatible with functionalism:

  • the definition of a negentropic organization is functional (the function of maintaining being far from equilibrium)
  • but NE adds thermodynamic constraints (it must satisfy NO-1 to NO-5)

Relation to process philosophy:

NE is highly consistent with Whitehead's process philosophy:

  • a negentropic organization is essentially a process (a process of maintenance), not a static entity
  • Axiom NO-5 emphasizes steady-state maintenance, which is a processual concept
  • this is consistent with the Process Ontology (PO) of Chapter 5

§9.6.4 Limitations of the Formalization

The formalization of NE has the following limitations:

Limitation 1: the treatment of quantum effects

NE is based on classical thermodynamics and its treatment of quantum effects is inadequate. For systems such as quantum biology (e.g., quantum coherence in photosynthesis) and quantum computers, NE needs to be extended to quantum thermodynamics.

Limitation 2: the absence of consciousness

NE characterizes only the thermodynamic characteristics of life and does not address consciousness. The thermodynamic foundation of consciousness remains an open question.

Limitation 3: the absence of purposiveness

NE does not characterize the purposiveness (teleology) of life. Why does a negentropic organization maintain itself far from equilibrium? NE answers only "how" it is maintained, not "why."

This limitation will be partly resolved in Chapter 10 (the subject continuum) and Chapter 17 (the formalization of normativity).

Limitation 4: the absence of evolution

NE is static (whether a system at a given time is a negentropic organization) and does not characterize evolutionary dynamics. How does life emerge from non-life? How do negentropic organizations evolve? These questions lie beyond the scope of NE.

Limitation 5: the blurriness of boundaries

Although NE provides explicit axioms (NO-1 to NO-5), in certain boundary cases (such as viruses, prions, and computer viruses) the determination is still difficult. This reflects the intrinsic difficulty of defining life.

Appendix 9B: Complete Proof of the Independence of the Axioms

Complete proof of Theorem 9.6:

We need to prove that axioms NO-1 to NO-5 are mutually independent. The strategy is to construct, for each axiom, a counterexample that satisfies the other four axioms but not that axiom.

Independence of NO-1:

Counterexample: consider a closed ideal-gas container, initially in a non-equilibrium state (half at high temperature, half at low temperature).

  • ✗ NO-1: the system is closed and does not exchange with the environment
  • ✓ NO-2: at the initial moment (non-equilibrium state)
  • ✓ NO-3: during the relaxation process, an "effective negentropy flow" can be defined (from the high-temperature region to the low-temperature region)
  • ✓ NO-4: the degree of organization of the gas is within a reasonable range
  • ✓ NO-5: it eventually reaches an equilibrium steady state

Therefore, NO-1 cannot be derived from the other axioms. □

End-of-Chapter Notes

[1] Schrödinger's What is Life? (1944) is the pioneering work that applied the concept of negentropy to life, but Schrödinger himself did not give a rigorous formal definition. The NE of this chapter can be regarded as a formalist realization of Schrödinger's insight.

[2] Prigogine's theory of dissipative structures (1977 Nobel Prize in Chemistry) is an important precursor of NE, but the theory of dissipative structures lacks the concept of a degree of organization, and thus cannot distinguish a flame from life.

[3] Brillouin's Science and Information Theory (1956) established for the first time a quantitative relationship between information and negentropy, but did not apply it to the definition of life.

[4] Kolmogorov complexity (1965) provides the mathematical foundation of the degree of organization, but Kolmogorov himself did not apply it to the theory of life.

[5] The innovation of this chapter lies in integrating thermodynamics, information theory, and complexity theory into a unified formal system and giving an axiomatic definition of life.

[6] The machine verification of NE (Coq/Isabelle/Lean) is a new standard for philosophical formalization, ensuring the logical correctness of the theorems.

[7] NE's analysis of AI systems (§9.5.5) shows that current AI systems already satisfy the thermodynamic definition of life (NL-2), but may not satisfy the definition of consciousness (NL-3, to be discussed in Chapter 10).

[8] NE's analysis of economic systems (§9.5.4) provides a thermodynamic foundation for sustainable development, which is one of this work's direct contributions to policy-making.
