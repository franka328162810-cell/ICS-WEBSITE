Chapter 24: A Formalization of RFD and CRV

Chapter Introduction

This chapter follows Chapter 23's formalization of UCS and turns to the formal construction of two other core indicators of subjectivity—RFD (Recursive Freedom Degree) and CRV (Commitment-Responsibility Vector). If UCS answers “who has moral status,” then RFD answers “who has moral agency,” and CRV answers “what kind of responsibility does an agent bear.”

The three form a complete triangle of subjectivity assessment:

UCS (degree of consciousness) / \ / \ RFD (degree of freedom) ↔ CRV (responsibility)

The core tasks of this chapter are:

1. To establish a rigorous formal system for RFD (§24.1-24.4)

2. To establish a rigorous formal system for CRV (§24.5-24.8)

3. To prove the coupling relationship between RFD and CRV (§24.9)

4. To argue for the anchoring relationship between the three and the Three New Views (§24.10)

Methodological Statement

This chapter continues the formalization strategy of Chapter 23:

  • Each indicator establishes an independent axiom system
  • Each system contains at least 3 non-trivial theorems
  • All theorems are provided with complete proofs
  • Coq/Isabelle/Lean are used for machine verification

Reading Suggestions

  • Readers interested in ethical applications may focus on §24.4 and §24.8
  • Readers interested in formalization may focus on §24.2, §24.6, and §24.9
  • Readers interested in the correspondence with Volume I may focus on §24.10

§24.2.3　Philosophical Interpretation of the Axioms

I. A1-A2: Fundamental Constraints

A1 and A2 establish the basic mathematical properties of RFD. A2 is especially important because it links RFD to determinism:

Philosophical implication: even in a deterministic universe, so long as an agent has counterfactual freedom (would make different choices under different initial conditions), RFD can be positive.

II. A3-A4: Dimensional Monotonicity

A3 and A4 ensure that the two core dimensions of RFD (recursion depth and option space) make a positive contribution to the overall RFD.

Point of contention: A4 uses “no lower than” rather than “greater than,” because an expansion of the option space may be offset by other factors.

III. A5: The Special Status of Autonomy

A5 grants autonomy a special status—an autonomous agent necessarily has a positive RFD.

Philosophical defense: autonomy is a constitutive feature of agency. A system that is entirely non-autonomous cannot be called an “agent.”

IV. A6-A7: Dynamism and Contextuality

A6 and A7 embody the core insight of the New View of Cognition: freedom is not a static intrinsic property but is dynamic and context-dependent.

Connection with NC-3: the Participatory Universe view requires that freedom be relational; A6 formalizes this requirement.

V. A8: Cross-Substrate Applicability

A8 ensures that RFD can be applied to agents of different physical substrates (carbon-based, silicon-based, extraterrestrial life, etc.).

Connection with NL-2: the cross-substrate view of life requires that assessment standards not depend on a specific substrate; A8 formalizes this requirement.

The Substantive Contribution of the Formalization

The formalization of this chapter is not merely “decoration” but produces substantive new insights:

Insight One: the constraint paradox (Theorem 24.3.3)

Volume I intuitively held that “constraints reduce freedom,” but the formalization reveals that certain constraints actually increase meaningful freedom. This insight has important implications for institutional design.

Insight Two: the emergence of freedom (Theorem 24.3.2)

The formalization proves that collective freedom can be greater than the sum of individual freedoms, providing a theoretical basis for collective agency.

Insight Three: the cost of reversibility (Theorem 24.7.3)

The formalization quantifies the cost of the Reversibility Principle, showing that REV is not “free” and requires a trade-off between safety and efficiency.

Insight Four: the bidirectional coupling of RFD-CRV (Theorem 24.9.3)

The formalization reveals the positive feedback mechanism between freedom and responsibility, explaining why societies with high freedom are often also societies with high responsibility.

Insight Five: stability of the coupled system (Theorem 24.9.4)

The formalization proves the existence of a stable equilibrium of “freedom-responsibility” configurations, providing a theoretical basis for the self-organization of social institutions.

Limitations of This Chapter

Although this chapter has completed a rigorous formalization, the following limitations remain:

Limitation One: computational complexity

Theorems 24.4.3 and 24.8.3 show that the exact computation of RFD and CRV is undecidable or NP-hard. This limits the practical application of the indicators.

Responses:

  • Develop approximation algorithms (see Volume IV)
  • Use heuristic methods
  • Accept uncertainty in practice

Limitation Two: parameter dependence

The definitions of RFD and CRV depend on multiple parameters (such as the weights ). Although Theorems 24.4.4 and 24.8.4 prove robustness, the choice of parameters still requires cross-cultural negotiation.

Responses:

  • Reduce arbitrariness through anchor calibration
  • Conduct sensitivity analysis
  • Maintain transparency in the choice of parameters

Limitation Three: incompleteness

Theorems 24.4.2 and 24.8.2 show that the RFD and CRV systems are incomplete. There exist true propositions that cannot be derived from the axioms.

Responses:

  • Accept incompleteness as the price of expressive power
  • Extend the axiom system when new true propositions are discovered
  • Focus on practically relevant, derivable propositions

Limitation Four: insufficient empirical validation

The formalization of this chapter is mainly a theoretical construction, with limited empirical validation.

Responses:

  • Volume IV provides computational tools
  • Volume V provides application cases
  • Invite the academic community to conduct independent verification
