New Cognition: Participatory, Social, Facing Deep Uncertainty

New Cognition proposes that cognition is not a mirror reflection of the objective world, but participatory construction; knowledge is not private property of individuals, but a social collaborative outcome; cognitive agents must face deep uncertainty—situations where outcomes, probabilities, and utilities are all indeterminate. This epistemology has profound implications for how we act.

Core Propositions

New Cognition comprises three core propositions:

Proposition NK-1: Participatory Cognition

Cognition is not mirroring, but participatory construction.

Traditional epistemology holds that cognition is the passive reflection of the world by the mind. New Cognition proposes: cognition is a process of interaction between subject and world, where the cognizer participates in the construction of the cognized object through cognitive acts.

Core Claim
Knowledge(x) = Interaction(Subject, Object, Context)

Proposition NK-2: Social Nature of Cognition

Knowledge is essentially social and collaborative.

Knowledge is not the private property of isolated individuals, but is produced, validated, and disseminated through social interaction.

Core Claims:

  • Knowledge production is a collaborative process
  • Knowledge validation depends on social mechanisms
  • Knowledge dissemination shapes epistemic communities

Proposition NK-3: Deep Uncertainty

Cognitive agents must face deep uncertainty.

In many important situations, we face not calculable risk, but deep uncertainty—where outcomes, probabilities, and utilities are all indeterminate.

Characteristics of Deep Uncertainty:

Dimension Risk Deep Uncertainty
Possible Outcomes Known Unknown or incomplete
Outcome Probabilities Estimable Inestimable
Utility Function Stable Unstable or unknown

Scientific Foundations

Embodied Cognitive Science

New Cognition is supported by embodied cognitive science:

  • Embodiment: Cognition depends on the body and its interaction with the environment
  • Situatedness: Cognition is embedded in specific physical and social contexts
  • Extendedness: Cognition can extend beyond the body to tools and environment

Social Epistemology

Social epistemology studies the social dimensions of knowledge:

  • Social organization of knowledge production
  • Epistemic status of testimony and expert knowledge
  • Division of cognitive labor

Decision Theory

Recent developments in decision theory recognize the challenge of deep uncertainty:

  • Limitations of Bayesian methods
  • Robust decision-making approaches
  • Adaptive management methods

Core Maxim Interpretation

"Cognition is intervention — Knowledge is collaboration"

This maxim condenses the core insights of New Cognition:

"Cognition is Intervention"

  • Cognition is not passive observation, but active participation
  • The cognitive act itself changes the cognized object
  • This resonates with the measurement problem in quantum mechanics

"Knowledge is Collaboration"

  • Knowledge is the product of social processes
  • Isolated individuals cannot produce genuine knowledge
  • Epistemic communities are the basic unit of epistemology

Deep Uncertainty and Decision-Making

Sources of Deep Uncertainty

Many problems faced by Interstellar Civilization Studies involve deep uncertainty:

  • AI Development: AGI's capabilities, behaviors, and impacts are highly uncertain
  • Space Exploration: Existence of extraterrestrial intelligence and contact consequences are indeterminate
  • Existential Risks: Probabilities and consequences of extreme risks are difficult to estimate
  • Deep Time Future: Developmental trajectories on millennial scales are unpredictable

Strategies for Addressing Deep Uncertainty

New Cognition is not pessimism or nihilism, but provides strategies for addressing deep uncertainty:

Strategy Content
Reversibility Priority Prioritize reversible actions, preserving fallback options
Diversity Protection Protect option diversity, avoid premature lock-in
Adaptive Management Adopt iterative, feedback-based, adjustable approaches
Collaborative Decision Leverage collective intelligence rather than individual judgment

Toward the Reversibility Principle

New Cognition provides the epistemological foundation for the Reversibility Principle:

Because we face deep uncertainty, our actions should maintain reversibility as much as possible, preserving the possibility of error correction for the future.

Implications for Agents

New Cognition has important practical implications for agents:

Epistemic Humility

  • Acknowledge the limits of our knowledge
  • Maintain skepticism toward certainty claims
  • Value dissent and criticism

Collaborative Spirit

  • Knowledge is co-created
  • Value contributions from diverse perspectives
  • Build inclusive epistemic communities

Practical Wisdom

  • Act despite uncertainty
  • But maintain reversibility when acting
  • Continuously learn and adjust
