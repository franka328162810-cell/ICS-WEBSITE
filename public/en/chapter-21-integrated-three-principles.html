§21.1 The Necessity and Strategy of Integration

§21.1.1 Why Integration Is Needed

Chapters 18 to 20 respectively constructed three independent formal systems:

These three systems are each independent, yet together they constitute Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (CRE). However, constructing them independently gives rise to four problems:

Problem One: The Consistency Problem

Are the three systems mutually consistent? For example:

  • RFP requires protecting the space of possibilities (maximizing options)
  • NRP requires maintaining negentropy (which may require restricting certain options)
  • Are these two compatible?

Problem Two: The Conflict Problem

How are conflicts among the Three Principles adjudicated? For example:

  • A certain action increases RFD (conforming to RFP) but reduces negentropy (violating NRP)
  • A certain action is reversible (conforming to REV) but seriously damages negentropy (violating NRP)
  • How should the trade-off be made?

Problem Three: The Completeness Problem

Do the three systems completely characterize CRE? Have some important norms been omitted?

Problem Four: The Emergence Problem

Does the integrated system possess new properties that no single principle possesses?

The necessity of integration:

Only through systematic integration can we:

1. Verify the consistency of the three systems

2. Establish a conflict-resolution mechanism

3. Test the completeness of the systems

4. Discover emergent properties

§21.1.2 The Strategy of Integration

We adopt a layered integration strategy:

Layer One: Syntactic integration

  • Unify the formal languages of the three systems
  • Establish a common symbolic system
  • Define cross-system connectives

Layer Two: Semantic integration

  • Unify the semantic domains of the three systems
  • Establish a common interpretive framework
  • Define cross-system truth conditions

Layer Three: Axiomatic integration

  • Retain the core axioms of the three systems
  • Add coordination axioms (Coordination Axioms)
  • Establish priority rules

Layer Four: Inferential integration

  • Unify the inference rules
  • Establish cross-system inference
  • Prove overall consistency

The principles of integration:

1. Conservativity: integration should not weaken the core claims of any single system

2. Minimal modification: integration should retain the original axioms and theorems as far as possible

3. Emergence: integration should generate new theoretical insights

4. Operability: the integrated system should support practical application

§21.1.3 The Challenges of Integration

Integration faces three major challenges:

Challenge One: Conceptual heterogeneity

The three principles use different core concepts:

  • RFP: space of possibilities, recursive depth
  • NRP: negentropy, thermodynamics
  • REV: reversibility, deep uncertainty

How can these heterogeneous concepts be accommodated within a unified framework?

Challenge Two: Normative conflict

The three principles may give different normative guidance:

  • RFP: maximize freedom
  • NRP: maintain order
  • REV: remain cautious

How can these potential conflicts be reconciled?

Challenge Three: Formalization complexity

The formalization complexity of the integrated system may grow exponentially. How can it be kept manageable?

Countermeasures:

  • For Challenge One: use category theory as the unifying language
  • For Challenge Two: establish a dynamic trade-off mechanism
  • For Challenge Three: adopt a modular design

The strategy of integration is: layered integration (syntax → semantics → axioms → inference).
