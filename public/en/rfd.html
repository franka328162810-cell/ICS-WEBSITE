Recursive Freedom Degree: Guarding the Space of Possibilities

The Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD) is an operationalized metric of the Recursive Freedom Principle, used to assess the impact of specific actions on the future space of possibilities. It answers a key question: Are our decisions expanding or contracting the conditions of freedom for future subjects? RFD provides a future-oriented quantitative reference for every major decision.

Definition of RFD

Definition:

The Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD) is a metric that measures the degree to which a specific action, policy, or technology impacts the space of possibilities for future subjects.

Core Formula:

RFD(a) = |Pt+Δta| / |Pt|

Where:
- a: the action being evaluated
- Pt: the space of possibilities before the action
- Pt+Δta: the space of possibilities after executing action a
- Δt: the time span of evaluation

Interpretation

RFD Value Meaning Examples
RFD > 1 Action expands the space of possibilities Universal education, open-source technology
RFD = 1 Action maintains the space of possibilities Reversible, retractable decisions
RFD < 1 Action contracts the space of possibilities Irreversible resource consumption, technological lock-in
RFD → 0 Action severely contracts the space of possibilities Actions touching forbidden red lines

Understanding the Space of Possibilities

What is the Space of Possibilities?

The Space of Possibilities refers to the set of actions, developmental paths, and modes of existence that future subjects can choose at a specific moment.

Figuratively speaking:

The space of possibilities is the "menu of future choices"—the more options on the menu, the richer they are, the greater the freedom of future subjects.

Dimensions of the Space of Possibilities

The space of possibilities is multidimensional, including but not limited to:

Dimension Content Examples
Material Dimension Available material resources and energy Minerals, energy, ecosystems
Technological Dimension Available technological paths Technological diversity vs. technological lock-in
Social Dimension Possible forms of social organization Institutional diversity vs. institutional lock-in
Cognitive Dimension Accessible knowledge and information Knowledge diversity vs. information monopoly
Biological Dimension Possible forms of life Biodiversity vs. species extinction
Value Dimension Pursuable value orientations Value pluralism vs. value lock-in

RFD Assessment Methods

Assessment Framework

RFD assessment includes four steps:

Dimensional Scoring

Each dimension can be scored independently:

RFDdimension i(a) = |Pi,t+Δta| / |Pi,t|

Overall RFD is a weighted synthesis of dimensions:

RFD(a) = Σ wi · RFDdimension i(a)

where wi is the weight of dimension i.

Time Scales

RFD assessment requires specifying a time scale:

Time Scale Applicable Scenarios
Short-term (1-10 years) Specific policy assessment
Medium-term (10-100 years) Technological pathway assessment
Long-term (100-1000 years) Civilizational strategy assessment
Deep Time (1000+ years) Existential risk assessment

Case Studies (Conceptual)

Case 1: Open-Source AI vs. Closed AI

Dimension Open-Source AI Closed AI
Technological Dimension RFD > 1 (multi-party innovation) RFD < 1 (technological concentration)
Cognitive Dimension RFD > 1 (knowledge sharing) RFD < 1 (knowledge monopoly)
Social Dimension RFD ≈ 1 (requires specific analysis) RFD < 1 (power concentration)
Overall RFD Higher Lower

Case 2: Fossil Fuels vs. Renewable Energy

Dimension Fossil Fuels Renewable Energy
Material Dimension RFD < 1 (resource depletion) RFD ≈ 1 (sustainable)
Biological Dimension RFD < 1 (climate impact) RFD ≈ 1 (minimal impact)
Technological Dimension RFD < 1 (path lock-in) RFD > 1 (technological diversity)
Overall RFD Lower Higher

Note: The above cases are simplified conceptual analyses. Actual assessments require more detailed data and modeling.

Relationship with the Recursive Freedom Principle

RFD is the operationalization of the Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP):

Recursive Freedom Principle (Theory) Recursive Freedom Degree (Operation)
Must not systematically weaken others' conditions of freedom Actions with RFD < 1 require higher justification
Protect the space of possibilities for future subjects RFD assessment covers long time scales
Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility RFD quantifies the effect of "stewardship"

Decision Rules (Advisory)

RFD Result Recommended Action
RFD > 1 Encourage, actively promote
RFD ≈ 1 Allow, routine review
0.5 < RFD < 1 Cautious, strengthen assessment and monitoring
RFD < 0.5 Highly cautious, requires full justification of necessity
RFD → 0 Approaching forbidden red lines, prohibited in principle
