2. The Core Objectives of This Chapter

This chapter has three progressive objectives:

Objective One (formalization objective): Construct the formal system PU to rigorously express the core claims of the "Participatory Universe view".

Objective Two (proof objective): Prove at least 5 non-trivial theorems, revealing the deep structure of participation.

Objective Three (philosophical objective): Generate new philosophical insights through formalization, going beyond the natural-language arguments of Volume 1.

3. Review of the Core Claims of the Participatory Universe View

Before entering the formalization, we briefly review the formulation of the Participatory Universe view (NC-3) given in Chapter 1 of Volume 1:

NC-3 (participation): The observer does not passively "discover" a reality that exists independently, but participates in constituting reality through the act of observation.

This claim contains three sub-propositions:

  • NC-3.1 (the constitutive character of observation): The act of observation not only "reveals" reality but also "constitutes" certain aspects of reality.
  • NC-3.2 (the inseparability of observer and observed): The observer and the observed object cannot be completely separated.
  • NC-3.3 (the context-dependence of reality): Certain properties of reality depend on the context of observation.

Volume 1 argued for NC-3 along three independent paths:

1. The quantum-mechanical path: Measurement causes the collapse of the wave function, and the observer participates in determining the measurement outcome.

2. The cognitive-science path: 4E cognition shows that the cognizer participates in constituting the object of cognition.

3. The philosophy-of-science path: Theory-ladenness shows that observation depends on a theoretical framework.

4. Why Is Formalization Needed?

The natural-language arguments of Volume 1 have the following limitations:

Limitation One (conceptual vagueness): Concepts such as "participation" and "constitution" are vague in natural language.

Limitation Two (argumentative leaps): There is a leap in the derivation from quantum measurement to "the observer participates in constituting reality."

Limitation Three (unclear boundaries): The scope of application of participation is unclear (does it apply to the macroscopic world?).

Limitation Four (distinction from idealism): The distinction between the Participatory Universe view and subjective idealism is not clear enough.

The goal of formalization is to overcome these limitations, making the Participatory Universe view a rigorous, testable theoretical framework.

Not all axioms are uncontroversial. We analyze the philosophical challenges each axiom may face:

Controversy of A1:

  • Challenge: Does the classical limit really make the degree of participation tend to zero? How should macroscopic quantum effects (such as superconductivity) be handled?

Controversy of A7:

  • Challenge: Does this contradict the unitary evolution of quantum mechanics?
  • Response: A7 applies to the measurement process (non-unitary), not to free evolution (unitary).

§6.7.2 Insight Two: The Ontological Status of Context

The limitation of Volume 1:

Volume 1 treated context as an epistemological concept, without clarifying its ontological status.

The contribution of PU:

By introducing the context as a basic type, PU grants context ontological status.

New insight:

Context is not merely "the background of observation" but a constitutive element of reality.

Relation to scientific practice:

The goal of science is not to discover "context-free truth" but to:

1. Clarify the boundary conditions of contexts

2. Establish transformation rules between different contexts

3. Seek invariants across contexts

§6.7.4 Reflection on the Limitations of the Formalization

Although PU brings new insights, we must acknowledge the limitations of the formalization:

Limitation One: The non-formalizability of subjective experience

The degree of participation quantifies the informational correlation between the observer and the system, but it cannot capture the "qualia" of subjective experience.

Example:

Two observers may have the same , yet their subjective experiences may be completely different (for example: color blindness versus normal vision).

Response:

The goal of PU is not to solve the "Hard Problem of Consciousness" but to formalize the objective aspects of observation. The formalization of subjective experience may require a framework beyond PU.

Limitation Two: The infinite regress of context

The context itself needs to be defined in some meta-context , which in turn needs to be defined in … This leads to an infinite regress.

Response:

PU partially alleviates this problem through the partial-order structure of contexts . In practice, we always work in some "sufficiently large" context, without tracing back to an "ultimate context."

Limitation Three: The controversiality of the axioms

Certain axioms (especially A1 and A7) are philosophically controversial.

Response:

The axiomatization of PU makes the controversy precise: opponents can clearly point out which axiom they reject and construct an alternative system. This in itself is philosophical progress.

This section compares PU with three related formal systems: quantum logic, modal realism, and Situation Semantics.

§6.8.1 Comparison with Quantum Logic

Quantum Logic (QL):

Quantum logic was proposed by Birkhoff and von Neumann in 1936, formalizing quantum mechanics using non-classical logic (lattice theory).

Core idea:

Quantum propositions do not satisfy the distributivity of classical logic:

The relation between PU and QL:

DimensionQuantum Logic (QL)Participatory Universe (PU)
Logical basisNon-distributive latticeClassical first-order logic + modal logic
Core conceptsLattice structure of propositionsObserver, context, degree of participation
Ontological commitmentQuantum systemsObserver-system-context triples
Scope of applicationQuantum mechanicsQuantum mechanics + cognitive science + philosophy of science

The advantages of PU:

1. Broader scope of application: PU applies not only to quantum systems but also to macroscopic cognition.

2. More intuitive semantics: PU uses standard logic, avoiding the counter-intuitiveness of QL.

3. An explicit role for the observer: PU takes the observer as a basic element, whereas in QL the observer is implicit.

The advantages of QL:

1. More concise: QL directly modifies the logical structure without introducing additional concepts such as context.

2. Direct correspondence with quantum mechanics: The lattice structure of QL directly corresponds to the subspace lattice of Hilbert space.

Possibility of integration:

PU can embed QL: interpreting the lattice structure of QL as the propositional structure in a specific context.

§6.8.2 Comparison with Modal Realism

Modal Realism (MR):

David Lewis's modal realism claims that all possible worlds exist in reality.

Core idea:

Possibility is equivalent to being true in some possible world.

The relation between PU and MR:

DimensionModal Realism (MR)Participatory Universe (PU)
Possible worldsAll possible worlds exist in realityPossible worlds are abstractions of contexts
ActualityThe actual world has no special statusActuality depends on the context of observation
Modal operators
Ontological commitmentExtremely rich (infinitely many worlds)Moderate (observer + system + context)

The advantages of PU:

1. Ontological parsimony: PU need not commit to infinitely many real worlds.

2. Context-relativity: PU's possibility is relative to context, better fitting scientific practice.

3. Observer-centered: PU makes explicit the role of the observer.

The advantages of MR:

1. The conciseness of modal semantics: MR provides the most direct possible-worlds semantics.

2. Treatment of counterfactual conditionals: MR offers an elegant treatment of counterfactual conditionals.

Possibility of integration:

PU can adopt MR's semantic framework but interpret "possible worlds" as "possible contexts of observation":

§6.8.3 Comparison with Situation Semantics

Situation Semantics (SS):

Proposed by Barwise and Perry in 1983, it replaces possible worlds with situations as the semantic basis.

Core idea:

The truth value of a sentence is relative to a situation, not to the entire possible world.

§6.8.4 Summary of the Comparison

PU synthesizes the strengths of the three systems:

  • Borrowed from quantum logic: the formalization of quantum systems
  • Borrowed from modal realism: possible-worlds semantics
  • Borrowed from Situation Semantics: the partiality and structure of contexts
