Chapter 3 A Formalization Case Study: Relational Ontology

Chapter Introduction

This chapter uses a complete case—the formalization of Relational Ontology—to demonstrate how the methodology established in Chapter 1 and the tools introduced in Chapter 2 are applied in practice.

We will:

1. Review the natural-language argument of Volume One

2. Identify the formalization goals

3. Select the formalization tool (category theory)

4. Build the formal system

5. Prove the core theorem (relational irreducibility)

6. Machine verification (Coq/Isabelle/Lean)

7. Explain the philosophical significance

This chapter is the concrete practice of the methodology and the template for subsequent chapters.

§3.1 Review of the Natural-Language Argument

§3.1.1 Core Claims of Volume One

The core argument of Chapter 1 of Volume One:

Claim 1 (Priority of Relations): Relations are ontologically prior to objects. Relations are not "additions" between objects, but basic constituents of reality.

Claim 2 (Irreducibility of Relations): Relations cannot be reduced to the intrinsic properties of the relata. There exist relations that cannot be completely determined by any object's intrinsic properties.

Claim 3 (Evidence from Quantum Entanglement): Quantum entanglement provides empirical evidence for the irreducibility of relations. In an entangled state, the relation between two particles cannot be completely determined by the intrinsic properties of either particle.

Argument structure:

[Premise 1] Quantum entanglement is a real physical phenomenon

[Premise 2] In an entangled state, the properties of the whole cannot be completely determined by the properties of the parts

[Premise 3] If the properties of the whole cannot be completely determined by the properties of the parts,

then relations cannot be completely determined by the intrinsic properties of objects

────────────────────────────────────────────────

[Conclusion] Relations cannot be completely determined by the intrinsic properties of objects

(Irreducibility of Relations)

Key concepts:

  • Relation: the structure connecting objects
  • Object: the relatum of a relation
  • Intrinsic Property: a property that an object possesses independently of other objects
  • Reduction: explaining a complex concept in terms of more basic concepts
  • Quantum Entanglement: the non-separability of a quantum system

§3.1.2 Problems Requiring Formalization

Problem 1: Precision of Concepts

The key concepts in the natural-language argument require precise definitions:

  • What is the precise definition of "intrinsic property"?
  • What is the precise meaning of "completely determine"?
  • What is the formalization criterion for "irreducible"?

Problem 2: Rigor of the Argument

The steps of the argument require rigorous verification:

  • How should Premise 2 (irreducibility of the whole) be formulated precisely?
  • Is the inference of Premise 3 (from the whole to relations) valid?
  • Are there hidden assumptions?

Problem 3: Consistency

It is necessary to verify:

  • The relationship between Relational Ontology and other ontological positions
  • The compatibility between the priority of relations and the existence of objects
  • The consistency between the interpretation of quantum entanglement and standard quantum mechanics

Problem 4: New Insights

The formalization should produce:

  • A precise characterization of the irreducibility of relations
  • The deep connection between quantum entanglement and Relational Ontology
  • Possible new theorems and corollaries

Goal 1: Precisely Define the Core Concepts

  • Determination

Goal 2: State the Core Theorem

Theorem 3.1 (Irreducibility of Relations):

There exist relations that cannot be completely determined by the intrinsic properties of objects.

Goal 3: Provide a Rigorous Proof

Prove Theorem 3.1 using the tools of category theory.

Goal 4: Machine Verification

Verify the proof in Coq/Isabelle/Lean.

Goal 5: Produce New Insights

Reveal the deep structure of the irreducibility of relations.

§3.2.2 Tool Selection: Why Category Theory?

Candidate tools:

1. Set theory

2. First-order logic

3. Modal logic

4. Category theory

Assessment:

Set theory:

  • Strengths: standard, familiar
  • Weaknesses: object-first (a set is a collection of objects)
  • Not suited to an ontology that prioritizes relations

First-order logic:

  • Strengths: strong expressive power, completeness
  • Weaknesses: relations are expressed as predicates, still object-first
  • Partially suitable, but not the best choice

Modal logic:

  • Strengths: can express necessity
  • Weaknesses: does not specifically handle relational structure
  • Not suited to this case

Category theory:

  • Strengths:
  • Relation-first: morphisms (relations) are primary
  • Structure preservation: functors naturally express structure mappings
  • Rich tools: limits, colimits, natural transformations
  • Track record: the category-theoretic formulation of quantum mechanics
  • Weaknesses:
  • Steep learning curve
  • Less familiar than set theory
  • Best suited to Relational Ontology

Decision: use category theory

§3.3 Category-Theoretic Formalization

§3.3.1 Basic Setup

Definition 3.1 (Category of Quantum Systems):

Define the category Quant:

  • Objects: finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
  • Morphisms: linear operators
  • Composition: operator composition
  • Identity: identity operator

Coq implementation:

```coq

Obj := HilbertSpace;

Hom := LinearOperator;

compose := fun A B C (g : Hom B C) (f : Hom A B) =>

matrix_mult g f;

}.

( Verify category axioms )

Lemma quantum_category_assoc : forall (A B C D : Obj)

(f : Hom A B) (g : Hom B C) (h : Hom C D),

compose h (compose g f) = compose (compose h g) f.

Proof.

intros.

unfold compose.

apply matrix_mult_assoc.

Qed.

compose id f = f.

intros.
```

§3.3.2 Formalization of Intrinsic Properties

Intuition: The intrinsic properties of an object should be the properties "of the object itself," which do not depend on other objects.

Category-theoretic formulation: Intrinsic properties correspond to endomorphisms.

Definition 3.2 (Endomorphism):

An endomorphism of an object A is a morphism f: A → A.

Write End(A) = Hom(A, A).

Explanation:

  • An endomorphism is a way for an object to "act on itself"
  • It captures the intrinsic structure of the object
  • It does not involve other objects

Coq implementation:

```coq

( Endomorphism )

Definition Endomorphism (A : Obj) := Hom A A.

Notation "'End' ( A )" := (Endomorphism A) (at level 70).

( Endomorphisms form a monoid )

Instance EndomorphismMonoid (A : Obj) : Monoid (End(A)) := {

monoid_op := compose;

monoid_unit := id

}.

```

§3.3.3 Formalization of Determination

Intuition: If a relation can be completely determined by the intrinsic properties of objects, then knowing the intrinsic properties of the objects is sufficient to determine the relation.

Category-theoretic formulation: A morphism f: A → B can be completely determined by End(A) and End(B) if f can be expressed as some combination of elements of End(A) and End(B).

Definition 3.3 (Determination):

A morphism f: A → B is completely determined by End(A) and End(B) if:

```

there exists a function Φ: End(A) × End(B) → Hom(A, B)

such that f = Φ(e_A, e_B) for some e_A ∈ End(A), e_B ∈ End(B)

```

Stronger version (functoriality):

There exists a functor F: End(A) × End(B) → Hom(A, B).

Coq implementation:

```coq

( Determination )

Definition determined_by_endomorphisms

{A B : Obj} (f : Hom A B) : Prop :=

exists (Φ : End(A) -> End(B) -> Hom A B)

(e_A : End(A)) (e_B : End(B)),

f = Φ e_A e_B.

( All morphisms are determined by endomorphisms (reducibility) )

Definition all_morphisms_determined (A B : Obj) : Prop :=

forall f : Hom A B, determined_by_endomorphisms f.

```

§3.3.4 The Irreducibility of Relations Theorem

Theorem 3.1 (Irreducibility of Relations):

In the quantum category Quant, there exist objects A, B and a morphism f: A → B such that f cannot be completely determined by End(A) and End(B).

Formal statement:

```coq

Theorem relation_irreducibility :

exists (A B : Obj) (f : Hom A B),

~ determined_by_endomorphisms f.

```

Proof sketch:

1. Construct concrete A and B (two-dimensional Hilbert spaces)

2. Construct the morphism f corresponding to an entangled state

3. Prove End(A) ≅ End(B) ≅ M₂(ℂ) (2×2 complex matrices)

4. Prove that f cannot be expressed as a combination of elements of End(A) and End(B)

5. Use the non-separability of quantum entanglement

Detailed proof:

```coq

( Step 1: construct A and B )

set (A := C2_Hilbert). ( ℂ² )

set (B := C2_Hilbert). ( ℂ² )

set (f := state_to_operator bell_state).

exists A, B, f.

( Step 3: assume f can be determined by endomorphisms )

intro H_determined.

destruct H_determined as [Φ [e_A [e_B H_eq]]].

( Step 4: derive a contradiction )

( If f = Φ(e_A, e_B), then bell_state is separable )

assert (H_separable : separable bell_state).

{

exact H_eq.

}

( But bell_state is entangled (non-separable) )

assert (H_entangled : ~ separable bell_state).

apply bell_state_entangled.

contradiction.

Qed.
```

```coq
(e_A : End(A)) (e_B : End(B)),

f = Φ e_A e_B.

( Quantum states )

Definition State (H : HilbertSpace) :=

Vector (projT1 H) C.

Definition separable {H1 H2 : HilbertSpace}

(ψ : State (tensor_product H1 H2)) : Prop :=

exists (ψ₁ : State H1) (ψ₂ : State H2),

ψ = tensor ψ₁ ψ₂.

( Bell state )

Definition C2 : HilbertSpace := existT _ 2 (identity_matrix 2).

Definition ket (i j : nat) : State (tensor_product C2 C2) :=

tensor (basis_vector 2 i) (basis_vector 2 j).

Definition bell_state : State (tensor_product C2 C2) :=

(ket 0 0 + ket 1 1) / sqrt 2.

( Key lemmas )

Lemma bell_state_entangled : ~ separable bell_state.

Proof.

intro H_sep.

destruct H_sep as [ψ₁ [ψ₂ H_eq]].

( Use the Schmidt decomposition )

assert (H_schmidt : schmidt_rank bell_state = 2).

{ apply bell_state_schmidt_rank. }

( The Schmidt rank of a separable state is 1 )

assert (H_sep_rank : schmidt_rank (tensor ψ₁ ψ₂) = 1).

{ apply separable_schmidt_rank_one. }

( Contradiction )

rewrite H_eq in H_schmidt.

rewrite H_sep_rank in H_schmidt.

discriminate.

Qed.

Lemma operator_determined_implies_state_separable :

forall (ψ : State (tensor_product C2 C2))

(Φ : End(C2) -> End(C2) -> Hom C2 (tensor_product C2 C2))

(e_A e_B : End(C2)),

state_to_operator ψ = Φ e_A e_B ->

separable ψ.

Proof.

intros ψ Φ e_A e_B H_eq.

( The action of endomorphisms can be decomposed )

assert (H_decomp : exists ψ₁ ψ₂,

apply_operator (Φ e_A e_B) (ket 0 0) = tensor (apply_endo e_A ψ₁) (apply_endo e_B ψ₂)).

{ apply endomorphism_action_decomposes. }

destruct H_decomp as [ψ₁ [ψ₂ H_decomp]].

( Therefore ψ is separable )

exists ψ₁, ψ₂.

apply operator_to_state_injective.

rewrite <- H_eq.

apply H_decomp.

Qed.

set (A := C2).
```

§3.5 Philosophical Interpretation and New Insights

§3.5.1 The Philosophical Significance of the Theorem

The core claim of Theorem 3.1:

In the quantum world, there exist relations (entanglement) that cannot be completely determined by the intrinsic properties (endomorphisms) of objects.

Philosophical significance:

1. Ontological Significance

  • The reality of relations: relations are not "added" to objects, but are independent ontological constituents
  • Holism: the properties of the whole (an entangled system) cannot be reduced to the properties of the parts (individual particles)
  • Anti-reductionism: physical reality cannot be completely reduced to objects and their intrinsic properties

2. Metaphysical Significance

  • Priority of relations: in certain cases, relations are ontologically prior to objects
  • Structural realism: supports the position of structure realism
  • Non-separability: physical reality possesses a fundamental non-separability

3. Epistemological Significance

  • Limits of knowledge: knowing only the intrinsic properties of objects is insufficient to know all relations
  • Holistic cognition: certain knowledge must be obtained at the level of the whole
  • The measurement problem: quantum measurement involves the establishment of relations and cannot be reduced to changes in objects

§3.5.2 New Insights from the Formalization

Insight 1: A Precise Characterization of Intrinsic Properties

In the natural-language argument, "intrinsic property" is vague. The formalization reveals:

  • Intrinsic properties correspond to endomorphisms
  • An endomorphism is a way for an object to "act on itself"
  • This is the category-theoretic characterization of intrinsic properties

Insight 2: Levels of Determination

The formalization reveals that determination has different levels:

  • Weak determination: there exists a function Φ such that f = Φ(e_A, e_B)
  • Strong determination: Φ is a functor
  • Complete determination: Φ is a natural transformation

Theorem 3.1 refutes the weakest form of determination.

Insight 3: The Nature of Quantum Entanglement

The formalization reveals that the nature of quantum entanglement is:

  • A state in a tensor product space cannot be decomposed
  • The corresponding operator cannot be expressed as a combination of endomorphisms
  • This is a precise formulation of the irreducibility of relations

Insight 4: Possible Generalizations

The formalization suggests possible generalizations:

  • The irreducibility of relations in other physical theories
  • The irreducibility of social relations
  • The holism of biological systems

  • Relations are dynamic (morphisms can be composed)
  • Objects are "moments" of processes
  • Chapter 5 will further formalize Process Ontology

§3.5.4 Feedback on the Argument of Volume One

Revision 1: The Definition of Intrinsic Properties

Volume One should make clear:

"Intrinsic property" corresponds to endomorphism in category theory. This is a technical definition, but it captures the intuition of "a property of the object itself."

Revision 2: Precision of Determination

Volume One should distinguish:

  • Weak determination: there exists a functional relation
  • Strong determination: there exists a functorial relation
  • Complete determination: there exists a natural transformation

Revision 3: The Interpretation of Quantum Entanglement

Volume One should emphasize:

The nature of quantum entanglement is the non-separability of states in a tensor product space. This has a precise formulation in category theory: the operator corresponding to an entangled state cannot be expressed as a combination of endomorphisms.

Revision 4: The Possibility of Generalization

Volume One should suggest:

The irreducibility of relations is not limited to the quantum world. The formalization framework can be applied to other domains, such as social relations, biological systems, and so on.
