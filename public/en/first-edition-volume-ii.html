Volume II: Operational Framework
Bridging Theory and Practice

Volume II of Normative Foundations of Interstellar Civilization builds upon the theoretical foundations of Volume I, transforming abstract normative principles into operational indicator systems and institutional designs, serving as a crucial bridge connecting philosophical speculation with policy practice. The core task of this volume is to answer the "how" question: How to measure cosmic-scale impacts? How to design institutions adapted to deep uncertainty? How to implement these ideas in the real world?

Core Mission

This volume is divided into two major parts, systematically transforming the theoretical foundations of Volume I into operational tools and institutions:

Part IV "Indicator Systems" develops six major indicator systems to operationalize normative principles; Part V "Institutional Design" proposes six core institutional prototypes to provide frameworks for practice.

Part IV: Indicator Systems

Develops six major indicator systems to operationalize the three normative principles from Volume I, providing quantification tools for cosmic-scale decision-making:

Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA)
Assess the cosmic impact of the action, including scope, duration, reversibility, uncertainty, stakeholders, and systemic risk dimensions.
Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD)
Post-action probability space size-Pre-action probability space size
Universal Consciousness Scale(UCS)
Assess the level of consciousness and moral standing of the subject, including information integration, temporal depth, reflexivity, value sensitivity, and social dimensions.
Commitment Reversibility & Verifiability (CRV)
Evaluate the credibility of commitments, including observability, enforceability, and rollback capability
Civilization Development Index (CDI)
Assessing the level of civilization development.including cognitive, technological, social, ecological, and ethical dimensions
Memetic Biosafety Containment Level (MBCL)
The classification system of risk level of information system and so on.including memes, AI systems, and more

Indicator Design Principles

Each indicator system includes detailed calculation methods, data requirements, application scenarios, and limitation descriptions. The author particularly emphasizes that these indicators are not "objective truths" but "useful tools" that need continuous testing and revision in practice.

The indicator design follows SMART principles (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) while maintaining sensitivity to deep uncertainty.

Part V: Institutional Design

Proposes six core institutional prototypes to provide practical frameworks for interstellar civilization governance:

Intergenerational Trust Institution
Represents the interests of future generations, establishes independent trustee institutions, manages long-term resources, and conducts intergenerational impact assessments of contemporary decisions
Multi-Intelligence Tribunal
Handles disputes between different types of intelligent agents, adopting deliberative mechanisms with multi-agent participation
Cosmic-Level Ethics Review Board
Conducts ex-ante review of actions that may produce cosmic-scale impacts (Cosmic IRB)
Negentropy Resource Management System
Establishes global negentropy accounts and implements negentropy quota trading
First Contact Protocol
Regulates initial interactions between humanity and extraterrestrial civilizations, setting strict procedures and taboo red lines
Versioned Governance Mechanism
Allows governance rules to be version-managed and rolled back like software, adapting to rapidly changing technological environments

Protocol Stack Methodology

These institutional designs adopt a "protocol stack" methodology, borrowing from the layered architecture of computer networks. This layered design ensures institutions have both a stable core and flexibility to adapt to different contexts:

Each institution includes detailed organizational structures, decision-making procedures, accountability mechanisms, and implementation pathways.

Implementation Strategy

Political Feasibility and Gradual Implementation

This volume particularly focuses on political feasibility and gradual implementation. The author soberly recognizes the radical nature of these institutions and proposes a three-phase "pilot-expand-integrate" implementation strategy:

  • Phase I: Pilot — Conduct pilots in specific domains (such as AI governance, space exploration) to accumulate experience
  • Phase II: Expansion — Expand to more domains and establish cross-domain coordination mechanisms
  • Phase III: Integration — Integrate into global governance frameworks and interface with existing international institutions

Case Studies

This volume also includes numerous case studies demonstrating how indicators and institutions apply to specific contexts:

  • How to use CSIA to evaluate large collider experiments?
  • How to use intergenerational trusts to manage nuclear waste?
  • How to use the Multi-Intelligence Tribunal to handle AI rights disputes?

These cases make abstract theory concrete and enhance operability.

Target Audience

This volume is suitable for professionals in the following fields:

  • Policymakers and Government Officials
  • International Organization Staff
  • Corporate Governance and Compliance Experts
  • Legal Practitioners and Legislators
  • Risk Management Professionals
  • Technology Ethics Researchers

This volume serves as an operational manual for applying interstellar civilization studies theory to practice and a blueprint for future institutional innovation. The entire volume includes detailed calculation formulas, flowcharts, decision trees, and simulation model technical documentation.
