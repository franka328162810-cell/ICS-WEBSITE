Space Governance: Protecting Cosmic Negentropy

Humanity is becoming a spacefaring species. Commercial spaceflight, lunar programs, and Mars exploration are extending human activities beyond Earth. But how should space activities be governed? How should space resources be allocated? How can we avoid bringing Earth's mistakes into the cosmos? Interstellar Civilization Studies provides a cosmic responsibility perspective that transcends national sovereignty for these questions.

Core Challenges of Space Governance

The New Space Era

The 21st century has witnessed fundamental transformations in space activities:

Change Content
Actor Diversification From state-dominated to state + commercial + private multi-actor participation
Activity Expansion From low Earth orbit to Moon, asteroids, Mars
Objective Shift From exploration to development and utilization
Scale Growth Explosive growth in satellite numbers

Inadequacies of Existing Governance Frameworks

Existing frameworks such as the 1967 Outer Space Treaty face challenges:

Limitation Content
Sovereignty Framework Based on national sovereignty, difficult to address commercial and private activities
Resource Allocation Lacks clear provisions on space resource allocation
Environmental Protection Insufficient provisions for space environment protection
Temporal Horizon Lacks consideration of long-term and intergenerational impacts
Actor Limitation Only considers humans, not AI or possible extraterrestrial intelligence

ICS Space Governance Framework

Application of Core Principles

Normative Principle Application in Space Governance
Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP) Space activities must not systematically undermine the ability of future agents to utilize space
Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP) Protect the negentropy of the space environment, avoid irreversible destruction
Reversibility Principle (REV) Space activities should maintain reversibility as much as possible
Forbidden Red Lines (FRL) Prohibit irreversible large-scale depletion of cosmic negentropy

Reframing Core Questions

Traditional Question ICS Reframing
Who owns space resources? How to allocate space resources to protect recursive freedom?
How to prevent conflict? How to establish a framework for multi-agent coexistence in space?
How to promote development? How to sustainably utilize space negentropy?

Space Resources and Negentropy Responsibility

Unique Characteristics of Space Resources

Space resources have unique characteristics:

Characteristic Description
Scarcity Accessible space resources are limited
Non-renewable Most space resources are non-renewable
Intergenerational Impact Current use affects future generations' availability
Negentropy Value Space resources are an important component of cosmic negentropy

Application of Negentropy Responsibility Principle

Based on NRP, space resource use should follow:

Principle Content
Sustainability Resource use rate should not exceed sustainable thresholds
Intergenerational Equity Reserve sufficient resource shares for future generations
Multi-agent Equity Resource allocation should consider interests of diverse agents
Negentropy Protection Prioritize protection of high negentropy value resources

Space Resource Allocation Framework

Resource Type Allocation Principle Control Mechanism
Orbital Resources Fair use, avoid congestion Orbital allocation coordination mechanism
Asteroid Resources First-come-first-served + intergenerational reserve Mining permits + reserve fund
Planetary Resources International co-management, sustainable development International regulatory body
Energy Resources Shared benefits, technology transfer International cooperation framework

Space Environment Protection

Core Issues

The space environment faces multiple threats:

Threat Content Reversibility
Space Debris Decommissioned satellites, debris accumulation Partially reversible
Orbital Congestion Increasingly crowded low Earth orbit Reversible
Planetary Contamination Earth biology/materials contaminating other celestial bodies Possibly irreversible
Light Pollution Mega-constellations affecting astronomical observation Partially reversible

Application of Reversibility Principle

Based on REV, space environment protection should:

Domain Reversibility Requirements
Satellite Design Safe disposal plan after end of design life
Mission Planning Prioritize options with reversible environmental impact
Planetary Protection Strictly prevent irreversible biological/chemical contamination
Technology Selection Prioritize cleanable, recyclable technologies

Application of CSIA in Space Activities

Cosmic Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA) applied to major space activities:

Activity CSIA Level Review Requirements
Regular Satellite Launch CSIA-1 Routine review
Mega-constellation Deployment CSIA-2 Enhanced assessment
Asteroid Mining CSIA-2~3 Strict review
Planetary Terraforming (Mars) CSIA-3~4 Highest level review
Active METI CSIA-4 Special review

Preparation for Cross-Civilization Contact

Ethical Framework for SETI/METI

Activity Ethical Issues ICS Guidance

METI (Messaging) Actively sending signals may bring risks Highly cautious, requires global consensus
Post-Contact How to respond after contact with extraterrestrial intelligence? Need to establish protocols in advance

Cross-Civilization Contact Principles

Based on the Recursive Freedom Principle and intersubjectivity principle:

Principle Content
Equal Respect Treat extraterrestrial intelligence as potential equal agents
Communication Priority Prioritize establishing communication over confrontation
Reversibility Avoid irreversible contact actions
Representativeness Contact decisions should represent all humanity

Role of Multi-Intelligence Arbitration Court

The Multi-Intelligence Arbitration Court (MIAC) can provide an institutional framework for cross-civilization contact:

  • Establish contact protocols in advance
  • Assess the agency status of extraterrestrial intelligence
  • Coordinate cross-civilization communication and dispute resolution
