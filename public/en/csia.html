Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment: Measuring Actions at the Largest Scale

Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA) is the most comprehensive evaluation tool in the ICS indicator system, used to assess the potential impacts of major actions on a cosmic scale. When our decisions involve existential risks, space activities, AI development, and other issues that may affect entire civilizations or even cosmic negentropy, CSIA provides a systematic evaluation framework.

Why Do We Need CSIA?

The Shift in Scale

Humanity is acquiring capabilities that can impact cosmic scales:

Capability Cosmic-Scale Impact
Nuclear Technology Could terminate human civilization
AI Technology Could create superhuman intelligence with irreversible effects
Space Expansion Beginning to affect environments beyond Earth
Genetic Engineering Could alter the evolutionary direction of life
Geoengineering Could change planetary-scale climate systems

Assessment Requirements

Traditional Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) is insufficient to address these challenges:

Dimension Traditional EIA CSIA
Temporal Scale Years to decades Centuries to millions of years
Spatial Scale Local to global Planetary to interstellar
Subject Scope Contemporary humans Multiple subjects (including future subjects)
Uncertainty Calculable risks Deep uncertainty

Definition of CSIA

Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA) is a comprehensive framework for evaluating the potential impacts of major actions on a cosmic scale, integrating dimensions such as temporal scope, spatial scope, reversibility, and cascade risk.

Assessment Dimensions

CSIA includes four core assessment dimensions:

Dimension Assessment Content Scale
Temporal Scope Time scale of impact duration Logarithmic scale
Spatial Scope Spatial scale of impact diffusion Logarithmic scale
Reversibility Degree of impact reversibility [0, 1]
Cascade Risk Likelihood of triggering cascade effects [0, 1]

Temporal Scope

Level Time Scale Example
T1 1-10 years Ordinary policy decisions
T2 10-100 years Infrastructure construction
T3 100-1,000 years Climate change impacts
T4 1,000-100,000 years Nuclear waste disposal
T5 100,000+ years Species extinction, genetic modification
T6 Civilizational scale Existential risk events

Spatial Scope

Level Spatial Scale Example
S1 Local Local environmental impact
S2 Regional Regional environmental changes
S3 Global Climate change
S4 Planetary System Space debris, planetary terraforming
S5 Stellar System Stellar engineering
S6 Interstellar Interstellar communication, interstellar travel

Reversibility

Level Description
0.8-1.0 Highly reversible, can be fully restored
0.6-0.8 Mostly reversible, but with residual effects
0.4-0.6 Partially reversible, requires significant effort
0.2-0.4 Mostly irreversible
0.0-0.2 Almost completely irreversible

Cascade Risk

Level Description
Low Impact largely confined to direct scope
Medium May trigger chain reactions in related domains
High May trigger cross-domain systemic cascades
Extreme May trigger civilization-level cascade collapse

Impact Matrix

The core output of CSIA is an impact matrix:

                      Spatial Scope
                  S1   S2   S3   S4   S5   S6
             T1 │  ○    ○    ○    ·    ·    ·  │
             T2 │  ○    ○    ●    ○    ·    ·  │
Temporal     T3 │  ○    ●    ●    ○    ·    ·  │
  Scope      T4 │  ·    ●    ●    ●    ○    ·  │
             T5 │  ·    ○    ●    ●    ●    ○  │
             T6 │  ·    ·    ●    ●      │

             ○ = Possible Impact
             ● = Significant Impact
             · = No Significant Impact

Risk Levels

Based on comprehensive assessment results, CSIA risk levels are determined:

CSIA Level Conditions Requirements
CSIA-1 Impact limited to T1-T2/S1-S2, high reversibility Routine review
CSIA-2 Impact reaches T3/S3, medium reversibility Enhanced assessment
CSIA-3 Impact reaches T4-T5/S3-S4, low reversibility Strict review
CSIA-4 Impact reaches T5-T6/S4-S6, nearly irreversible Highest-level review
CSIA-5 May touch Forbidden Red Lines Principally prohibited

Integration with Other Indicators

CSIA is the top-level assessment tool in the indicator system, integrating all other indicators:

Input Indicator Role in CSIA
UCS Determines consciousness level of affected subjects, influences weighting
RFD Assesses impact on possibility space
CRV Assesses credibility of risk mitigation commitments
CDI Provides civilizational background data
MBCL Assesses information-level risks

Application Scenarios

Scenario CSIA Application
AGI Development Decisions Assess potential impact of AGI on civilization (CSIA-4 to CSIA-5)
METI Decisions Assess risks of sending signals to extraterrestrials (CSIA-4)
Planetary Terraforming Assess long-term impacts of Mars terraforming (CSIA-3 to CSIA-4)
Geoengineering Assess impacts of large-scale climate intervention (CSIA-3)
Gene Drive Release Assess ecological impacts of gene drives (CSIA-3)

Relationship with Institutional Design

CSIA assessment results are directly related to institutional design:

CSIA Level Review Institution Decision Procedure
CSIA-1 Routine institutions Standard procedure
CSIA-2 Professional committees Enhanced review
CSIA-3 Cosmic-level IRB Strict review
CSIA-4 Multi-Intelligence Tribunal Highest-level review
CSIA-5 — Principally prohibited
