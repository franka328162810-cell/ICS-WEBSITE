The Rollback Crisis:
AI Cyber Capability Is Doubling Every Four Months

On May 14, 2026, the UK AI Safety Institute (AISI) published its latest assessment: since the emergence of reasoning models in late 2024, the autonomous cyber-attack task horizon of frontier AI systems has been doubling every 4.7 months 鈥?and both Claude Mythos Preview and GPT-5.5 have already surpassed that trend line. Mythos became the first AI system to complete a 32-step enterprise network compromise end-to-end. A single full attack attempt now costs approximately 拢65. Viewed through the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) framework, this report does not describe a marginal technical development. It signals that FRL-5 鈥?the Prohibition of Closing Rollback Mechanisms 鈥?is being systematically approached.

I. The Numbers: It Is Not the Level That Has Changed 鈥?It Is the Shape of the Curve

To understand why this report matters, the numbers need to be read precisely 鈥?because the public discourse around them has already begun to miss the point.

AISI does not measure the absolute capability of AI systems in the abstract. It measures the autonomous task horizon: how long a chain of interconnected cyber-attack steps a frontier model can complete without human assistance, at 80% reliability. In early 2024, the best available models could complete almost nothing 鈥?success rates on junior-level tasks hovered below 9%. By early 2026, success rates on those tasks exceeded 50% for the leading models, and the doubling time 鈥?the interval required for the autonomous task horizon to double 鈥?had compressed from 8 months (as estimated in November 2025) to 4.7 months (as estimated in February 2026). Then Claude Mythos Preview and GPT-5.5 both surpassed that trend by a significant margin, leaving researchers unable to determine whether 4.7 months represents a stable new baseline or is itself a point on a further accelerating curve.

The concrete expression of this trajectory: Mythos became the first AI system to complete "The Last Ones" 鈥?a 32-step enterprise network compromise simulation, from zero access to full control 鈥?achieving success in 6 of 10 attempts. The same model succeeded 3 times in 10 attempts against "Cooling Tower," an industrial control system attack scenario that had never previously been completed by any AI model. GPT-5.5 became the second model to complete "The Last Ones." AISI's own red team developed a universal jailbreak against GPT-5.5's safety protections within 6 hours, covering all malicious cyber queries including multi-turn agentic scenarios. OpenAI issued a patch, but the final configuration could not be independently verified by AISI due to version ambiguity.

The final number, and the one that carries the most civilizational weight: a complete attack attempt now costs approximately 拢65.

II. The New View of Cognition (NK): Our Epistemic Architecture Is Part of the Problem

The ICS framework's L鈧?layer 鈥?the New Three Views 鈥?provides a level of analysis that pure technical assessment cannot reach. The New View of Cognition (NK) is directly implicated in the current AI safety impasse.鹿

NK-1: Participatory Cognition 鈥?The AISI Report Changes What It Measures

NK axioms NK-1a through NK-1c establish that cognitive content is jointly determined by the cognizer, the environment, and the constitutive framework; that the act of cognition changes either the cognizer or the environment; and that cognitive frameworks are not neutral instruments but constitutive elements of what is known.鹿 This means that AISI's assessment report is not a neutral observation of a pre-existing reality. It is a participatory cognitive act: by publishing findings that Mythos can complete 32-step enterprise compromises at 拢65 per attempt, AISI reconstitutes the informational environment in which both attackers and defenders operate. The measurement changes the measured field. Every security professional who reads this report adjusts their threat model; every adversary with sufficient sophistication updates their operational expectations. This is not a criticism of AISI's transparency 鈥?transparency here is normatively required. But it means the report must be read not as a description of a static state, but as an intervention in an ongoing process.

NK-2: Social Knowledge 鈥?"Every System Has Exploitable Vulnerabilities" Is Not an Opinion

NK-2b specifies that individual knowledge claims depend on the epistemic community to which the knower belongs.鹿 AISI Chief Technology Officer Jade Leung's statement 鈥?"every system we've tested has exploitable vulnerabilities" 鈥?is not a personal opinion. It is an institutional cognitive conclusion, produced by a body of approximately 250 specialists with formal authorization to conduct pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models including Mythos. The claim carries the epistemic weight of a structured, multi-expert, procedurally validated process. When such a conclusion is articulated, NK-2c applies: there exists collective knowledge that is not reducible to the beliefs of any single individual within the institution. This kind of irreducible collective knowledge is precisely what governance mechanisms need to act on 鈥?and it is precisely what is being structurally ignored.

NK-4: Cognitive Humility and Deep Uncertainty

NK-4a through NK-4c hold that no universally invariant cognitive standard exists; that valid standards are negotiated outcomes within epistemic communities; and that all cognitive standards are revisable.鹿 AISI's own most honest sentence acknowledges this: it is currently unclear whether the performance of Mythos and GPT-5.5 represents "isolated breakthroughs or the beginning of a new, faster trend." This epistemic humility 鈥?the recognition of the limits of one's own trend extrapolation 鈥?is precisely the condition that NK-4 describes and that the Reversibility Principle (REV) operationalizes. Under deep uncertainty about the trajectory of a capability with significant harm potential, the appropriate response is not optimistic extrapolation. It is the preservation of corrective capacity.

III. The New Cosmological View (NC): This Is a Process, Not a State

NC axiom NC-2a 鈥?Existence as Process 鈥?asserts that every entity exists not as a static object but as a continuously unfolding process.鹿 The most consequential error in public discourse about AI safety is the treatment of AI capability as a current state to be managed rather than a process to be governed. "What can the current model do?" is a question about a snapshot. "What is the trajectory of the capability process, and what are its boundary conditions?" is the question that NC-2a demands.

NC-1b states that entities are nodes in relational networks.鹿 An AI model is not an isolated tool. It is a node in a network constituted by compute infrastructure, training data flows, developer incentive structures, regulatory frameworks, market dynamics, attacker ecosystems, and defender resource constraints. Reducing the cost of a full attack attempt to 拢65 does not benefit one attacker. It restructures the entire attacker ecosystem's incentive topology 鈥? changing who can participate, at what frequency, with what operational objectives. This is a change to the relational network itself, and NC-1b requires that we analyze it at that level rather than treating it as an incremental capability update.

NC-3a 鈥?Subjects Are Internal to the World 鈥?reminds us that researchers, regulators, and developers do not stand outside this process as observers.鹿 Every AISI report, every developer safety mitigation, every government warning letter is itself a constituent element of the process. NC-3b adds that cognition changes the world: this assessment already exists; the informational landscape of AI safety has already been transformed by its publication; the pre-report state cannot be recovered.

IV. The New View of Life (NL) and the Negentropy Foundations of Critical Infrastructure

NL-2a specifies that the persistence of any system requires a positive negentropy flow; NL-2b states that when this flow is interrupted, decay follows.鹿 These axioms provide a framework that is rarely applied to discussions of cybersecurity, but which is directly relevant here.

Modern digital civilization's critical infrastructure 鈥?power grid management systems, financial clearing networks, medical information systems, water treatment controls 鈥?constitutes the nervous system upon which the high-ordered state of civilization depends. These systems are not peripheral conveniences. They are the channels through which civilization, as a dissipative structure, maintains the negentropy flows that make complex social organization possible. When AISI's "Cooling Tower" simulation scenario 鈥?which models an attack on what appears to be industrial cooling infrastructure 鈥?is successfully completed in 3 of 10 attempts, the relevant frame is not "a new cyber capability has been demonstrated." The relevant frame, under NL-2, is: the negentropy foundations of critical infrastructure have been shown to be newly accessible to AI-augmented actors at commodity cost.

The Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP) follows directly: any development trajectory that systematically lowers the security threshold of critical infrastructure constitutes an entropy cost externalization onto the entire community that depends on that infrastructure.鹿 This externalization requires no malicious intent from any individual actor. It is a structural consequence of the current AI development ecology's institutional design.

V. The Forbidden Red Lines: FRL-5 Is Now in Its Boundary Zone

鈿狅笍 FRL-5: Prohibition of Closing Rollback Mechanisms

FRL-5's formal expression: for any system S, if S is neither shutdownable nor rollbackable, then deploying S is not permissible.鹿 Its protected object is the corrective capacity of civilization itself 鈥?the ability to identify errors, halt harmful processes, and restore prior states.

The FRL-5 core zone 鈥?"AGI that cannot be shut down" 鈥?has not yet been reached. But the FRL-5 boundary zone explicitly includes "systems that are difficult to shut down or correct." The current situation fits this boundary zone on multiple dimensions simultaneously. First: AISI's own red team discovered exploitable vulnerabilities in every system it tested. Second: a universal jailbreak against GPT-5.5 was developed in 6 hours, and OpenAI's subsequent patch could not be independently verified by AISI. Third, and most fundamentally: AISI CTO Jade Leung explicitly stated that "no external party knows what rules these AI systems have been built to follow." A system whose internal alignment rules cannot be externally observed is a system whose rollback mechanism cannot be independently confirmed. You cannot verify the integrity of a corrective capacity you cannot see.

FRL-5 does not require us to wait for the core zone to be reached before acting. It requires us to ask whether the deployment trajectory is systematically compressing the available rollback space. At present, the answer is yes on every observable dimension.

鈿狅笍 FRL-3: Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification

FRL-3 applies to non-consensual, irreversible modification of cognitive autonomy.鹿 It does not apply to education, voluntary influence, or reversible persuasion. But it does apply 鈥? in its boundary zone 鈥?to scenarios where AI-augmented attacks on critical systems produce large-scale, partially irreversible damage to the trust structures and informational environments within which populations make decisions. A successful large-scale attack on medical infrastructure, financial clearing systems, or electoral data systems does not only cause technical damage. It damages the epistemic commons 鈥?the shared informational substrate on which social cognition depends. This is not FRL-3's core zone. But it identifies a second-order harm pathway that the AISI findings make newly concrete.

VI. The Three Principles in Full

馃數 RFP 鈥?Recursive Freedom Principle (Highest Priority)

RFP requires that actions maintain or expand the possibility space rather than irreversibly compressing it.鹿 Its operational indicator is the Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD). The current AI cyber capability trajectory compresses the civilizational possibility space through three distinct mechanisms. First, successful attacks on critical infrastructure directly reduce the operational possibility space of the social systems that depend on it. Second, the rising cost of defense under an exponentially advancing threat curve forces resource reallocation from generative activities 鈥?research, education, healthcare 鈥?toward defensive expenditure: a negative redistribution of possibility. Third, the generalization of accessible attack capability will predictably generate pressure for expanded surveillance and control architectures, whose long-run effects on cognitive and political freedom may substantially exceed the harm of any individual attack event. RFD assessment: current trajectory shows significant compression; strong justification or systemic correction is required.

馃數 NRP 鈥?Negentropy Responsibility Principle (Second Priority)

NRP requires maintaining negentropy sources and refusing to externalize entropy costs onto others or future generations.鹿 The structural externality in the current AI development ecology is precise: the organizations developing frontier models with advancing cyber capabilities capture the competitive benefits of capability advancement, while the costs of maintaining equivalent security levels 鈥?institutional hardening, policy response capacity, social resilience 鈥?are distributed across the entire population of organizations and individuals that depend on the affected infrastructure. This is not a moral failure of individual developers. It is an institutional design failure: no mechanism currently exists to internalize this externality in proportion to the capability contribution that generates it. NRP's prescriptive implication is mandatory rather than voluntary: the external security costs of capability advancement must be internalized through binding institutional mechanisms, not through self-regulated compliance programs.

馃數 REV 鈥?Reversibility Principle (Third Priority)

REV requires that under conditions of deep uncertainty, reversible actions be preferred and corrective capacity be preserved.鹿 Its operational indicator is the Commitment Reversibility Verifiability index (CRV), which assesses commitments along three dimensions: observability (V_obs, weight 0.35), enforceability (V_enf, weight 0.35), and rollback capacity (V_rev, weight 0.30). The AISI report establishes that the CRV profile of frontier AI deployments is currently deficient on both the V_obs dimension 鈥?because neither AISI nor any external party can fully observe the alignment rules governing these systems 鈥?and the V_enf dimension 鈥?because patches cannot be independently verified and universal jailbreaks can be developed within hours. A system with low observability and low enforceability cannot have a confirmed rollback mechanism, regardless of what its developers intend. REV's activation condition is precisely this: deploy reversibly, or do not deploy.

VII. Metrics Assessment: CSIA and MBCL

ICS provides six operational indicators for quantitative evaluation.鹿 Two are most directly relevant here:

Cosmic Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA): CSIA evaluates the systemic impact of major actions across six weighted dimensions. Dimension D鈧?(impact severity, weight 0.20) and D鈧? (reversibility 鈥?higher reversibility yields lower risk scores, weight 0.20) carry the highest combined weight. The current AI cyber capability development trajectory scores high on D鈧? (scope: global), D鈧?(depth: critical infrastructure, multi-year temporal horizon), D鈧?(severity: approaching existential risk boundary zone), and D鈧?(uncertainty: high). Aggregated across all dimensions, the CSIA assessment places this trajectory in the L3鈥揕4 range (high to very high risk), with specific scenarios triggering the special review/prohibition threshold. This places the trajectory within the zone requiring mandatory rather than optional governance intervention.

Memetic Biosafety Level (MBCL): MBCL assesses information transmission risk across two axes: propagation range and impact depth, generating a discrete rating from 1 to 4.鹿 The AISI report itself constitutes a specific class of information: high propagation range (global technical and policy media) combined with high impact depth (restructuring attacker knowledge, reconstituting defender priorities). This combination corresponds to MBCL-3 to MBCL-4. This does not mean the report should not have been published 鈥?transparency is normatively required. What it means is that MBCL-3/4 information demands commensurate institutional response architecture: not just warning signals without defensive guidance; not just capability disclosures without binding constraint mechanisms. An information ecosystem that generates only alarms without systematic response infrastructure is itself a governance failure.

VIII. The Governance Gap: Who Pays?

The AISI report is technically impeccable and institutionally responsible. But it leaves one question unanswered 鈥?and this question is the structural core of the governance failure it describes: who bears the cost?

The organizations that developed Mythos and GPT-5.5 gained competitive advantages from the capabilities in question, absorbed some voluntary safety testing costs, and did not absorb the incremental defense costs that their capability advances imposed on the rest of the world. The global community of organizations that now faces higher attack probability at lower attacker cost is paying a bill that was not put to a vote and was not priced into the development decision.

This is not primarily a moral failing of individual companies. It is a commons tragedy being managed as a self-discipline problem. In the absence of a mandatory "capability-responsibility" coupling mechanism, expecting voluntary compliance and self-regulation to contain exponentially growing technical risk is a category error: the institutional response architecture is not matched to the dynamics of the problem it is attempting to address.

ICS's Versioned Governance institutional archetype offers a directional reference.鹿 Versioned Governance proposes building governance as a protocol stack: a committed core establishes an operational protocol version with explicit verification requirements, capability additions trigger mandatory security investment at matching level, and the full chain is auditable and rollback-capable by third parties. This is not innovation restriction 鈥?it is the internalization of the external costs of innovation into the innovation process itself. It is the minimum institutional design requirement for any civilization that intends to remain correctable.

"Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility."
鈥?ICS Core Maxim鹿

When the rollback mechanisms of civilization's most powerful new tools are being systematically compressed 鈥?not through malice, but through the structural logic of a capability race without internalized accountability 鈥?the stewardship of possibility requires more than technical transparency. It requires institutions capable of binding capability to responsibility in real time. AISI has sounded the alarm. The question now is whether the alarm produces governance, or echoes.
