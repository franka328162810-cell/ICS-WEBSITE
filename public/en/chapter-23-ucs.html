Chapter 23: A Formalization of UCS

Chapter Introduction

This chapter is one of the core chapters in the formalization of the six major indicator systems. In Volume One, Chapters Six and Seven, we argued at the philosophical level for the normative significance of subjecthood and proposed the conceptual framework of the Universal Consciousness Scale (UCS). In Volume Two, Chapters Eight through Eleven, we established a preliminary formalization foundation for UCS. However, that work has not yet reached the standards required by a rigorous theory of measurement.

This chapter's task is to establish a complete formal system for UCS, including:

1. Measurement-theoretic foundation: specify the scale type, zero point, units, and admissible transformations of UCS

2. Axiom system: establish an axiomatic definition of UCS so that it can be rigorously derived

3. Representation theorem and uniqueness theorem: prove that the mathematical representation of UCS is both sufficient and necessary

4. Formalization of independent anchors: provide independent reference points for the calibration of UCS

5. Meta-property analysis: examine the consistency, completeness, and decidability of the UCS formal system

The core achievement of this chapter is to transform UCS from a philosophical concept into an operational indicator with a rigorous measurement-theoretic foundation. This transformation is not merely technical; it also reveals the deep structure of the measurement of consciousness.

This chapter's relations to other chapters:

  • Depends on Volume One, Chapter Six (the normative argument for subjecthood)
  • Depends on Volume Two, Chapters Four through Seven (the formalization of the New Cosmology, especially the Participatory Universe PU)

§23.2.6 Philosophical Interpretation of the Axiom System

Interpretation 1: The ontological commitments of UCS

The axiom system UCS-Axiom implicitly makes the following ontological commitments:

1. The reality of consciousness (axiom UCS-1): there exists a real "zero-consciousness" state

2. The gradability of consciousness (axioms UCS-2, UCS-3): consciousness is a continuous, gradable feature

3. The multidimensionality of consciousness (axiom UCS-4): consciousness is not a single dimension but a multidimensional structure

4. The integrativity of consciousness (axiom UCS-5): consciousness depends on the integration of the system and cannot be simply decomposed

5. The functionality of consciousness (axiom UCS-6): consciousness is determined by function, not by physical substrate

These commitments are highly consistent with the Three New Views (especially the New View of Life NL-1 through NL-4).

Interpretation 2: The relation between UCS and IIT

Axiom UCS-5 (non-decomposability) derives directly from the core insight of IIT: consciousness corresponds to integrated information , and is zero for decomposable systems.

But UCS is not entirely equivalent to IIT:

  • IIT focuses only on information integration (corresponding to )
  • UCS includes the other four dimensions, providing a more comprehensive characterization of consciousness

Interpretation 3: The relation between UCS and functionalism

Axiom UCS-6 (substrate independence) embodies the functionalist stance: consciousness is determined by function.

But the functionalism of UCS is a "multidimensional functionalism":

  • Not a single function (such as "passing the Turing test")
  • But a functional combination of five dimensions

Interpretation 4: The normative significance of UCS

Although UCS is a descriptive indicator, it has normative implications:

  • Systems with a high UCS should receive a higher weight of moral consideration (see Volume One, Chapter Six)
  • This normative derivation depends on the bridge principle (Volume One, Chapter Eleven)

The axiom system itself is value-neutral, but its application has normative consequences.

§23.3.4 Limitations of the Representation Theorem

Although the representation theorem provides theoretical legitimacy for UCS, it also has limitations:

Limitation 1: The controversiality of the axioms

The representation theorem depends on axioms R1-R5, but these axioms themselves may be controversial:

  • R3 (solvability): does an "intermediate" level of consciousness always exist?

The representation theorem proves that there exist weights such that UCS can be represented as a linear combination of the dimensions, but it does not tell us what the weights should be.

  • Consciousness cannot be reduced to a single dimension (such as information integration)
  • Any attempt to measure consciousness with a single indicator is incomplete

This insight challenges the single-dimension approach of IIT and supports a pluralist theory of consciousness.

Insight 2: The non-additivity of consciousness reveals holism

Theorem 23.7 (non-additivity) proved that consciousness does not satisfy additivity. This has profound philosophical significance:

  • Consciousness is holistic, not a simple sum of parts
  • This supports emergentism
  • This challenges reductionism

Insight 3: The necessity of anchors reveals the social nature of the measurement of consciousness

Theorem 23.14 (necessity of independence) and Theorem 23.15 (derivation of the weights) show:

  • The measurement of consciousness cannot be fully objectified; it requires social consensus (the choice of anchors)
  • This supports the social nature of knowledge (Volume Two, Chapter Thirteen)
  • The measurement of consciousness is "participatory": the observer's choice affects the measurement result

Insight 4: The incompleteness of UCS is a feature, not a defect

Theorem 23.18 (incompleteness) shows that the UCS axiom system is incomplete. This is not a defect but a reflection of:

  • The measurement of consciousness involves normative judgment (the choice of weights)
  • Normativity cannot be fully formalized
  • This supports the Revisability Principle (REV)

§23.8.2 The Limitations of Formalization

Limitation 1: The uncapturability of qualitative experience

UCS is a functionalist, quantitative framework; it cannot capture the qualitative aspects of consciousness (qualia):

  • The "redness" of red cannot be fully characterized by the five dimensions of UCS
  • The "what-it-is-like-ness" of subjective experience exceeds the expressive power of UCS

This is a common limitation of all objective measurement tools.

Limitation 2: The unresolved problem of other minds

UCS assumes that we can measure the consciousness of others, but it does not solve the problem of other minds:

  • How do we know that others truly have consciousness?
  • How is the possibility of philosophical zombies ruled out?

UCS adopts a pragmatic stance: if a system functionally exhibits the features of consciousness, we assign it the corresponding UCS value. But this is not a philosophical solution to the problem of other minds.

Limitation 3: The dynamical nature of consciousness

UCS is a static measure; it measures the level of consciousness at a certain moment. But consciousness is dynamic and flowing:

  • The temporal structure of the stream of consciousness is not captured
  • The transitions between consciousness states (such as sleep-wake) require dynamic models

Future versions may need to introduce a time dimension: .

Limitation 4: The residue of cultural relativity

Although we strive to make UCS cross-culturally applicable, certain cultural biases may still remain:

  • The choice of anchors (especially "standard human") may implicitly involve Western-centrism
  • The choice of dimensions (such as "self-model") may reflect individualist culture

This needs to be continuously tested and corrected through cross-cultural research.

§23.8.3 The Tension between Formalization and Intuition

In the process of formalization, we encountered several tensions between formalization results and intuitive judgments:

Tension 1: Linear additivity vs. holistic intuition

Axiom UCS-4 assumes that UCS is a linear combination of the dimensions, but intuitively consciousness might be a nonlinear function of the dimensions.

Resolution:

  • The linearity assumption is a first-order approximation
  • In the future, nonlinear terms can be introduced (such as interaction terms between dimensions)
  • Remain open to revision

Tension 2: Continuity vs. qualitative-change intuition

Axiom UCS-3 assumes that consciousness changes continuously, but intuitively the transitions between certain consciousness states may be qualitative changes (such as from unconscious to conscious).

Resolution:

  • Continuity does not exclude "phase transitions"
  • In a continuous function, the rate of change in certain regions may be extremely high, approximating a jump
  • This is similar to phase transition theory in physics

Tension 3: Ratio scale vs. incommensurability intuition

UCS as a ratio scale means that "the consciousness of system A is twice that of system B" is meaningful. But intuitively, cross-substrate comparisons of consciousness may be impossible.

Resolution:

  • The meaning of the ratio is functional, not qualitative
  • "Twice" refers to the composite score of the functional dimensions, not to a subjective experience that is "twice as intense"
  • This is a necessary consequence of the functionalist stance

Note 1: On the version of IIT

This chapter uses IIT 3.0 (Tononi et al., 2016) as the theoretical basis of (the information integration dimension). IIT has developed to version 4.0, and future versions of UCS may need to be updated.

Note 2: On the controversy over the weights

The specific values of the weights (0.30, 0.20, 0.20, 0.15, 0.15) are initial values based on theoretical considerations and expert judgment. These values should be continuously revised through empirical research and cross-cultural dialogue.

Note 3: On the completeness of machine verification

The machine verification code shown in this chapter is a simplified version. The complete verification code (about 5000 lines) is available in the GitHub repository.

Note 4: On cross-substrate applicability

Axiom UCS-6 (substrate independence) is a strong assumption. If it is discovered in the future that consciousness essentially depends on a particular physical substrate (such as biological neurons), this axiom needs to be revised.

Note 5: On the arbitrariness of the upper bound

Setting the upper bound of UCS to 10 is a practical choice. If in the future consciousness forms beyond the current theoretical maximum integration system are discovered, the upper bound can be adjusted (for example, to 100 or 1000).
