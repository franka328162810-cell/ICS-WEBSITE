Recursive Freedom Principle: Freedom as Guardianship of Possibility

The Recursive Freedom Principle is the core of Cosmic Responsibility Ethics, asserting that the exercise of freedom by any agent must not systematically undermine the conditions for freedom of other agents (including future agents). This principle extends freedom from individual rights to responsibility for possibility space.

Principle Statement

Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP)

The exercise of freedom by any agent must not systematically undermine the conditions for freedom of other agents (including future agents).

Formal Expression
∀x∀y∀t: Free(x,t) → ¬Diminish(Action(x,t), Free(y,t+n))
Where:
  • x, y: any agents (including humans, AI, possible extraterrestrial intelligence)
  • t, t+n: current moment and future moment
  • Free(x,t): agent x possesses freedom at moment t
  • Action(x,t): action of agent x at moment t
  • Diminish: systematic undermining

Key Terms Analysis

Meaning of "Recursive"

"Recursive" indicates the self-applicability of the principle:

  • The principle applies not only to current agents
  • But also to future agents
  • And these future agents bear the same responsibility

This creates an "infinitely extending" chain of responsibility.

Understanding "Freedom"

"Freedom" here is not the capacity for arbitrary action, but rather:

  • Positive: the capacity to realize valuable goals
  • Negative: freedom from coercion and oppression
  • Substantive: possession of resources and conditions necessary for exercising freedom

"Systematic Undermining"

The principle prohibits systematic undermining, not any impact:

  • Single, local, compensable impacts are not prohibited
  • What is prohibited: persistent, widespread, irreversible undermining
  • The focus is on protecting possibility space, not every specific option

Justification of the Principle

Derivation from the New Three Views

The Recursive Freedom Principle can be derived from the New Three Views:

Argument Steps:

  • Existence is relational (NC-1) → agents have intrinsic connections
  • The essence of life is maintenance and growth of negentropy (NL-2) → freedom is a form of negentropy
  • We face deep uncertainty (NK-3) → need to protect the freedom space of future agents
  • Conclusion: we should not systematically undermine the conditions for freedom of others (including future agents)

Relationship with Kantian Principle

The Recursive Freedom Principle can be seen as an extension of Kant's universalization principle:

Kantian Principle Recursive Freedom Principle
Applies to contemporary rational beings Extends to future agents
Focuses on universalizability of actions Focuses on protection of conditions for freedom
Formal principle Contains substantive content (possibility space)

Core Maxim

"Freedom is the guardianship of possibility."

This maxim reveals the deeper meaning of the Recursive Freedom Principle:

  • Freedom is not consumption: freedom is not the power to exhaust options
  • Freedom is responsibility: true freedom includes responsibility for possibility
  • Guardianship of the future: we are guardians of future possibilities
  • Possibility space: the most fundamental object of protection is possibility space itself

Practical Guidelines

How Does the Recursive Freedom Principle Guide Action?

Question Guidance
Whether to develop a technology? Assess: will it systematically undermine conditions for freedom of future agents?
Whether to make a decision? Check: does it preserve sufficient reversibility and option diversity?
How to allocate resources? Principle: do not overconsume, preserve resource base for the future
How to treat AI? Standard: if AI has agent status, protect its conditions for freedom

Coordination with Other Principles

The Recursive Freedom Principle needs to be used in coordination with other principles:

  • Negentropy Responsibility Principle: concretizes "conditions for freedom" — negentropy is the material basis of freedom
  • Reversibility Principle: operationalizes "not undermining" — maintain reversibility under uncertainty
  • Forbidden Red Lines: delineate absolute boundaries — certain undermining is absolutely prohibited

Relationship with Intergenerational Justice

The Recursive Freedom Principle provides a new theoretical foundation for intergenerational justice:

Traditional Intergenerational Justice Recursive Freedom Principle
Focuses on resource allocation Focuses on possibility space
Mainly considers near generations Extends to deep time future
Anthropocentric Cross-substrate, multi-agent

The core insight of the Recursive Freedom Principle: we have responsibility to the future not because future people are our descendants, but because they are free agents whose conditions for freedom should not be undermined by us.
