Dialogue with Environmental Ethics: From Earth to Cosmos

Environmental ethics explores the ethical relationship between humans and the natural environment, developing rich ideas about sustainability, intergenerational responsibility, and the value of non-human beings. ICS shares environmental ethics' concern for sustainability but extends the perspective from Earth to the cosmos, using the concept of negentropy to unify understanding of environmental value.

Overview of Environmental Ethics

Core Questions

Core questions addressed by environmental ethics:

Question Content
Human-Nature Relationship What should be the relationship between humans and the natural environment?
Value of Non-Human Beings Do animals, plants, and ecosystems have intrinsic value?
Sustainability What is sustainable development? How to achieve it?
Intergenerational Responsibility What environmental responsibilities do we have to future generations?
Environmental Justice How should environmental benefits and burdens be justly distributed?

Major Positions

Position Core Claim Representatives
Anthropocentrism Only humans have intrinsic value, nature has instrumental value Traditional ethics
Animal Liberation/Rights Sentient animals have moral status Peter Singer, Tom Regan
Biocentrism All life has intrinsic value Paul Taylor
Ecocentrism Ecosystems as wholes have intrinsic value Aldo Leopold
Deep Ecology Nature has intrinsic value, humans are part of ecological web Arne Naess

Shared Concerns

ICS and environmental ethics share the following concerns:

Shared Concern Content
Sustainability Focus on long-term sustainability of development
Intergenerational Responsibility Recognize responsibility to future generations
Non-Human Value Acknowledge non-human beings may have some value or status
Systems Thinking Emphasize holistic and systemic thinking
Awareness of Limits Acknowledge resource limitations

Conceptual Unification
