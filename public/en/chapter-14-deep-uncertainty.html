Chapter 14: A Formal System for Deep Uncertainty

Chapter Opening Guide

In Chapter 3, §3.7 of Volume One, we tentatively defined the concept of deep uncertainty (DU) and treated it as one of the core propositions of the New View of Cognition (NK-3). We argued that deep uncertainty differs from risk (describable by a probability distribution) and Knightian uncertainty (whose probability distribution is unknown but learnable), being instead a fundamental, irreducible uncertainty.

However, the first edition's treatment had two major defects:

1. Inadequate precision in conceptual delimitation: the boundary between deep uncertainty and other types of uncertainty was blurred

2. Insufficient rigor in formalization: rigorous mathematical characterization and provable theorems were lacking

The goal of this chapter is to construct the formal system DU (Deep Uncertainty), providing a rigorous mathematical foundation for deep uncertainty. We shall:

  • Precisely distinguish risk, Knightian uncertainty, and deep uncertainty (§14.1-14.2)
  • Establish the formal language and model of deep uncertainty (§14.3)
  • Prove the irreducibility theorem for deep uncertainty (§14.4)
  • Provide an epistemological foundation for the Reversibility Principle (REV) (§14.5)
  • Demonstrate applications of DU in areas such as AI alignment and climate decision-making (§14.6)

Relations of This Chapter to Other Chapters

  • Backward link: relies on the philosophical argument for deep uncertainty in Chapter 3 of Volume One
  • Lateral link: together with Chapter 13 SK (sociality of knowledge), it constitutes the formalization foundation of the New View of Cognition
  • Forward link: lays the foundation for Chapter 15 RK (revisability of knowledge) and Chapter 16 NK (integration of the New View of Cognition)
  • Normative link: provides epistemological support for the Reversibility Principle (REV) of Chapter 9 of Volume One

§14.1　Construction of the Formal System DU

§14.1.1　Motivation and Goals

Why do we need to formalize deep uncertainty?

Deep uncertainty is a core feature of interstellar civilization decision-making. Consider the following situations:

Situation 1: AGI development

  • We do not know when AGI will appear (temporal uncertainty)
  • We do not know the capability limits of AGI (capability uncertainty)
  • We do not know whether the value alignment of AGI is possible (fundamental uncertainty)
  • We do not even know the correct definition of "alignment" (conceptual uncertainty)

Situation 2: Interstellar colonization

  • We do not know the environment of the target star system (informational uncertainty)
  • We do not know the future development of technology (technological uncertainty)
  • We do not know the evolution of the colonists' values (value uncertainty)
  • We do not know what alien civilizations we may encounter (existential uncertainty)

These uncertainties cannot be described by probability distributions, because:

1. We lack sufficient historical data

2. The system itself is evolving

3. The observer's actions will change the system

4. The conceptual framework itself may not apply

Goals of the formalization

Construct the formal system DU so that it can:

1. Precisely distinguish: rigorously distinguish risk, Knightian uncertainty, and deep uncertainty

2. Characterize structure: reveal the intrinsic structure of deep uncertainty

3. Prove theorems: prove the irreducibility of deep uncertainty

4. Guide decisions: provide a formalization framework for decision-making under deep uncertainty

5. Support REV: provide an epistemological foundation for the Reversibility Principle
