Reviewer Evaluations

"This is constitutional source code written for a future yet to arrive."

"For too long, we have treated 'interstellar civilization' as a playground for science fiction writers, forgetting that it is essentially a rigorous engineering problem concerning thermodynamics, information theory, and game theory. The author has not attempted to predict the future, but has done something more ambitious and dangerous: the author attempts to install an 'operating system' for that unfathomable future by reconstructing the underlying definitions of universe, life, and cognition.

The 'Recursive Freedom Theory' and 'Cosmic Responsibility Ethics' proposed in this work are no longer floating metaphysical rhetoric, but are anchored on the solid foundation of non-equilibrium thermodynamics. At this delicate moment when human civilization attempts to governance protocol stack we urgently need. It is austere, precise, yet filled with ultimate care for the possibilities of life."

"From philosophy's 'clouds' to protocol's 'soil,' this work completes the missing engineering turn."

"Contemporary ethics often appears pale and powerless in the face of technological singularity because it lacks the ability to calculate long-range externalities. What is astonishing about this work is that it refuses to stop at moral preaching, but forcibly 'compiles' philosophical principles into executable code—UCS (Universal Consciousness Scale), RFD (Recursive Freedom Degree), and CSIA (Cosmic System Impact Assessment).

This is a bold academic adventure. The author shows us that in the fog of deep uncertainty, true freedom is not doing whatever one wants, but 'not closing off the future.' For those who care not only about AI governance and outer space law, but also about the fate of intelligence itself in the universe, this is a blueprint that must be kept on the desk and studied repeatedly. It marks that 'Interstellar Civilization Studies' has finally matured from a literary imagination into an auditable normative science."

"At the twilight of the Westphalian system, it is the first ray of legal light toward the interstellar commons."

"If we view current space law as patches for version 1.0, then the 'protocol stack' constructed by this work is a systematic reconstruction of version 2.0. It keenly points out that in a universe where multi-agent, cross-substrate entities coexist, traditional 'anthropocentric' jurisprudence is not only arrogant but dangerous.

Through the axiomatic derivation of the 'New Three Views,' this work establishes a shared ontological negotiation table for 'heterogeneous subjects' (whether AI, post-humans, or potential others). It has both the grand narrative of looking up at the stars and highly operational institutional prototypes such as 'Multi-Intelligence Arbitration Court' and 'Rollback Mechanism.' This is a memorandum for future legislators, reminding us to complete the dimensional upgrade of civilizational subjectivity inward before expanding outward."

"It not only explains the world, but participates in constructing the world's boundaries."

"Just as quantum mechanics revealed the ontological status of the observer, this work reveals the ontological responsibility of cognition. It argues with a chilling precision that every observation we make, every protocol we establish, every technological decision we take, is collapsing the wave function of cosmic possibilities in real time.

The author defines this work itself as a 'provisional covenant' and 'updatable artifact,' and this humility precisely embodies the true spirit of science. In an era filled with illusions of certainty, this work embraces 'deep uncertainty' and attempts to build an improvisational yet robust order within it. This is not merely an academic monograph, but a solemn promise that human reason makes to itself before venturing toward the stars."
