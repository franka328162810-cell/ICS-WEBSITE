II. From Intersubjectivity to Possibility Protection

BP- requires agents to adopt principles that can be reasonably accepted by other rational agents. The question now is: what kind of principles can be reasonably accepted by other rational agents?

Proposition 11.4 (The unacceptability of possibility compression)

A principle of action that irreversibly compresses the possibility space of others cannot be reasonably accepted by those others.

Argument 11. (The argument for the unacceptability of possibility compression)

Premise P1: rational agents value their possibility space (because the possibility space is the precondition for realizing any goal)

Premise P2: actions that irreversibly compress the possibility space of others permanently reduce those others' opportunities for choice

Premise P: actions that permanently reduce opportunities for choice harm the fundamental interests of others

Premise P4: a principle of action that harms the fundamental interests of others cannot be reasonably accepted by those others

Conclusion: a principle of action that irreversibly compresses the possibility space of others cannot be reasonably accepted by those others

Proposition 11.5 (The acceptability of possibility protection)

A principle of action that preserves or expands the possibility space of others can be reasonably accepted by those others.

Argument 11.4 (The argument for the acceptability of possibility protection)

Premise P1: rational agents value their possibility space

Premise P2: actions that preserve or expand the possibility space of others maintain or increase those others' opportunities for choice

Premise P: actions that maintain or increase opportunities for choice serve the interests of others

Premise P4: a principle of action that serves the interests of others can be reasonably accepted by those others

Conclusion: a principle of action that preserves or expands the possibility space of others can be reasonably accepted by those others

III. Survival Rationality and Possibility Space

BP-1 (the survival-rationality principle) is also related to possibility space. Survival requires possibility space, because survival means continuing to exist in the future, and continuing to exist requires that there be possible states that can be attained.

Proposition 11.6 (The link between survival and possibility space)

An agent's survival depends on the non-emptiness of its possibility space. If the possibility space is fully compressed (becoming the empty set), the agent cannot survive.

Formal statement:

Argument 11.5 (The argument for the link between survival and possibility space)

Premise P1: survival means continuing to exist in the future

Premise P2: continuing to exist requires that there be possible states that can be attained

Premise P: the set of possible states is precisely the possibility space

Premise P4: if the possibility space is empty, then no possible state can be attained

Conclusion: survival depends on the non-emptiness of the possibility space

IV. From Survival Rationality to Possibility Protection

Combining BP-1 (the survival-rationality principle) and Proposition 11.6, it can be derived that agents have reason to protect possibility space.

Proposition 11.7 (Survival rationality entails possibility protection)

From BP-1 (the survival-rationality principle) and Proposition 11.6, rational agents with a will to survive should maintain their possibility space.

Argument 11.6 (The argument that survival rationality entails possibility protection)

Premise P1 (BP-1): rational agents with a will to survive should maintain their conditions of survival

Premise P2 (Proposition 11.6): the non-emptiness of the possibility space is a necessary condition of survival

Premise P: if X is a necessary condition of Y, and an agent should maintain Y, then the agent should maintain X

Conclusion: rational agents with a will to survive should maintain their possibility space

V. From Self-Protection to Mutual Protection

So far, we have argued that agents have reason to protect their own possibility space. But the Recursive Freedom Principle requires mutual protection—protecting not only one's own possibility space but also the possibility space of others.

Proposition 11.8 (From self-protection to mutual protection)

From BP- (the intersubjectivity principle), agents should protect not only their own possibility space but also the possibility space of others.

Argument 11.7 (The argument from self-protection to mutual protection)

Premise P1 (Proposition 11.7): agents should maintain their own possibility space

Premise P2 (BP-): agents should adopt principles that can be reasonably accepted by other rational agents

Premise P: if an agent protects only its own possibility space while compressing the possibility space of others, this principle cannot be reasonably accepted by those others

Premise P4: if a principle cannot be reasonably accepted by others, then it violates BP-

Intermediate conclusion C1: an agent should not protect only its own possibility space while compressing the possibility space of others

Premise P5: a principle that protects the possibility space of others can be reasonably accepted by those others

Premise P6: a principle that can be reasonably accepted by others conforms to BP-

Conclusion: agents should protect the possibility space of others

VI. The Recursiveness of Possibility Protection

Possibility protection is recursive: the principle of protecting possibility space should itself remain open, so that it can be revised when necessary.

Proposition 11.9 (The recursiveness of possibility protection)

The principle of protecting possibility space should apply to the principle itself. That is, the application of the principle should not close off the possibility of revising the principle.

Argument 11.8 (The argument for the recursiveness of possibility protection)

Premise P1: possibility space includes cognitive possibility space (possible beliefs, principles, theories)

Premise P2: if the application of a principle closes off the possibility of revising the principle, then it compresses the cognitive possibility space

Premise P: compressing the cognitive possibility space violates the principle of possibility protection

Conclusion: the principle of possibility protection should apply recursively to itself

This recursiveness is one source of the name "the Recursive Freedom Principle." The principle requires protecting not only the possibility space of action but also the revisability of the principle itself.

§11.2.2 The Meaning of "Freedom"

I. Review of Traditional Concepts of Freedom

In the Western tradition of political philosophy, there are multiple concepts of freedom. Isaiah Berlin, in Two Concepts of Liberty (1958), distinguished negative freedom from positive freedom.

Negative Freedom:

  • Definition: freedom from external interference
  • Core question: "To what extent am I not interfered with by others?"
  • Representative figures: Hobbes, Locke, Mill, Berlin

Positive Freedom:

  • Definition: freedom of self-realization and self-mastery
  • Core question: "Who is the master?"
  • Representative figures: Rousseau, Hegel, Green

Republican Freedom:

  • Definition: freedom from domination
  • Core question: "Am I subject to the arbitrary power of others?"
  • Representative figures: Machiavelli, Harrington, Pettit

II. The Difference between Recursive Freedom and Traditional Concepts of Freedom

The "freedom" in the Recursive Freedom Principle differs importantly from traditional concepts of freedom.

Definition 11.15 (Recursive freedom)

Recursive freedom is the preservation and expansion of possibility space. Recursive freedom is not freedom from interference (negative freedom), not self-realization (positive freedom), and not freedom from domination (republican freedom), but rather the openness of possibility space.

III. The Distinctive Features of Recursive Freedom

Recursive freedom has the following distinctive features:

Feature one: oriented toward the future

Recursive freedom concerns future possibilities rather than the present state. It requires keeping future choices open, not merely protecting present choices.

Feature two: relationality

Recursive freedom is relational. The possibility space depends on the relational network, so recursive freedom depends on relations among agents. This is consistent with NC-1 (relational reality).

The Capability Approach developed by Amartya Sen and Martha Nussbaum is akin to recursive freedom.

Core concepts of the capability approach:

  • Capabilities: the set of functionings that an individual can choose

The relation between recursive freedom and the capability approach:

  • Similarity: both concern a set of possibilities rather than actual choices
  • Difference: recursive freedom places greater emphasis on the time dimension and recursive structure

Recursive freedom can be understood as the temporal extension of the capability approach. The capability approach concerns the current set of capabilities, whereas recursive freedom concerns the long-range evolution of the set of capabilities.

§11.5.2 Possible Exceptions

I. The General Framework of Exceptions

Definition 11.28 (Exceptions)

Exceptions are cases in which the RFD falls below but the action is still acceptable. Exceptions must satisfy the special-justification conditions.

Special-justification conditions:

1. Necessity: the action is a necessary means to an important goal

2. Proportionality: the benefits of the action are proportional to the compression of the possibility space

3. Minimality: the action is the minimal-compression plan for realizing the goal

II. Specific Exceptions

Exception one: necessity (emergency avoidance)

In emergencies, it may be necessary to take actions that compress possibility space in order to avoid a greater loss.

Case 11.4 (Emergency avoidance)

Situation: A ship is about to sink, and the captain must decide whether to jettison part of the cargo to save the passengers' lives.

Analysis:

  • Jettisoning the cargo compresses the possibility space of the cargo's owner
  • But not jettisoning the cargo would cause the passengers' death, compressing a greater possibility space
  • Therefore, jettisoning the cargo satisfies the special-justification conditions

Conclusion: emergency avoidance is an exception to the Recursive Freedom Principle

Exception two: Pareto improvement

If an action, although compressing some possibilities, expands more important possibilities, it may be acceptable.

Case 11.5 (Pareto improvement)

Situation: A technological innovation will eliminate some old technologies but open up more new possibilities.

Analysis:

  • Eliminating the old technologies compresses the possibility space of those who depend on them
  • But the new technologies open up more new possibility space
  • If the net effect is an expansion of possibility space, the action is acceptable

Conclusion: Pareto improvement can be an exception to the Recursive Freedom Principle

Exception three: negotiated consent

If all affected agents agree to compress certain possibilities, it may be acceptable.

Case 11.6 (Negotiated consent)

Situation: A community decides to permanently protect a piece of land as a nature reserve, forgoing the possibility of development.

Analysis:

  • Permanent protection compresses the possibility space of development
  • But the community members agreed to this decision through negotiation
  • The negotiated consent provides a special justification

Conclusion: negotiated consent can be an exception to the Recursive Freedom Principle

§11.5.3 Handling Conflicts with Other Principles

I. Potential Conflict with the Negentropy Responsibility Principle

Conflict Scenario 11.1

Situation: A certain technology can expand possibility space but requires consuming a large amount of negentropy resources.

Conflict analysis:

  • The Recursive Freedom Principle supports this technology (it expands possibility space)
  • The Negentropy Responsibility Principle may oppose this technology (it consumes negentropy resources)

Handling:

1. Assess the net effect: does the expansion of possibility space exceed the cost of negentropy consumption?

2. Seek alternatives: is there an alternative technology that consumes less negentropy?

3. Time trade-off: does short-term negentropy consumption buy long-term expansion of possibility space?

II. Potential Conflict with the Reversibility Principle

Conflict Scenario 11.2

Situation: A certain irreversible action can significantly expand possibility space.

Conflict analysis:

  • The Recursive Freedom Principle supports this action (it expands possibility space)
  • The Reversibility Principle may oppose this action (it is irreversible)

Handling:

1. Assess the degree of irreversibility: is it completely irreversible or partially reversible?

2. Assess the certainty of the expansion of possibility space: is the expansion certain or uncertain?

3. Risk assessment: if the expansion fails, what are the consequences?

III. A General Framework for Handling Principle Conflicts

Definition 11.29 (A framework for handling principle conflicts)

When the Recursive Freedom Principle conflicts with other principles, the following framework is adopted:

Step 1: Identify the conflict

  • Clarify which principles are in conflict
  • Assess the nature and degree of the conflict

Step 2: Assess the weight of each principle

  • In the specific context, what is the relative importance of each principle?
  • Does any principle have priority?

Step : Seek a reconciliatory solution

  • Is there a solution that simultaneously satisfies each principle?
  • Can the conflict be reduced by modifying the action?

Step 4: If reconciliation is impossible, make a trade-off

  • Determine the priority according to context
  • Choose the overall optimal solution

Step 5: Record the reasons for the decision

  • Explicitly record why a particular solution was chosen
  • Provide a reference for similar situations in the future

Chapter 11 Appendix: The Axiom System of the Recursive Freedom Principle

Axiom System 11.A (Recursive Freedom Principle Axiom System RFP-AX)

[Basic Definitions]

Definition D1 (possibility space):

PS(S) = {s ∈ Σ : Achievable(S, s, R_S)}

where Σ is the state space and R_S is the relational network in which S is situated

Definition D2 (measure of the possibility space):

M: PS → ℝ⁺

M(PS) = α·Dim(PS) + β·Vol(PS) + γ·Conn(PS)

where α + β + γ = 1

Definition D (recursive freedom degree):

RFD: A → ℝ

RFD(a) = [M(PS(s₁, T)) - M(PS(s₀, T))] / M(PS(s₀, T))

where s₀ is the state before the action, s₁ is the state after the action, and T is the evaluation time range

Definition D4 (special justification):

SpecialJustification(a) ↔ Necessity(a) ∧ Proportionality(a) ∧ MinimalCompression(a)

[Axioms]

Axiom A1 (possibility-protection axiom):

∀a ∈ A: O(RFD(a) ≥ θ_min) ∨ O(SpecialJustification(a))

Actions should maintain the possibility space, or have a special justification

Axiom A2 (recursion axiom):

Apply(RFP) → ¬Close(Revise(RFP))

The application of the principle should not close off the possibility of revising the principle

Axiom A (intersubjectivity axiom):

∀S₁, S₂ ∈ Agents: Protect(S₁, PS(S₁)) → Protect(S₁, PS(S₂))

Protecting one's own possibility space entails protecting others' possibility space

Axiom A4 (irreversible-compression axiom):

IrreversibleCompress(a, PS) → ¬Permissible(a) ∨ StrongJustification(a)

Irreversible compression requires strong justification

Axiom A5 (temporal-extension axiom):

∀t₁ < t₂: Protect(PS(t₁)) → Protect(PS(t₂))

Protecting the present possibility space entails protecting the future possibility space

[Inference Rules]

Rule R1 (RFD evaluation rule):

If RFD(a) ≥ θ_min, then a satisfies RFP

Rule R2 (special justification rule):

If RFD(a) < θ_min and SpecialJustification(a), then a satisfies RFP

Rule R (non-satisfaction rule):

If RFD(a) < θ_min and ¬SpecialJustification(a), then a does not satisfy RFP

Rule R4 (recursive application rule):

RFP applies to the application of RFP itself

[Theorems]

Theorem T1 (possibility-protection theorem):

∀a: Permissible(a) → (RFD(a) ≥ θ_min ∨ SpecialJustification(a))

Proof: follows directly from Axiom A1

Theorem T2 (mutual-protection theorem):

∀S₁, S₂: RFP(S₁) → Protect(S₁, PS(S₂))

Proof: follows from Axiom A and the definition of RFP

Theorem T (recursivity theorem):

RFP applies to the revision of RFP

Proof: follows from Axiom A2
