The Line of the Day — On 29 September the White House handed six labs’ voluntary pledges to their own auditors and, the same day, renamed “artificial intelligence” as “super intelligence”. Two hundred and thirty-seven days earlier the EU ratified the first legally binding AI treaty — and it remains the only party to have done so. Weeks earlier the pledge track — the voluntary code that was supposed to cover what the treaty could not — quietly acquired its first independent verification annex. The two paths did not swap places; they converged on the same destination from opposite ends: a world in which frontier AI is governed by commitments that are monitored, and not by obligations that are enforceable.

Opening Note

Editor's Greeting

I had planned to write this week about the technical detail of Anthropic’s withdrawal of its pause commitment, which is the more interesting change, until I set the White House Accord beside the ratification record of the Framework Convention. Its weight is not in what was added but in what is missing: an industry pledge with no penalties has acquired external audit, while a treaty already signed and already ratified is not yet in force. My judgement is narrower than it sounds: this is not a report on whether AI governance is weakening, but a document about where a decision now sits — and it sits inside a company’s own board committee and with the auditor the audited party selects.

— Xue Zhixin · 2 October 2026

What Happened

Fact one · characterisation: on 29 September six frontier labs signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence at a one-page joint commitment with four layers of control — capability monitoring, an internal review team, independent external audits, and a board committee — but no penalties, no named regulator and no reporting deadline. The President called it “morally binding” and, the same day, changed the official term from “artificial intelligence” to “super intelligence”. Constraint entered not through law but through wording.

Fact two · event: in the same quarter the voluntary path acquired its verification machinery. The third layer of the White House Accord introduces independent external auditors; the EU’s General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, in force since July, carries a presumption of conformity — signatories may cite it to assert compliance to regulators, at the price of accepting its audit format. February’s revision of the Responsible Scaling Policy showed the same mechanism’s weakness from the other side: when Anthropic replaced its hardest pause commitment with a two-part test it applies to itself, SaferAI cut its governance score from 2.2 to 1.9. Verifiable, but self-judging.

Fact three · the drift: the treaty track is moving too, but in the opposite direction. The Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI — the first legally binding international treaty in this field — opened for signature in September 2024 and drew 21 signatories; on 15 May 2026 the European Union ratified, becoming its first Party. Entry into force requires five ratifications, at least three from Council of Europe members; that threshold has not been met. A treaty signed, ratified, and binding on no one; and beside it, an industry pledge with no legal force and a working audit apparatus. Two tracks, running at different speeds, and only one carries an alarm.

Fact four · the measure that matters (to be distinguished from the above): the pledge track now attaches a number to each declaration — declared compute above a stated threshold — which converts a promise into a checkable quantity. Reach narrowed; measurability widened. Any forecast about which path governs frontier AI in 2027 turns on whether that number survives its first audit cycle.

Material Signals  4 items

Brain-Computer Interfaces  Invasive brain-computer interfaces have entered first-in-human long-term implant trials, where firmware and neural-signal updates are pushed remotely by the vendor while the participant holds no rollback key. Switchability is written into the product description, not into the participant’s hands.

International Space Law  The White House Accord leaves the choice of auditor to the audited firms themselves, and permits an “evaluator” rather than an independent audit. A norm can be softened without being scrapped — it is enough to change who holds the right to check.

Nuclear Proliferation  The only legally binding AI treaty in the field was drafted and opened for signature by the Council of Europe, drew 21 signatories, and has been ratified by the European Union alone. The binding regime and the voluntary regime are now running in opposite directions.

Synthetic Biology · Dual Use  The EU’s General-Purpose AI Code of Practice writes in a presumption of conformity: signatories may cite it to assert compliance to regulators, at the price of accepting its audit format. The threshold for admission is now a citable document.

Continued Watch  2 items

Existential Risk  The US Vice President explicitly rejected an FDA-style AI regulator, arguing that existing FTC and Justice Department powers suffice. The contest has moved from whether to regulate to who enforces. The enforcer’s identity has become the live variable.

Quantum Computing  The New Delhi summit’s 88-nation declaration and the Frontier AI Impact Commitments signed by 13 developers list safety duties as voluntary. The migration timetable is being set where the hardware and compute sit, not where the declaration is drafted.
