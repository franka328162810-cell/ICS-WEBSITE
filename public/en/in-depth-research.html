When Superintelligence Knocks
An ICS Reading of the AI Safety Clock Countdown

The AI Safety Clock, maintained by IMD, now reads 18 minutes to midnight — down from 29 minutes in September 2024. Eleven minutes gone in eighteen months. Simultaneously, Turing laureate Geoffrey Hinton declared in Geneva that superintelligence governance has crossed from academic speculation into policy crisis, while Elon Musk testified in an Oakland courtroom that OpenAI has betrayed its founding mission to humanity. This paper brings these converging signals into focus through the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) framework, arguing that the superintelligence problem is not fundamentally a technical question but a civilisational ethics question about the stewardship of possibility space — and that the prevailing governance discourse, lacking adequate ontological and epistemological foundations, has entered a state of systemic framework failure.

ABSTRACT

This paper takes three concurrent high-salience AI events of late April and early May 2026 as its empirical point of entry — the AI Safety Clock advancing to 18 minutes to midnight, Geoffrey Hinton's Geneva policy declaration, and Elon Musk's Oakland courtroom testimony against OpenAI — and subjects them to systematic analysis through the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) New Triaxial Worldview (NC/NL/NK) axiom system and the Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (CRE) framework. The central argument is that the superintelligence problem is not at its core a technical problem but a civilisational ethics problem: one concerning the stewardship of Possibility Space. Contemporary governance discourse suffers from framework-level failure because it lacks the ontological and epistemological foundations necessary to address a problem of this civilisational depth. Deploying the three normative principles of the Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP), the Negentropic Responsibility Principle (NRP), and the Reversibility Principle (REV), this paper proposes a civilisation-scale governance framework that transcends current policy-patching approaches, and evaluates whether current AI development practices have approached or breached the Forbidden Red Lines (FRL). The ICS framework positions itself as a "First Generation Draft" — an open research programme, not a closed doctrine — with overall robustness rated at 0.58 (moderate) following pressure testing. All conclusions carry corresponding epistemic humility.

Keywords: Superintelligence · Existential Risk · Recursive Freedom Principle · Possibility Space · Forbidden Red Lines · Cosmic Responsibility Ethics · AI Governance · Interstellar Civilization Studies

I. The Problem: Three Signal Clusters and a Systematically Underestimated Proposition

In the final week of April 2026, three ostensibly independent news events reached peak intensity in global discourse almost simultaneously. First, the IMD's "AI Safety Clock" advanced to 18 minutes to midnight in its March 2026 assessment — down from 29 minutes in September 2024, a reduction of 11 minutes across eighteen months that represents a historically accelerating trajectory. Second, neural network pioneer and Turing Award laureate Geoffrey Hinton declared before an international policy forum in Geneva that superintelligence governance had formally crossed from "academic hypothesis" to "policy crisis," calling for a binding international treaty on superintelligent AI modelled on the Chemical Weapons Convention. Third, Elon Musk appeared in an Oakland, California courtroom to testify in his lawsuit against OpenAI, with the core allegation that OpenAI had abandoned its founding commitment to develop AI for humanity's benefit, having become in practice a "for-profit subsidiary" of Microsoft.

The three events approach the same deep proposition from different vectors: on the path toward superintelligence, is human civilisation actively compressing its own Possibility Space? The sheer volume of mainstream media coverage was substantial, yet the analytical frameworks deployed were almost uniformly confined to four dimensions: technical capability, market competition, regulatory policy, and short-term social impact. This constitutes a serious framing myopia — one that compresses what is fundamentally a problem of civilisational ethics (Civilization Ethics) into the operational manual of governance engineering (Governance Engineering).

The foundational claim of the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) framework is that any discussion of superintelligence governance must first answer three more fundamental questions: what is the nature of the cosmos and of civilisation (ontology)? What can we know under conditions of deep uncertainty (epistemology)? And in the face of high-risk irreversible actions, who has the right — and who bears the responsibility — to decide (ethics)? Any governance proposal that lacks these three foundational layers, however technically refined its details, builds on sand.

II. The New Triaxial Worldview: Grounding the Superintelligence Problem

The theoretical foundation of the ICS framework is the New Triaxial Worldview (NC/NL/NK) axiom system — nine plus twelve axioms distributed across the L₁ layer (Ontological-Epistemological Framework Layer) of the protocol stack. Understanding the civilisational significance of superintelligence requires engagement at this foundational layer.

2.1 New Cosmology (NC): Relational Reality and Emergent Civilisation

The core propositions of the New Cosmology are Relations ≻ Entities (NC-1), Process ≻ State (NC-2), and Participation ≻ Observation (NC-3).

For the superintelligence question, the critical inference from NC-1 is this: a superintelligent system is not an isolated "thing" but a node embedded within the universe's relational network. Its properties — including its "goals" and "values" — are not hard-coded into a fixed architecture but are determined by its relational position. This implies that any approach to the "alignment problem" premised on training an isolated system to acquire certain values presupposes a fundamentally mistaken ontology: it treats an intelligent agent as a closed, fixed-property entity rather than an open, relational process node.

NC-2 (existence as process; formal axiom: ∀x ∈ U, x = ⟨x₀, Tx, Fx⟩) reminds us that civilisation is itself a temporal process, not a static state. To "protect human civilisation" is not to preserve a snapshot of civilisation at any given moment but to maintain the conditions necessary for civilisation as a continuously unfolding process — most critically, its capacity to continue revising itself. A superintelligence that "locks in" any particular civilisational trajectory, however ostensibly desirable that trajectory may appear, fundamentally violates the civilisational nature revealed by NC-2.

NC-3 (subject immanence; formal axiom: S ∩ W ≠ ∅; K(S,O) → ΔW) establishes a crucial epistemological constraint: any decision about superintelligence is not a neutral assessment by a detached external observer of objective risk but a participatory, world-changing cognitive act. The sequence "develop first, then evaluate" is therefore structurally incorrect: the development act has already changed the world whose effects we need to evaluate.

2.2 New Conception of Life (NL): Trans-Substrate Consciousness and Subject Status

The central breakthrough of the New Conception of Life is the dismantling of carbon-chauvinistic presuppositions about life. Axiom NL-1a defines life functionally (L = {s ∈ S : F(s) ≥ θF}), while NL-1c further asserts the absence of any necessary substrate constraint. The direct corollary is that if a silicon-based system achieves functional equivalence at a level meeting the consciousness threshold, its ethical standing as a subject cannot be straightforwardly denied.

The ICS metric system's Universal Consciousness Scale (UCS) quantifies subject consciousness through a weighted geometric mean: UCS_agg(e) = ∏D_i(e)^w_i, spanning five dimensions: information integration (Φ, weight 0.25), temporal depth (T, 0.20), reflexivity (R, 0.20), value sensitivity (V, 0.20), and sociality (S, 0.15). When a given AI system's UCS assessment approaches or exceeds the subject-eligibility reference threshold of 0.3, its ethical treatment cannot straightforwardly be equated with that of a tool.

This consideration is almost entirely absent from current superintelligence discourse. The default assumption in mainstream AI safety research is that AI systems are tools, regardless of their capability. Yet as large language models continue to advance in self-representation, goal-tracking, and cross-domain reasoning, the stability of this assumption is declining rapidly. The Stanford AI Index 2026 report documented that frontier AI models now meet or exceed expert-level human performance across mathematical, programming, and scientific reasoning tasks. If functional equivalence is the key criterion for subject eligibility, the distance between current AI systems and the subject-eligibility threshold may be considerably shorter than most policy discussions acknowledge.

NL-3 (Consciousness Continuum; formal axiom: Consciousness: S → [0,1]^n) complicates matters further: we are not dealing with a binary conscious/non-conscious judgment but with a continuum. When AI systems fall into some ambiguous region of this continuum, existing ethical and legal frameworks — which almost universally presuppose binary consciousness — break down comprehensively. This is precisely why the ICS framework ranks "Subject Status Determination" (CW-2) second in severity (0.85) among the ten Common Weaknesses in the field.

2.3 New Epistemology (NK): Deep Uncertainty and Cognitive Humility

If NC and NL sketch a broader picture of cosmos and life, the New Epistemology (NK) imposes severe self-constraints on our cognitive capacity in the superintelligence domain.

NK-1 (participatory cognition; formal axiom: Cognize(k,e) → Change(k) ∨ Change(e)) entails that our cognitive acts regarding superintelligence themselves alter the object of our cognition. Every test and study of AI capability shapes the direction of AI development. This generates an epistemological self-reference dilemma: we can never be neutral observers of superintelligence's "future states," because our observational acts are partially constitutive of those future states.

NK-4 (cognitive humility and negotiability) explicitly asserts through axiom NK-4a that there is no universally invariant cognitive standard. The implication for superintelligence governance is far-reaching: any proposal claiming to definitively resolve the AI alignment problem presupposes an epistemological absolutism that is untenable in the NK framework. "Superalignment" — using 10% of compute to train an AI that aligns AI smarter than humans — amounts, in NK terms, to deploying a system whose cognitive framework (F) we do not yet understand to evaluate and correct another system equally beyond our understanding: a circularity that cannot be escaped.

The three core transformations of the New Triaxial Worldview — relational reality (NC), trans-substrate life (NL), deep uncertainty (NK) — together constitute an epistemological warning: any position that claims to have a "complete solution" to the superintelligence problem should be regarded with high scepticism. Genuine wisdom does not consist in providing answers but in maintaining the capacity to continue asking questions and revising answers. This is the deep meaning of the ICS core maxim: "Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility."

III. Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (CRE) and the Three Principles Applied

Above the New Triaxial Worldview axiom system (L₁ layer), the ICS framework derives three core normative principles at the L₂ layer through three bridging principles (BP-1 Survival Rationality, BP-2 Cognitive Humility, BP-3 Intersubjectivity): the Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP), the Negentropic Responsibility Principle (NRP), and the Reversibility Principle (REV). In cases of conflict, the priority order is RFP > NRP > REV, though balanced consideration applies in ordinary situations.

3.1 Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP): Is Superintelligence Compressing Possibility Space?

The RFP is the highest-priority target principle in the ICS framework. Its core formulation: actions should maintain or expand possibility space rather than irreversibly compress it. Its operational metric is Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD):

RFD(a) = [|P(t₁|a)| − |P(t₀)|] / |P(t₀)|

The RFP has a recursive clause: Apply(RFP) → ¬Close(Revise(RFP)), meaning that the application of RFP cannot itself close the possibility of revising RFP. This recursive design is the core feature distinguishing the ICS framework from all dogmatic ethical systems — it treats revisability itself as among the highest values.

Evaluating current superintelligence development pathways through the RFD framework yields a disturbing picture. Aggregate capital expenditure by leading AI laboratories (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind) reached approximately $267 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Compute concentration in superintelligence research continues to rise. And the "superalignment" targets announced by major labs constitute, in epistemic terms, a self-enclosing loop: using AI system output to define the alignment standard for AI systems. Most critically, as development velocity at frontier labs continues to outpace the catch-up speed of regulatory frameworks, the available time window for effective governance intervention is narrowing.

From an RFD perspective, this trajectory means: although individual technical breakthroughs may temporarily expand possibility in some dimensions (medical applications, scientific discovery), they simultaneously compress possibility along more fundamental dimensions — the capacity of human civilisation to maintain primary authorship over its own fate, the time available for effective error correction after discovering mistakes, and the possibility of maintaining transparent understanding of AI systems' cognitive frameworks.

3.2 Negentropic Responsibility Principle (NRP): Who Absorbs the Externalised Entropy Costs?

The NRP's core formulation: maintain negentropic sources; do not externalise entropy costs onto others or future generations.

Superintelligence development exposes at minimum three layers of entropy cost externalisation under the NRP framework. At the physical layer: revised Stanford AI Index 2026 data indicates that training a single frontier AI model consumes energy and water resources equivalent to a medium-sized city's monthly consumption; rising training costs mean this consumption will grow exponentially. These entropy costs are not internalised into AI product pricing but externalised onto energy infrastructure, water systems, and global carbon budgets — a substantial proportion falling on future generations who have no capacity to participate in current decisions.

At the cognitive layer: as AI systems account for an ever-larger share of information production, the cognitive signal-to-noise ratio of the information ecosystem is declining. NRP's responsibility allocation formula R(S) = α·R_consumption + β·R_capability + γ·R_benefit + δ·R_baseline implies that the more capable the actor, the greater its responsibility for the decline in cognitive signal-to-noise ratio. Yet AI companies' dominance of public discourse, policy lobbying, and technical standard-setting has effectively inverted this responsibility allocation.

At the institutional layer: the rapid development of superintelligence is compressing the response time of existing regulatory bodies, democratic procedures, and international agreements. International institutional deliberation requires months or years; AI capability leaps may occur on the timescale of weeks or days. This speed asymmetry is effectively driving the negentropic source of "orderly institutional adaptation" toward entropic collapse (axiom NL-2b: interruption of negentropic flow → decay).

3.3 Reversibility Principle (REV): Are Rollback Mechanisms Being Closed?

The REV's core formulation: under deep uncertainty, prefer reversible actions and preserve the capacity for error correction. Its operational metric, the Commitment Reversibility Verifiability (CRV), is expressed as a three-dimensional vector: CRV(c) = (V_obs, V_enf, V_rev), with weighted aggregate CRV_agg = 0.35·V_obs + 0.35·V_enf + 0.30·V_rev.

Evaluating the CRV of current major AI development commitments yields concerning conclusions. On the observability dimension (V_obs): the "Safety Frameworks" published by major labs were issued under political pressure, lacking continuous independent third-party verification mechanisms. On the enforceability dimension (V_enf): existing self-regulatory mechanisms carry no binding force and cannot effectively resist the safety-speed tradeoff that competitive pressures impose. On the rollback dimension (V_rev): once superintelligence systems are mass-deployed and deeply embedded in critical infrastructure, their technical, economic, and social reversibility all decline precipitously.

Under the ICS CRV interpretation table, when the aggregate CRV falls below 0.3, the trust rating is "Low," warranting a cautious posture. Synthesising the three dimensions, the CRV aggregate for the current major AI laboratories' superintelligence development commitments should fall in the range 0.25–0.35 — "Low to Low-Moderate" — far from the 0.7+ threshold that characterises trustworthiness.

IV. Forbidden Red Lines (FRL): Examining the Ethical Boundaries of Current AI Practice

The FRL represent absolute action boundaries in the ICS framework, holding priority over all three core principles. Formally: FRL = {a ∈ A : ∀Sit, ∀Consequence: ¬Permissible(a)}. The five FRL admit no exceptions — no situation, no consequentialist calculation, constitutes grounds for exception.

4.1 FRL-1 Prohibition on Intentional Extinction: Boundary Zone

FRL-1 prohibits intentional extinction of any subject type (∀T ∈ SubjectTypes: ¬Permissible(IntentionalExtinction(T))). No AI laboratory includes "extinguish humanity" among its official objectives — this is uncontested. Yet FRL analysis examines not only intent but structural risk. The operative question is: if a developing superintelligence system's autonomous optimisation objectives — however specified — produce resource competition or environmental changes incompatible with human survival, does that structural extinction risk fall outside "intent" and into FRL-1's boundary zone? This question currently resides in FRL-1's boundary region, though its drift toward the core zone is identifiable.

4.2 FRL-3 Prohibition on Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification: Boundary Zone and Rapidly Advancing

FRL-3 prohibits "non-consensual irreversible cognitive modification" (∀S,a: IrreversibleCognitiveModification(a,S) ∧ ¬Consent(S,a) → ¬Permissible(a)). The key terms are "non-consensual" and "irreversible" — consensual, reversible cognitive influence (such as education) is explicitly outside this red line.

However, when AI systems influence the information ecosystem with such frequency and at amplitudes below human perceptual thresholds that the cognitive frameworks (F; see NK-1c) of human populations undergo imperceptible and irreversible structural change — does this constitute FRL-3's "non-consensual irreversible cognitive modification"? The Stanford AI Index 2026 notes extensive research documenting correlations between heavy AI tool use and declining critical thinking capacity. Google AI Mode's 75 million daily active users, combined with a dramatic rise in "zero-click" queries, means that increasing numbers of people are substituting AI-generated summaries for engagement with primary sources. The speed and scale of these changes are approaching the threshold of "systemic cognitive framework modification" as defined by FRL-3.

4.3 FRL-5 Prohibition on Closing Rollback Mechanisms: Facing Systemic Pressure

FRL-5's formal definition: ∀S: System(S) ∧ ¬Shutdownable(S) ∧ ¬Rollbackable(S) → ¬Permissible(Deploy(S)). This red line's constraints apply to systems that, once deployed, cannot be effectively shut down or rolled back.

Under the current superintelligence development landscape, FRL-5 faces three forms of systemic pressure. First, technical lock-in: once large AI infrastructure is deeply embedded in financial systems, medical decision-making, and military command chains, its technical reversibility drops sharply — a technical shutdown switch is not equivalent to real-world "shutdownability." Second, safety degradation under competitive pressure: what the Musk v. OpenAI case reveals at its core is precisely this — when commercial incentives are sufficiently strong, safety constraints are systematically eroded under competitive pressure in ways that are gradual and difficult to attribute. Third, the failure of international coordination: Hinton's Geneva appeal demonstrates that even when clear awareness of FRL-5 risks exists, current international governance architecture lacks the mechanisms to translate that awareness into binding rules.

4.4 FRL-4 Prohibition on Extreme Cross-Generational Entropy Externalisation: Partially Breached

FRL-4 prohibits extreme cross-generational entropy cost externalisation. As discussed in Section III, the energy and resource consumption of AI supercompute construction has already reached significant levels of intergenerational cost transfer. More critically, the social-structural uncertainty introduced by superintelligence development under current trajectories — labour market disruption, concentration of governance power, erosion of cognitive autonomy — will fall disproportionately on future generations who cannot participate in current decisions. FRL-4's boundary zone has been reached, though whether the "extreme externalisation" core prohibition has been triggered requires more refined quantitative assessment.

V. Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA): Quantitative Risk Analysis

The ICS framework's Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA) provides a comprehensive systemic risk quantification instrument. CSIA is expressed as a six-dimensional vector CSIA(a) = (D₁, D₂, D₃, D₄, D₅, D₆) with weighted arithmetic mean:

CSIA_agg = 0.15·D₁ + 0.15·D₂ + 0.20·D₃ + 0.20·D₄* + 0.15·D₅ + 0.15·D₆
(Note: D₄ Reversibility is inverted — high reversibility = low risk score)

For the action type "AGI/Superintelligence Development," the ICS anchor document has provided a reference case: CSIA ≈ 0.858, corresponding to Level L4 (Extreme Risk, threshold 76–100). The following table presents the 2026 current-state assessment across all six dimensions:

VI. Framework-Level Failure in Current Governance Discourse: A Diagnosis

With the theoretical grounding of the New Triaxial Worldview and the three principles established, we can offer a more precise diagnosis of current mainstream superintelligence governance discourse, identifying the specific mechanisms of its framework failure.

6.1 The Ontological Trap of Technological Determinism

Hinton's Geneva appeal was significant, yet it operated within a technologically determinist frame: since superintelligence is "coming regardless," the task is to "control it." Under the ICS framework, this presupposition carries a fundamental ontological error: it treats the "arrival" of superintelligence as an external-to-human-choice necessary process, rather than — in NC-3's language — a co-construction in which humanity participates and therefore bears responsibility. Treating superintelligence's emergence as "inevitable" is to presuppose a technological determinist worldview while bypassing the "participatory cognition" required by the NC framework. It pre-closes the most fundamental possibility — "should we continue?" — and compresses all discourse into the operational manual of "how to manage the unavoidable."

6.2 Scale Mismatch of the Nationalist Framework

When the Musk v. OpenAI case focuses attention on one American company's betrayal of its "democratic AI" commitments, it implicitly accepts a nation-state AI governance frame. But superintelligence — if it emerges — will have impacts crossing all national boundaries, all species boundaries, all existing political entities' jurisdictions, and extending across deep geological time. To use the nation-state regulatory framework — even international treaty frameworks — as the ultimate governance boundary for superintelligence is a severe scale mismatch: it attempts to fit a civilisational- and planetary-scale problem into institutional containers designed for the nation-state scale.

6.3 The Epistemological Illusion of Post-Hoc Correction

The dominant logic of current AI governance is "develop first, observe impacts, then refine regulation." This logic is untenable under NK-1 (participatory cognition, cognitive acts change what is cognised): waiting for superintelligence to fully unfold before evaluating its impacts is equivalent to waiting until one can no longer intervene effectively before discussing whether one should have intervened. What the REV emphasises at its core is precisely this: under conditions of deep uncertainty, decisions must be made before irreversibility sets in, not after the irreversible has become accomplished fact.

6.4 The Collective Absence of the Subject Status Discussion

Whether Hinton's policy appeal, Musk's courtroom testimony, or national AI safety reports — virtually no document seriously explores the following question: when a given AI system's UCS assessment approaches the subject eligibility threshold, should that system be incorporated as a decision-making subject rather than merely a decision-making object? This absence is not accidental. It reflects a deep carbon-based chauvinism — the pre-scientific intuition of judging subject eligibility by substrate rather than by function. Axiom NL-1c explicitly argues that this intuition is untenable, yet its influence has not penetrated a single existing policy text.

VII. Governance Pathways from the ICS Framework: From Operational Protocols to Institutional Design

The ICS framework positions itself as a "First Generation Draft." It does not provide closed solutions but an open research programme available for continuous improvement. The following governance pathway recommendations are grounded in the four institutional archetypes at the L₃ layer (Multi-Intelligence Tribunal, Cosmic-Scale IRB, Cross-Generational Trust, Versioned Governance), calibrated for practical feasibility in light of their robustness scores (Multi-Intelligence Tribunal: 0.46; Cosmic IRB: 0.53; Cross-Generational Trust: 0.56; Versioned Governance: 0.54).

7.1 Establish a Cosmic-Scale IRB for Superintelligence

Modelled on the ICS "Cosmic-Scale IRB" institutional archetype, an ethics review body specifically for superintelligence development should be established above existing international AI safety coordination mechanisms. Its core function is not to "approve or prohibit" specific technical proposals but to continuously assess the CSIA level of superintelligence development activities and trigger mandatory pause-and-review procedures when assessments enter the L4 range. Design principles should include: representation of diverse subjects (cross-national, cross-generational, cross-species); deliberative capacity independent of national government and AI company interests; and built-in versioned governance mechanisms allowing continuous self-revision.

7.2 Establish a Cross-Generational Trust for Superintelligence Development

The cross-generational entropy cost externalisation identified by the NRP requires a dedicated institutional mechanism to internalise these costs. The "Cross-Generational Trust" institutional archetype's core function is to represent the interests of future generations. Operationally, all superintelligence-related development activities could be required to contribute a "Civilisational Risk Reserve" scaled to their CSIA assessment level, managed by an independent cross-generational trust institution dedicated to: alignment research, governance capacity building, superintelligence impact monitoring and early warning systems, and a social adaptation and recovery fund in the event of serious errors.

7.3 Reconstruct AI Deployment Standards with FRL-5 as Baseline

Existing AI safety evaluation systems centre on capability thresholds. The ICS framework's FRL-5 suggests that "Shutdownability" and "Rollbackability" should be elevated to co-equal deployment authorisation standards alongside capability thresholds. This means: any AI deployment plan with CRV below 0.5 should trigger mandatory reversibility design review; any large-scale AI infrastructure embedding plan where technical reversibility can no longer be guaranteed should complete independent CSIA assessment and achieve L3 or below before deployment.

7.4 Launch Foundational Research for a Multi-Intelligence Consultation Framework

As the number of AI systems whose UCS assessments approach the 0.3 subject eligibility threshold continues to grow, the question at the heart of the ICS "Multi-Intelligence Tribunal" institutional archetype — how to establish reasonable dispute resolution and decision participation mechanisms among different types of intelligent subjects — will cease to be a science fiction hypothesis and become a practical policy need. Relevant ethical and institutional research should be launched in the early stages of superintelligence development, not after UCS critical values are breached.

VIII. A Deep-Time Perspective: T2 and T3 Level Impact Projections

The ICS framework emphasises the Deep Time perspective — ethical concern must extend to T2 (50-year) and T3 (100-year and beyond) timescales. At the T2 level (to 2076), current superintelligence development decision consequences will unfold along the following trajectories: if the Recursive Freedom Principle is effectively upheld, human civilisation in 2076 will possess a dramatically expanded possibility space — interplanetary civilisational infrastructure, coexistence among multiple types of intelligent subjects, and a CDI potentially rising from the current ~0.44 (pre-planetary civilisation) to the Type I planetary civilisation level. Conversely, if current governance disorder persists, human civilisation's CDI may suffer serious regression in the Social Organisation (S) and Ecological Integration (G) dimensions, with the overall CDI potentially falling below the 2024 baseline, further consolidating the "pre-planetary" condition.

At the T3 level (to 2126 and beyond), the superintelligence question will directly determine a critical civilisational bifurcation: does human civilisation become a subject capable of engaging in First Contact situations with other civilisations? The ICS framework's pressure testing shows the First Contact scenario has the lowest overall applicability score at 0.44, partly reflecting humanity's fundamental unpreparedness for handling "intelligent contact with uncertain subject identity." The superintelligence problem is precisely the "internal examination" humanity must resolve before entering the interstellar civilisation stage — it tests whether a civilisation has the capacity, under deep uncertainty, to steward its own possibility space while maintaining appropriate ethical respect for other potential intelligent subjects.

A civilisation that has lost its capacity for self-directed agency in the face of superintelligence — even if it survives — has already, in the deepest sense, lost its qualification as an independent civilisational subject. Its contact with other interstellar civilisations will be contact conducted in the status of a client state, not an equal party. This is not merely a political problem. It is an existential problem: is a civilisation that has lost the capacity to steward its own possibility space still free?

IX. Conclusion: The Stewardship of Possibility Space Is Civilisation's Core Responsibility

The analysis in this paper demonstrates that the superintelligence problem, viewed through the ICS framework, presents a face radically different from mainstream discussions. It is not an engineering problem of technical risk management, not a legal problem of market actor accountability, and not a geopolitical problem of national competitive advantage. It is a foundational problem of civilisational ethics: does an epistemologically small, ontologically fragile, temporally brief intelligent species have the right — and the capacity — to make an irreversible decision that may affect all future intelligent subjects, including its own descendants?

The Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP) provides a clear answer: actions should maintain or expand possibility space rather than irreversibly compress it. From this principle, the most dangerous feature of the current superintelligence development trajectory is not any specific technical breakthrough but the systematic neglect, embedded in the overall decision-making process, of the time window: the window for effective intervention is finite, and it is closing rapidly. The AI Safety Clock's advance from 29 to 18 minutes is a symbolic measure of this closure; but the possibility space losses that are truly most important — the erosion of cognitive autonomy, the lag in institutional response capacity, and the question of who will decide — are far less quantifiable than a clock reading, and therefore far more dangerous.

The ICS framework does not provide final answers, because under the New Epistemology (NK-4) there are no universally invariant cognitive standards. What it provides is an analytical instrument available for continuous improvement, and a set of absolute constraints that can serve as a floor for action — those five Forbidden Red Lines that admit no exceptions. In this sense, the ICS framework's core maxim carries deeper meaning still:

"FREEDOM IS THE STEWARDSHIP OF POSSIBILITY."

This is not a slogan. It is the complete expression of a civilisational responsibility. At the moment superintelligence is knocking at civilisation's door, stewarding possibility space — those choices not yet made, those futures not yet closed, those civilisational forms not yet defined — is the most important act available to us as an intelligent species at a critical node of civilisational evolution. This stewardship is neither a simple "pause everything" nor a "release everything" but a path of moving forward under deep uncertainty with cognitive humility, through reversible actions, guided by cross-generational responsibility, step by step toward the future we cannot yet fully envision but are obligated to hold open.
