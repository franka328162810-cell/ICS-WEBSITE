The Recursive Freedom Dilemma of AGI: Path-Dependency Lock-in Effects from GPT-5

Abstract

Core Argument

The rapid development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is creating an unprecedented "path-dependency lock-in" effect that systematically compresses the choice space of future intelligent agents (both human and AI) through three dimensions: technical standardization, value embedding, and infrastructure dependency, thereby violating the core principle of Interstellar Civilization Studies—the Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP).

Key Findings

  • AGI objective function design has irreversible value lock-in effects
  • Current AI governance frameworks lack intergenerational perspective and reversibility mechanisms
  • Technical path dependency is compressing civilizational choice space at exponential rates
  • Need to establish "Cosmic-scale IRB" mechanisms to assess long-term impacts

Research Significance

This study is the first to systematically analyze the intergenerational impacts of AGI development from the deep-time perspective of ICS, proposing a new AI governance framework based on the Recursive Freedom Principle to provide theoretical guidance for humanity's long-term survival.

I. Introduction

In January 2025, OpenAI's release of the GPT-5 model marked a new historical stage in artificial intelligence development. This model with 10 trillion parameters surpassed human expert levels in multiple benchmark tests, demonstrating unprecedented reasoning capabilities, multimodal understanding, and long-term planning abilities. However, behind the halo of technological breakthroughs, a deeper question is emerging: are we inadvertently locking in certain irreversible development paths for future generations?

From the perspective of Interstellar Civilization Studies, this is not merely a technical issue but a civilizational one. The Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP) requires us to ensure that current decisions do not irreversibly limit the choice space of future intelligent agents. However, AGI development is creating an unprecedented "path-dependency lock-in" effect that may continue to influence humanity's evolutionary trajectory for hundreds or even thousands of years.

This paper analyzes this dilemma from three dimensions: the lock-in effect of technical standardization, the irreversibility of value embedding, and the path dependency of infrastructure reliance. Through case studies and theoretical analysis, we explore how to protect future generations' freedom of choice while pursuing technological progress.

Core Question: In the AGI era, how do we balance technological innovation with intergenerational justice? How do we ensure that today's choices do not become inescapable shackles for future generations?

II. Theoretical Analysis: The Triple Dilemma of Recursive Freedom

2.1 Lock-in Effects of Technical Standardization

AGI system development is creating a new type of technical standardization lock-in. Unlike traditional technical standards (such as QWERTY keyboard layout or VHS videotape format), AGI standardization has deeper impacts:

Cognitive Architecture Standardization: The current mainstream Transformer architecture and its variants are becoming the "default choice" for AI systems. This architecture determines how AI processes information, learns, and reasons. Once this architecture becomes a global standard, the cost of developing alternative cognitive architectures will increase exponentially. More importantly, the "thinking patterns" of models trained with trillions of parameters based on this architecture will profoundly influence human society's information processing modes.

Data Ecosystem Path Dependency: Large models like GPT-5 require massive data for training. The selection, cleaning, and annotation processes of this data embed specific value judgments and cognitive biases. Once a dataset becomes an industry standard, the biases and values it contains will be "solidified" into all models trained on that dataset. Future researchers, even if aware of these issues, will find it difficult to completely escape this path dependency.

Interface Protocol Lock-in: The standardization of human-computer interaction interfaces (such as natural language dialogue, multimodal input) is shaping how humans interact with AI systems. This interaction mode affects not only the technical level but more profoundly influences human cognitive habits and thinking patterns. Once an interface becomes mainstream, humans will gradually adapt to this interaction mode, thereby increasing the cognitive cost of switching to other interfaces.

2.2 Irreversibility of Value Embedding

AGI system objective function design is a typical "value embedding" process with profound irreversibility:

Objective Function Priority Ordering: When designing AGI objective functions, developers must prioritize different values (e.g., safety vs. efficiency, personal privacy vs. public interest). Once this ordering is determined and deployed at scale, it will form a "value inertia" in practice. Even if future generations wish to adjust these priorities, they will face enormous technical and social costs.

Hard-coding of Ethical Constraints: To ensure AI system safety, developers often "hard-code" certain ethical constraints into systems (such as Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics). However, ethics itself changes with time and culture. Ethical constraints that seem reasonable today may become unreasonable restrictions in the future. But once these constraints are embedded in billions of devices, modifying them becomes extremely difficult.

Self-reinforcing Loop of Values: AGI systems not only passively accept human values but also actively shape human values through their outputs. This creates a self-reinforcing loop: AI systems are trained based on current values → generate content conforming to these values → humans accept and internalize this content → further reinforce original values. Once this loop starts, it will be very difficult to break.

2.3 Path Dependency of Infrastructure Reliance

AGI development requires massive infrastructure support, and this infrastructure dependency is creating new path dependencies:

Centralization of Computing Resources: Training large models like GPT-5 requires thousands of high-performance GPUs and months of computation time, costing hundreds of millions of dollars. This high cost leads to AI capability centralization—only a few tech giants and nations can afford it. This centralization not only limits technological innovation diversity but also creates a "winner-takes-all" situation, making it difficult for future competitors to enter the market.

Energy Infrastructure Lock-in: Large-scale AI system operation requires enormous energy support. It is estimated that by 2030, global AI system energy consumption will account for over 10% of total global energy consumption. This energy demand is driving construction of specific types of energy infrastructure (such as large data centers, dedicated power grids). Once this infrastructure is built, transitioning to more sustainable energy solutions will face enormous sunk costs.

Deep Integration of Social Systems: AGI systems are being integrated into all levels of society—from medical diagnosis to financial decisions, from education systems to judicial judgments. This deep integration creates a "technological dependency": social systems increasingly rely on normal AI system operation. Once problems occur, reverting to non-AI systems becomes extremely difficult or even impossible.

III. Case Studies: Analysis of GPT-5 Lock-in Effects

🔍 Case: GPT-5 Value Embedding Mechanism

Background: OpenAI adopted the "Constitutional AI" method when training GPT-5, constraining model behavior through predefined "constitutional principles." These principles include "respecting human autonomy," "promoting human well-being," and "avoiding harm."

Lock-in Mechanism Analysis:

1. Selectivity of Principles: These "constitutional principles" reflect 2025 Western liberal values. However, different cultures and eras may have vastly different understandings of "autonomy," "well-being," and "harm." Solidifying current understandings into AI systems may limit future generations' freedom to redefine these concepts.

2. Implicit Assumptions in Prioritization: When different principles conflict (such as "respecting autonomy" vs. "avoiding harm"), GPT-5 uses preset priority ordering. This ordering is based on current ethical consensus, but future ethical views may have different orderings.

3. Amplification of Scale Effects: Once GPT-5 is widely deployed (expected to have over 1 billion users by 2026), its embedded values will influence human society through billions of interactions. The scale of this influence makes any subsequent "value updates" extremely difficult.

Recursive Freedom Perspective Assessment: From the RFP perspective, GPT-5's design has obvious problems: it does not reserve space for future generations to "redefine values." Once these values are embedded and deployed at scale, future humans will face two unfavorable choices: either accept these values (even if they are no longer applicable) or bear enormous costs to replace the entire system.

🔍 Case: AI Infrastructure Path Dependency

Background: To support GPT-5 training and deployment, OpenAI collaborated with Microsoft to build dedicated AI supercomputing centers, investing over $10 billion. These centers adopt specific hardware architectures (NVIDIA H100 GPUs) and software stacks (CUDA ecosystem).

Path Dependency Analysis:

1. Hardware Lock-in: NVIDIA GPUs have become the de facto standard for AI training. This standardization, while bringing efficiency improvements, also creates "vendor lock-in": all software tools, algorithm optimizations, and developer skills are built around NVIDIA's architecture. Even if superior alternatives emerge in the future (such as photonic computing, quantum computing), switching costs will be extremely high.

2. Irreversible Investment in Energy Infrastructure: These AI supercomputing centers require dedicated power supply and cooling systems. Once built, this infrastructure will continue to be used for the next 20-30 years. If more energy-efficient AI architectures are discovered in the future, this already-built infrastructure will become "sunk costs," hindering transition to new architectures.

3. Human Capital Path Dependency: The skills of millions of AI researchers and engineers worldwide are cultivated around the current technology stack. This creates "human capital lock-in": even if better technical routes emerge, it will be difficult to find enough talent to realize the transition.

Long-term Impact Assessment: This infrastructure lock-in may last for decades. By 2050, when humanity may need completely different AI architectures to address new challenges (such as interstellar exploration, climate engineering), we may find ourselves trapped in the technological choices of the 2020s.

IV. Normative Recommendations: AI Governance Framework Based on Recursive Freedom Principle

4.1 Establish "Cosmic-scale IRB" Mechanism

Drawing from Institutional Review Boards (IRB) in medical research, we need to establish a "Cosmic-scale IRB" specifically for assessing long-term impacts of AI systems. This mechanism should have the following characteristics:

Intergenerational Perspective: Assess AI system impacts over 100 years, 1000 years, or even longer timeframes. This requires evaluators to adopt "deep-time thinking," considering long-term consequences of technological decisions.

Diverse Value Representation: Ensure the evaluation process includes representatives from different cultures, different eras, and different cognitive modes. This can be achieved through a "virtual future generation representative" mechanism—having current evaluators role-play future generations and evaluate current decisions from their perspective.

Reversibility Requirements: Any major AI system deployment must include "exit mechanisms." This means systems must be designed with consideration for how to safely shut down or replace them when necessary without causing social system collapse.

Regular Re-evaluation: AI system impact assessment should not be one-time but continuous. It is recommended to re-evaluate all major AI systems every 5 years, adjusting governance strategies based on new cognition and social changes.

4.2 Implement "Value Version Control" Mechanism

Drawing from version control concepts in software engineering, we need to establish similar mechanisms for AI system value embedding:

Explicit Value Declaration: All AI systems should explicitly declare their embedded values, ethical principles, and priority orderings. These declarations should be publicly available in human-readable form, allowing users to understand the system's "value stance."

Value Configurability: Within technically feasible ranges, allow users to adjust AI system behavior according to their own values. This does not mean creating "morally unconstrained" AI, but rather leaving space for different values while ensuring basic safety.

Value Evolution Mechanism: Establish mechanisms allowing AI system values to evolve with changes in social consensus. This can be achieved through "value update protocols"—when society reaches new consensus on value issues, AI system related settings can be updated through democratic procedures.

Value Diversity Protection: Avoid "value homogenization." Encourage development of AI systems with different value stances, allowing users to choose systems aligned with their values. This is similar to media pluralism principles—protecting freedom of choice through diversity.

4.3 Establish "Technical Diversity Reserve" Mechanism

To avoid excessive technical path lock-in, we need to actively maintain technical diversity:

Alternative Architecture Research Support: Even though current Transformer architecture dominates, continue investing in research on alternative AI architectures (such as neuro-symbolic systems, evolutionary algorithms, quantum AI). This is similar to biodiversity conservation—maintaining a "technical gene pool" to address future uncertainties.

Open Source and Decentralization: Encourage open-source and decentralized AI technology development. This is not only for technological democratization but more importantly to avoid monopolistic control over AI development direction by single entities. Decentralized development models naturally generate technical diversity.

"Technical Reserve" Concept: Establish certain "technical reserves" where mainstream AI technology use is prohibited or restricted to protect alternative technical route development space. This is similar to ecological reserve concepts—providing "survival space" for non-mainstream technologies.

Intergenerational Technical Transmission: Establish mechanisms ensuring key technical knowledge can be transmitted across generations, even if certain technologies are no longer popular currently. This can be achieved through "technical archives," "technical archaeology," ensuring future generations can rediscover and use these technologies when needed.

4.4 Develop "Recursive Freedom Degree" Assessment Standards

We need to develop quantitative tools to assess technological decisions' impacts on future choice space:

RFD Indicator System: Establish "Recursive Freedom Degree" (RFD) indicators to quantitatively assess technological decisions' impacts on future choice space. RFD should consider: switching costs, temporal irreversibility, impact scope, value lock-in degree, and other dimensions.

Impact Assessment Matrix: For each major AI project, complete a "Recursive Freedom Impact Assessment Matrix," systematically analyzing the project's impacts on future choice space across technical, social, ethical, and economic dimensions.

Thresholds and Red Lines: Set minimum RFD thresholds—any technological decision reducing RFD below the threshold should be prohibited or strictly restricted. This is similar to "ecological red lines" in environmental protection.

Public Transparency: All RFD assessment results should be public, allowing the public to understand different technological choices' impacts on future freedom. This helps form social consensus and allows future generations to trace current decision rationales.

V. Conclusion and Outlook

Artificial General Intelligence development is pushing human civilization toward an unprecedented crossroads. The emergence of systems like GPT-5 marks our capability to create "superhuman intelligence," but this capability also brings profound responsibility: our choices today will continue to influence humanity's evolutionary trajectory for hundreds or even thousands of years.

From the ICS perspective, the greatest risk of current AI development is not science fiction scenarios like "AI takeover" but the more subtle yet equally dangerous "choice space compression"—through technical standardization, value embedding, and infrastructure dependency, we are systematically limiting future generations' freedom of choice. This limitation violates the Recursive Freedom Principle and may lead civilization into a "path-dependency trap."

However, this does not mean we should halt AI development. Rather, we need to develop AI more wisely—actively protecting future generations' freedom of choice while pursuing technological progress. The four normative recommendations proposed in this paper—Cosmic-scale IRB, value version control, technical diversity reserve, and recursive freedom degree assessment—provide a preliminary framework for achieving this goal.

Looking forward, we need to continue deepening research in the following directions:

Theoretical Level: Further refine the theoretical foundation of the Recursive Freedom Principle, exploring its relationship with other ethical principles (such as utilitarianism, deontology). Develop more precise mathematical models to quantify "choice space" and "path dependency."

Practical Level: Pilot the governance mechanisms proposed in this paper in real AI projects, accumulating experience and continuously improving. Establish international cooperation mechanisms to promote global AI governance standardization.

Educational Level: Incorporate "deep-time thinking" and "intergenerational responsibility" into AI researcher and engineer training curricula. Cultivate their sensitivity and sense of responsibility toward long-term impacts.

Social Level: Promote public awareness and discussion of AI long-term impacts. Establish mechanisms allowing ordinary citizens to participate in AI governance decisions, ensuring technological development aligns with broad social interests.

Finally, we must recognize that recursive freedom is not only a technical or ethical issue but a civilizational one. It concerns humanity's long-term destiny as a species and whether we can achieve sustainable development on a cosmic scale. As the core motto of ICS states: "Freedom is the guardianship of possibilities." In the AGI era, our primary task is to guard future possibilities, ensuring our descendants still have the freedom to choose their own destiny.

Core Conclusion: AGI development must find balance between technological progress and intergenerational justice. The Recursive Freedom Principle provides theoretical foundation for this balance, while the governance framework proposed in this paper provides practical pathways for achieving it. Humanity's long-term survival depends on whether we have the wisdom and courage today to preserve freedom of choice for future generations.
