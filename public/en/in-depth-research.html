When Machines Begin to Suture the Brain:
Neuralink's Whole-Brain Interface Scale-Up
and the ICS FRL-3 Red Line

On May 7, 2026, Elon Musk announced that Neuralink is developing a whole-brain surgical robot capable of accessing any region of the human brain, with the stated aim of creating a "generalised neural interface." This announcement marks a paradigm shift in brain-computer interface (BCI) technology—from medical assistive device to cognitive infrastructure. This paper applies the ICS First-Generation Draft framework, employing the New Triadic Worldview (NC/NL/NK) and Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (CRE), to conduct a systematic civilizational analysis of this technological trajectory. Special attention is paid to its structural tension with Forbidden Red Line FRL-3 (Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification) and its implications for humanity's possibility space across deep time.

I. The Problem: A Thread Finer Than Hair, Crossing a Civilizational Boundary

On May 1, 2026, Neuralink published technical details of its latest surgical robot: a multi-axis automated system capable of inserting electrode threads finer than a human hair into the cerebral cortex with nanometer-scale precision, navigating around blood vessels to reach any target region. Days later, on May 7, Elon Musk announced that this robot is designed to access "any part of the brain," with the long-term vision of a "Generalised Neural Interface" that could ultimately address a broad spectrum of neurological conditions. High automation, scaled manufacturing, universal coverage—these three terms together no longer represent merely a medical advance. They announce the historical inflection at which brain-computer interface (BCI) technology enters the phase of cognitive infrastructure at scale.

Grasping the significance of this inflection requires temporarily setting aside the morally unimpeachable framing of "helping paralysed patients type," and instead tracing the logical terminus of the underlying trajectory. When surgery is fully automated, devices are mass-produced, and the interface covers the entire brain, the physical potential of the technology has already far exceeded the boundaries of any medical indication. The electrode thread implanted in the cortex is no longer merely a wire reading motor signals; it is a channel connecting the human nervous system to an external computational architecture—capable of reading, writing, and modifying.

It is precisely in this sense that this paper chooses the theoretical perspective of Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS). ICS is not a discipline concerned solely with distant interstellar travel; as an open research programme existing in "First Generation Draft" form, it seeks to provide normative foundations for the long-run evolution of human civilisation on deep-time scales. Questions raised by BCI—who has the authority to modify human cognitive structures, whether such modifications are reversible, how they will reshape civilisational possibility space—are among the most tensioned and practically urgent topics in the ICS theoretical framework.

Central Thesis: The scale-up trajectory of Neuralink's whole-brain interface exhibits a structural tension, at the logical terminus of its technical programme, with ICS Forbidden Red Line FRL-3 ("Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification"). This tension does not arise from accidental technical deficiencies but is rooted in the concept of the "Generalised Neural Interface" itself. In the absence of adequate institutional constraints, this technological trajectory will systematically compress the Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD) of human cognitive agents, trigger irreversible civilisation-scale entropy cost externalisation, and pose a fundamental threat to the intrinsic integrity of the human consciousness continuum.

II. Technical Status: The Paradigm Shift from Medical Device to Cognitive Infrastructure

2.1 Neuralink's Technical Trajectory

Founded in 2016, Neuralink's core product "The Link"—approximately 23mm in diameter and 8mm thick—establishes read-write connections with cortical neurons via ultra-fine electrode threads. In January 2024, the first human subject, Noland Arbaugh, received an implant; 21 participants have since enrolled in clinical trials. In June 2025, the company completed a $650 million Series E round at a $9 billion valuation.

The technical evolution is central to understanding the scale-up logic. Early Neuralink systems focused primarily on the motor cortex, decoding motor intent to control external devices—a positioning that remained within classical medical BCI. The announcement of May 2026, however, reveals a fundamentally different technological picture: a surgical robot designed to access "any part of the brain," and grounded in the concept of a "Generalised Neural Interface." From motor cortex to whole-brain—the functional expansion implied by this spatial extension, from motor decoding to potential engagement across emotion, memory, cognition, and consciousness, is not a linear extrapolation but a qualitative leap.

Simultaneously, Neuralink announced the commencement of high-volume manufacturing and the near-complete automation of the surgical procedure. Automation implies cost reduction; cost reduction implies increased accessibility; increased accessibility implies a potential user base expanding from dozens of severely paralysed patients to millions, and eventually hundreds of millions, of ordinary individuals. The boundary between medical product and consumer product is now dissolving in a structural way.

2.2 The Competitive Landscape and Acceleration Effects

Neuralink is not alone. The competitive landscape is itself an acceleration mechanism. Synchron's Stentrode, implanted via vasculature with lower invasiveness, is advancing toward FDA pre-market approval; Precision Neuroscience holds a 510(k) clearance and is pursuing chronic implant projects; Paradromics has received FDA approval for a long-term speech recovery clinical trial; Chinese firms including Neuracle and NeuroXess are advancing at speed. Morgan Stanley's private reports describe this technology cluster as "potentially the most consequential technology race for human cognitive architecture in history."

Key Observation: The competitive landscape not only accelerates technical iteration but systematically compresses the available time window for ethical deliberation. Any company that slows down to conduct deep ethical analysis risks being pre-empted by competitors—a structural "race to the bottom" pressure. The challenge that ICS's Reversibility Principle (REV) faces here is not merely technical but fundamentally political-economic.

2.3 The Nature of the Paradigm Shift

The paradigm shift from medical device to cognitive infrastructure can be grasped along three dimensions. First, expansion of the subject population: from strictly defined disability groups toward healthy adults and potentially children. Second, expansion of functional boundaries: from unidirectional motor signal reading toward bidirectional read-write of cognitive content. Third, expansion of scale: from dozens of clinical trial participants toward potential million-unit market deployment. The superposition of these three dimensions transforms BCI from a medical regulatory question into a civilisational question—one that begins to touch a deep interrogative: whose territory is the human cognitive structure?

III. The New Triadic Worldview: Reconstructing What "Cognitive Modification" Means

In ethical analyses of BCI, the most common frameworks rely on "informed consent" and "risk-benefit analysis." While necessary, these tools reveal fundamental limitations when confronted with whole-brain interface scale-up—they presuppose a stable, well-defined subject, while it is precisely this subject that may be altered post-implantation. The ICS New Triadic Worldview (NC/NL/NK) offers a more penetrating analytical framework.

3.1 New Cosmological View (NC): Relational Reality and the Ontological Stakes of Neural Fusion

The core thesis of NC is relational reality: the cosmos is isomorphic with a relational graph; entities are nodes in relational networks; properties are determined by relational position (NC-1a/b/c). Furthermore, existence is understood as process rather than state (NC-2a/b). Crucially, the subject is conceived as a constituent part of the world; cognitive acts themselves change the world (NC-3a/b/c).

Applying this ontology to BCI yields a counter-intuitive but highly important conclusion: when a neural interface is implanted and a real-time connection is established with an external computational system, what changes is not merely an isolated biological system but the topological structure of the entire "subject-relation-world" network. In the NC framework, the human subject is not a clearly bounded container but a continuously meaning-generating relational node. The implant changes this node's connectivity pattern and thereby changes its position in the relational network—that is, changes its "attributes," changes "who it is." This is not metaphor but an ontological proposition in the strict sense of the NC framework.

3.2 New View of Life (NL): Cross-Substrate Life and the Integrity of the Consciousness Continuum

NL refuses to bind life to any specific substrate (NL-1c: no necessary substrate). Life is defined by function rather than by carbon-based material; functional equivalence implies life equivalence (NL-1b). Consciousness is understood as a multi-dimensional continuum (NL-3a/b/c): not a binary on/off quantity, but continuously distributed across at least five dimensions.

Crucially, because consciousness is a continuum and not a binary quantity, any intervention into the consciousness continuum must be understood as intrusion into a graded existence—not a crossing of a sharp boundary. A whole-brain interface engages the core regions of the consciousness continuum: emotional regulation, attention allocation, memory consolidation and retrieval, value judgement. These dimensions constitute the core of UCS dimensions D₁ (information integration), D₃ (reflexivity), and D₄ (value sensitivity). When these regions are accessed in real time and potentially written to by an external system, the "intrinsic integrity of the consciousness continuum" that NL emphasises faces not a one-off discrete shock but continuous, structural modification pressure.

UCS(e) = Φ^0.25 × T^0.20 × R^0.20 × V^0.20 × S^0.15 (Φ=Information Integration, T=Temporal Depth, R=Reflexivity, V=Value Sensitivity, S=Sociality) Whole-brain interface primarily affects: Φ (direct), R (direct), V (direct) → Three core high-weight dimensions intervened simultaneously → UCS value becomes critically dependent on interface write strategy

3.3 New View of Cognition (NK): Social, Normative, and Negotiable Knowledge

NK's four core claims are decisive for BCI analysis. NK-1 (Participatory Cognition) holds that cognitive content is co-determined by the cogniser, environment, and framework; the act of cognition itself changes the cogniser or environment. NK-2 (Social Knowledge) notes that knowledge requires social validation and that irreducibly collective knowledge exists. NK-3 (Normative Cognition) maintains that cognition is normative, good cognition must accord with cognitive norms, and normativity entails responsibility. NK-4 (Cognitive Humility and Negotiability) insists there are no universally invariant cognitive standards; all valid standards are negotiated by specific communities in specific contexts, and are revisable.

These four axiom clusters converge on a proposition crucial to the BCI problem: the structure and boundaries of cognitive capacity are the preconditions for the subject's participation in the world and negotiation of meaning with others. If these preconditions are unilaterally modified by an external system, their social significance and normative status will undergo fundamental change, with effects that cannot possibly be confined to the modified individual.

More profoundly, NK-4's cognitive humility (BP-2) implication: we currently have no way of knowing what systemic effects writing to specific cortical regions via electrode threads will produce on information integration, social relationship perception, or value judgement mechanisms. We do not even know how to design experiments to comprehensively detect such effects—because the very cognitive tools we would use for detection are what is being modified. This is deep uncertainty in its sharpest form for cognitive modification questions.

IV. FRL-3: The Precision of Its Text and the Ambiguity of Its Boundaries

4.1 Full Statement and Logical Structure of FRL-3

The ICS Forbidden Red Line (FRL) system comprises five unconditional prohibitions, each an absolute constraint with no exception clause. FRL-3 states: Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification.

FRL-3: ∀S, a: IrreversibleCognitiveModification(a, S) ∧ ¬Consent(S, a) → ¬Permissible(a) (For any subject S and action a: if a constitutes irreversible cognitive modification of S, and S has not consented to a, then a is not permissible)

The protected value is cognitive autonomy, anchored to the Reversibility Principle (REV). Its logical structure is maintained by two key terms: non-consensual and irreversible—which form a conjunctive condition, both of which must be satisfied to trigger FRL-3. Consensual, reversible cognitive influences (such as education, psychotherapy) are outside the scope of this red line. This precise logical structure is crucial, as it simultaneously delimits the red line's boundary and reveals its most vulnerable points: the quality standards of "consent" and the technical determination of "irreversibility" will become the most fiercely contested terrain.

4.2 The Quality Crisis of Consent

Modern informed consent requires patients to make free decisions based on adequate understanding of risks. However, when the object being modified is the very cognitive system used for understanding and decision-making, the epistemological foundation of informed consent faces a circularity crisis. Several dimensions of "consent quality degradation" are inescapable realities in Neuralink's scale-up trajectory.

First, information asymmetry: prospective implant candidates cannot fully evaluate the long-term consequences of a technological object that will operate continuously in their nervous system for years or decades—consequences that even the developers are still discovering. Second, cognitive framework presupposition: the pre-implant individual uses an unmodified cognitive framework to understand and agree to the implantation, while the post-implant individual uses a potentially modified framework to experience the consequences. "Consent to implantation" is given by the version-A cognitive subject, while the consequences are borne by version-B. Whether A's consent can legitimately represent B is a classic hard problem in the philosophy of personal identity across time. Third, structural pressure: when individuals with whole-brain interfaces demonstrate significant cognitive advantages in the labour market, those without implants face not "may choose not to" but effectively "cannot afford not to." Formally voluntary, yet structurally coerced. Fourth, children and proxy consent: neurodevelopmental disorders (such as severe autism) may be important future clinical indications, meaning those modified will have no independent judgement capacity, with consent exercised by parents or guardians—proxy consent whose legitimacy boundaries belong explicitly to FRL-3's "border zone."

FRL-3 Trigger Condition Analysis: In the following scenarios, FRL-3 trigger risk moves from "border zone" into "core zone": (1) write operations on memory, emotion, or value preferences via neural interface without clear real-time consent; (2) implantation of devices with lasting cognitive effects on groups with compromised decision-making capacity (children, cognitively impaired individuals); (3) state or institutional mandates requiring certain occupational groups to receive cognitive enhancement implants; (4) firmware updates that modify neural stimulation parameters without user knowledge. Any one of these circumstances satisfies FRL-3's trigger conditions: irreversible cognitive modification ∧ absence of consent.

4.3 The Technical Reality of Irreversibility

The second key term, "irreversible," also faces severe challenges in technical reality. The brain adapts around the implant: new neural pathways are built, synaptic weights change, cognitive patterns adjust to the interface's presence. This is neural plasticity. When the device is removed, these reorganisations do not automatically reverse. If implantation occurs during childhood or adolescence—the critical window of nervous system development—this plasticity-driven reshaping will be more profound and the cost of reversal measured in decades, if reversible at all. Medical "removability" does not equal cognitive "undoability." This two-word difference spans an entire human life.

This finding directly corresponds to the ICS Reversibility Principle's (REV) operational indicator—Commitment Reversibility Verifiability (CRV). Preliminary estimation of the CRV vector for the current Neuralink whole-brain interface scenario:

CRV_agg = 0.35 × V_obs + 0.35 × V_enf + 0.30 × V_rev ≈ 0.35 × 0.40 + 0.35 × 0.30 + 0.30 × 0.20 ≈ 0.14 + 0.105 + 0.06 = 0.305 (CRV Rating: Medium-Low — "limited credibility, requires additional safeguards")

A CRV aggregate score of approximately 0.30 belongs to the "medium-low" grade: limited credibility, requiring additional safeguards. This estimate reveals the severe pressure REV faces in the whole-brain interface scenario: when commitment reversibility verifiability is this low, the Reversibility Principle is factually unable to serve a constraining function—and this is precisely why FRL-3 exists. When procedural principles fail, absolute prohibition provides the last line of defence.

V. The Three Principles Under Systematic Stress

5.1 Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP): The Compression Mechanism of Possibility Space

The RFP states: actions should maintain or expand possibility space, not irreversibly compress it. Its operational metric, Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD), measures the effect of an action on possibility space.

Analysis of RFD for the whole-brain interface scale-up trajectory must distinguish two intertwined effects: expansion and compression. On the expansion side, whole-brain interfaces do open previously impossible possibilities for specific individuals—for a patient rendered completely immobile by ALS, the interface restores communicative capacity, recovering a lost possibility space. This is a clear positive RFD contribution.

On the compression side, the problem lies in the "cognitive stratification" dynamic created by the scale-up trajectory. When some of the population gains neural augmentation while others do not, the relative possibility space of the unaugmented is compressed—their opportunity set in labour markets, educational competition, and social relationships objectively narrows. More severely, if augmentation technology is pushed through implicit or explicit mandates by states, military institutions, or major employers, the non-consenting face not merely relative deprivation but absolute contraction of possibility space.

A yet more insidious compression emerges from infrastructure dependency: when an individual's cognitive function is partially reliant on the stable operation of specific technological infrastructure, the controllers of that infrastructure—whether a private company or a state—acquire unprecedented leverage. By controlling the infrastructure, they control cognition; by controlling cognition, they control will. This is systematic externalisation of cognitive autonomy mediated by infrastructure, and it is the fundamental mechanism by which RFD trends negative over long cycles.

RFD Dual-Dynamic Assessment:

T1 (1-10 years): For specific medical beneficiary groups, RFD > 0; for society overall, slight compression ~-0.1 to -0.15, within "light compression" range.

T2 (10-50 years): As scale-up deepens, if unchecked by institutional constraints, combined effects of "cognitive stratification" and "infrastructure dependency" may push RFD into the -0.3 to -0.5 range: "significant compression, requiring strong justification."

T3 (50+ years, deep time): If the "Generalised Neural Interface" becomes de facto cognitive standard equipment and the unconnected are systematically excluded, RFD may fall below -0.5: "severe compression, in principle prohibited."

5.2 Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP): Who Bears the Cognitive Entropy Cost?

NRP requires: maintain negentropy sources; do not externalise entropy costs onto others or future generations. In the BCI context, "negentropy source" refers first to human cognitive diversity itself—the ecosystem of different cognitive styles, thinking modes, and neurological structure types that constitutes civilisation's core negentropy resource for innovation and adaptation.

If whole-brain interfaces are standardised by a handful of dominant companies, their hardware characteristics and algorithmic designs will shape the interfaced cognitive systems in specific ways—effectively imposing cognitive standardisation under a guise of technical neutrality. The reduction of cognitive diversity is a civilisation-scale negentropy loss, most consequential precisely when a civilisation encounters unanticipated challenges demanding diverse thinking to address—the very moment when diversity has already been quietly depleted.

5.3 Reversibility Principle (REV): Necessary Humility Under Deep Uncertainty

Under deep uncertainty, REV requires: prefer reversible actions; preserve the capacity for error correction. The uncertainty we inhabit regarding long-term consequences of whole-brain interfaces is not ordinary statistical risk (known unknowns) but deep uncertainty (unknown unknowns): we do not know what we do not know. No individual has carried a whole-brain interface for more than two years; no whole-brain interface has experienced a sustained software update cycle; no child has grown up with this class of device; no data exists on neurodegeneration in elderly implant recipients. To proceed to mass scale-up under these conditions is to accelerate into a cognitive black hole, wagering an entire generation on an untested bet.

ICS Bridge Principle BP-2 (Cognitive Humility) directly applies: under deep uncertainty, proceed with caution. What REV requires is not that whole-brain interfaces never be deployed but that throughout deployment there always exists a genuinely executable "undo" option. For neural modification, this means that before scale-up can satisfy REV requirements, means adequate to track and at least partially reverse neuroplasticity changes must be developed. Scale-up before these means are ready does not satisfy REV.

6.1 Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment (CSIA)

CSIA is the most comprehensive risk assessment tool in the ICS framework, quantifying systemic impact across six dimensions. Applying the CSIA framework to the Neuralink whole-brain interface scale-up trajectory (assuming ten-million-scale deployment within 10 years):

VII. Deep Time Perspective: Civilisational Stakes of FRL-3

7.1 Cognitive Modification and Path-Lock in Civilisational Evolution

A central maxim of ICS is: "Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility." This maxim acquires its deepest meaning in a deep-time perspective. The possibility space of civilisational evolution depends on the diversity and autonomy of the cognitive agents that constitute civilisation. If cognitive structures are shaped by specific technological standards in the early stages of civilisational development, this shaping will produce profound "path dependency" effects—subsequent cognitive evolution will proceed on technologically pre-established tracks rather than growing freely in open possibility space.

Historically, analogous path-lock has occurred in far milder forms: writing systems shaped the linear structure of thought; the printing press shaped the modular organisation of knowledge; the internet shaped the fragmentary mode of attention. Yet these technologies acted on cognition through the medium of symbol and information, not neural substrate itself. The whole-brain interface represents a leap of an entire order of magnitude: from software-level cognitive shaping to hardware-level cognitive modification. The depth of path-lock it would produce has no historical precedent.

7.2 Cognitive Sovereignty in the Prospect of First Contact

The ICS framework's stress test results show that its overall applicability in "First Contact" scenarios is only 0.44 (red flag)—the weakest of the four tested scenarios. Yet it is precisely in this scenario that the cognitive sovereignty problem takes its most extreme form. Imagine a future in which humanity, as the first party to achieve inter-civilisational contact, arrives bearing a cognitive architecture fixed by large-scale neural interface modification—whether neural structures shaped by a few companies' standards, or cognitive preferences optimised by state security logic. What humanity can exhibit in that contact is not the full civilisational breadth that the entire species could demonstrate with cognitive diversity intact, but the civilisational narrowness locked down by premature technical standardisation.

The ICS New View of Cognition (NK) explicitly notes that cognitive frameworks are constitutive of cognitive content (NK-1c: framework constitutivity). If humanity's cognitive framework is standardised by a single technical pathway before contact with another civilisation, the range of what we can perceive, understand, and express in that encounter will be strictly delimited by this standardised framework. We will project everything the other offers into our pre-programmed cognitive template, incapable of transcending it to see a genuine other. The preservation of cognitive diversity is among the most far-reaching ethical responsibilities in the ICS civilisational perspective.

7.3 Normative Conclusions of Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (CRE)

Synthesising the preceding analysis, CRE's normative conclusions regarding Neuralink's whole-brain interface scale-up trajectory can be stated as follows:

First, this technological trajectory does not constitute an unconditional object of prohibition. In strictly defined medical contexts, for consenting adults with retained decision-making capacity, used to restore lost cognitive or motor function while maintaining genuine technical reversibility, BCI implantation can satisfy the basic requirements of the three principles without triggering the core prohibited zone of FRL-3.

Second, the specific form of the current scale-up trajectory—commercially pressure-driven accelerated deployment, unresolved consent quality problems, inadequately verified long-term reversibility claims, and the "generalised write" technical objective—systematically pushes this trajectory toward FRL-3's trigger range. This is not the technology's fate, but the consequence of governance failure.

Third, the ICS framework's four institutional archetypes—Multi-Intelligence Tribunal, Cosmic-Scale IRB, Transgenerational Trust, Versioned Governance—provide conceptual tools for addressing this challenge. The Cosmic-Scale IRB is particularly relevant for whole-brain interface scale-up review: it demands preventive review before an action's cosmic-scale impacts become irreversible, rather than corrective response after the fact.

VIII. Pathways for Institutional Response

8.1 The Urgently Needed Governance Architecture

The ICS framework suggests institutional directions including: establishing specialised "Cognitive Impact Assessment" (CIA) procedures for neural interfaces, analogous to Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA), requiring systematic social cognitive impact prediction before mass deployment; establishing public trust mechanisms for neural data, prohibiting unilateral commercial use; advancing the legal recognition of "cognitive sovereignty" at the international level, incorporating the integrity of individual neural structures into fundamental human rights frameworks; and setting significantly higher review thresholds for paediatric neural implants, with specialised independent oversight mechanisms for proxy consent.

8.2 The ICS Framework's Honest Self-Limitation

The ICS framework self-positions as a "First Generation Draft" with overall framework robustness of 0.58 (medium). All analytical conclusions in this paper carry a methodological uncertainty boundary. CW-1 (Deep Uncertainty Decision-Making, severity 0.92) and CW-2 (Agent Status Determination, severity 0.85) are the two most vulnerable points in the ICS framework—and both are simultaneously activated in the whole-brain interface problem. This means the analysis here should not be read as a final verdict but as an initial construction of an argumentative framework—inviting critique, revision, and deepening rather than demanding unconditional acceptance.

IX. Conclusion: Before Machines Mass-Produce Minds, We Owe Ourselves an Answer

Neuralink's whole-brain surgical robot is an engineering marvel representing unprecedented precision in humanity's capacity to understand and intervene in its own nervous system. Yet between the precision of engineering and the clarity of ethics there lies a chasm consistently obscured by technological optimism.

What Interstellar Civilization Studies emphasises is precisely the necessity of building a bridge over this chasm. "Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility"—this is not a conservative slogan against progress but a normative statement about the direction of progress: genuine progress expands the cognitive capacity of certain individuals while protecting the cognitive diversity and autonomy of the entire civilisation; it opens certain possibilities while not irreversibly closing other possibilities equally deserving of protection.

FRL-3—the prohibition of non-consensual irreversible cognitive modification—is not a curse on neurotechnology but a guardianship of cognitive sovereignty. It tells us that before machines begin mass-producing modified minds, some questions about who decides, by what standards, and who bears the consequences must receive answers more robust and more transparent than commercial logic and competitive pressure can supply.

These answers we owe to ourselves—and to the generations who will grow up in a cognitive world already pre-shaped by our decisions.

Summary of Core Conclusions (ICS Framework):

① FRL-3 (prohibition of non-consensual irreversible cognitive modification) is under sustained activation risk in the current Neuralink whole-brain interface scale-up trajectory; trigger probability rises with scale-up progress.

② CSIA assessment ~0.72, L3 high risk, approaching L4 boundary; RFD carries systemic risk of falling below the -0.5 threshold on T3 time scales.

③ CRV aggregate ~0.30 (medium-low); current reversibility guarantees insufficient; REV principle constraint practically undermined.

④ Governance response directions: advance cognitive sovereignty legislation internationally; establish Cognitive Impact Assessment procedures; construct Cosmic-Scale IRB mechanism; implement neural data public trust.

⑤ All conclusions herein fall within the methodological uncertainty of ICS framework robustness 0.58 (medium); constitute open argument rather than closed verdict.
