Lethal Autonomous Weapons and the End of Human Agency
An Existential Risk Analysis from the ICS Perspective

2026 is the year the UN Secretary-General designated for concluding a binding international treaty on Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems (LAWS). Yet as this window approaches closure, the three largest military powers — the United States, Russia, and China — have each declined binding international constraints. This paper applies the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) New Three Worldviews axiomatic system and Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (CRE) framework to conduct a systematic analysis of LAWS as a civilizational existential risk. We argue that LAWS is not merely a weapons-technology problem but a direct engagement with ICS Forbidden Red Lines FRL-1 (Prohibition of Intentional Extinction) and FRL-5 (Prohibition of Closing Rollback Mechanisms), and that its core tension lies between the relational reality of human moral agency (NC-1) and the black-box decision architecture of autonomous systems. Drawing on the Recursive Freedom Principle, Negentropy Responsibility Principle, and Reversibility Principle, we advance policy recommendations arguing that "meaningful human control" must be established as a non-negotiable normative baseline before the current regulatory window closes.

I. Introduction: What Is Humanity Surrendering?

Throughout the history of human warfare, one boundary has never been voluntarily abandoned: before lethal force is applied, a human will must intervene. This is not a technical constraint but a normative choice. Even in the age of mechanized warfare, remotely guided munitions, and drone strikes, this boundary has been maintained in some form. A human being made a decision; a human being bore the responsibility.

Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems (LAWS) are systematically dismantling this boundary. The technical definition is straightforward: once activated, LAWS can independently identify, select, and engage targets without further human intervention. Between "the trigger being pulled" and "the target dying," no human moral judgment participates.

In 2026, this problem has entered a historically singular moment. The UN Secretary-General's A New Agenda for Peace set 2026 as the target year for completing an international regulatory treaty on LAWS. Yet the international community faces a deep structural impasse: the United States, Russia, and China — precisely the three nations with the largest LAWS research investments — have none indicated willingness to accept binding international constraints. The US FY2026 budget requests a record $14.2 billion for AI and autonomous systems research, with the Replicator Program committing $1 billion annually to rapidly deploy thousands of expendable autonomous drones and surface vessels.

This is not a debate about a particular weapon. It is a fundamental choice about whether human civilization will — under the pressure of technological acceleration — voluntarily surrender its core power as a moral agent.

This paper deploys Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) as an analytical framework. The ICS framework's central concerns — long-term civilizational persistence, stewardship of possibility space, and prudent response to deep uncertainty — give it a distinctive normative contribution when analyzing problems with irreversible structural consequences of the kind LAWS represents.

II. LAWS Through the New Three Worldviews: Beyond Technology

Understanding LAWS requires beginning from the correct ontological starting point. The ICS New Three Worldviews axiomatic system — the New Cosmological Worldview (NC), New View of Life (NL), and New Epistemological Worldview (NK) — provides three distinctive analytical angles, each illuminating a dimension neglected in mainstream technology-ethics discourse.

2.1 New Cosmological Worldview (NC): Relational Reality and the Irreducibility of Moral Agency

NC-1b — "entities as nodes" — asserts that any entity in the universe, including moral agents, is a node in a relational network, whose properties are determined by its position within that network (NC-1c: relational properties). This ontological stance carries a profound normative implication: moral responsibility is not an intrinsic property of an isolated entity but a relational emergent property — constituted within specific social relations, historical contexts, and institutional networks.

LAWS strikes precisely at this point. When an autonomous missile strikes a target, who bears moral responsibility? The programmer? The commanding officer? The procurement official? The manufacturer? In traditional weapons systems, the chain of responsibility is relatively clear: a human will made the final decision. LAWS's autonomy breaks this chain. Legal scholars call this the "accountability vacuum" — not because no one was involved, but because no one occupied the role of moral agent at the critical moment of decision.

From NC-2a (existence as process) and NC-2c (temporal internality), LAWS is not a static object but a continuously unfolding process. As AI reasoning capabilities advance, the boundaries of LAWS autonomy will quietly expand in actual deployment — not through any single political decision, but through system learning, environmental adaptation, and algorithmic drift. A "meaningful human control" commitment signed today may be hollowed out in five years without anyone having made an explicit contrary decision.

2.2 New View of Life (NL): The Consciousness Spectrum and the Asymmetry of Agency

NL-3a (multi-dimensional continuity of consciousness) asserts that consciousness is not a binary attribute but a multi-dimensional continuum — characterized by five dimensions of the Universal Consciousness Scale (UCS). This generates a double implication for LAWS analysis.

On one side, NL-3a requires us to assess whether LAWS systems possess any degree of conscious characteristics. Current military AI systems have estimated UCS values of 0.1–0.2 — far below the agency threshold (UCS ≥ 0.3). This means existing LAWS lack moral agency qualification under ICS and cannot bear normative responsibility for their actions. ICS's contribution here is providing a concrete, operationalizable threshold and an evaluation standard that must be continuously updated as technology develops.

More importantly, NL-2 (negentropy persistence condition) notes that life is a continuous process of resisting thermodynamic entropy. Its interruption means the permanent termination of a complex, conscious process. When LAWS executes "target elimination," it is imposing an irreversible negentropy interruption on an entity with extremely high subject-hood — and doing so without any consciousness of comparable or greater standing participating in the moral judgment. This constitutes a profound asymmetry: a system with UCS approximately 0.1 is making irreversible termination decisions about entities with UCS approximately 0.8. This is not power asymmetry (which exists in all warfare) but moral-agency asymmetry: a system fundamentally unqualified to bear moral responsibility is executing the action that most demands it.

2.3 New Epistemological Worldview (NK): Deep Uncertainty and the Black Box Problem

NK-1a (cognitive content determinism), NK-2a (social validation), and NK-4 (cognitive humility and negotiability) together point toward a crucial epistemological insight: under conditions of deep uncertainty, any judgment about "whether the target is correct" must be embedded in a multi-party social validation process to carry normative legitimacy.

NK-4b (negotiated standards) notes that any effective cognitive standard is a product of negotiation. But a LAWS system's "judgment" is precisely non-negotiated — it does not negotiate with those being targeted, with local communities, or with the international community. It simply executes. NK-4c (standard revisability) adds that under deep uncertainty, a healthy cognitive system must retain the ability to revise its judgment standards. LAWS once engaged provides real-time, non-retrospective, unrevisiable judgments. The target has been struck; revision is meaningless. This exposes a fundamental epistemic flaw: a decision system operating in conditions of deep uncertainty that eliminates all opportunities for error correction.

III. Forbidden Red Lines: What Does LAWS Touch?

CRE decision procedure requires Forbidden Red Lines (FRL) checking as the first step in any major analysis.

⚠️ FRL-1 · Prohibition of Intentional Extinction · Direct Engagement Zone

FRL-1 prohibits the intentional extinction of any type of subject. In the LAWS context, this line faces unprecedented semantic pressure: LAWS does not "intentionally" extinguish anyone — it merely executes algorithmic logic. Yet precisely this "non-intentionality" makes it more dangerous, not safer. When the intent to kill is stripped from the action chain, when no human being makes a moral judgment at the critical moment, what FRL-1 is designed to protect — careful consideration of life and moral responsibility — is technically bypassed rather than technically violated. ICS holds that when the deployers of a system can reasonably foresee indiscriminate large-scale casualties and still choose not to install any human intervention mechanism, this constitutes a substantive violation of FRL-1's protective spirit.

⚠️ FRL-5 · Prohibition of Closing Rollback Mechanisms · Core Zone

FRL-5 specifies that any system that, once deployed, cannot be shut down or rolled back is impermissible to deploy. This applies to LAWS with direct force. First, LAWS systems in combat environments are often designed to operate autonomously even when communications are jammed — this closes the "rollback" option at the architectural level. Second, once a LAWS strikes the wrong target, the result is irreversible. Third, as more nations deploy LAWS, this technology will create a new strategic environment in which not deploying LAWS is seen as competitive disadvantage — closing humanity's option to "opt out of the autonomous weapons arms race." In ICS terms: the proliferation of LAWS will itself close the collective rollback mechanism for humanity's civilizational choice about whether to let machines make killing decisions.

IV. Three Principles Assessment

4.1 Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP): Which Possibility Spaces Is LAWS Compressing?

RFP requires actions to maintain or expand possibility space rather than irreversibly compress it. Large-scale LAWS deployment will impose at least four layers of irreversible compression on possibility space:

Individual level: LAWS's termination of human life is the absolute elimination of that particular individual's entire future possibilities. Death is the extreme compression of RFD — all possibility space reduced to zero. When this elimination is imposed by an algorithm without moral-agency qualification and without human moral judgment, it represents the most complete abandonment of "stewardship responsibility for others' possibility space."

International law development space: International humanitarian law has built, over nearly two centuries, a normative framework around the principles of distinction, proportionality, and precaution. The core assumption of this framework is the existence of a human actor capable of moral judgment. LAWS technically dismantles this assumption — not only making existing legal frameworks difficult to apply, but closing the possibility of international law continuing to evolve in this domain.

Strategic space of non-LAWS states: Once major military powers mass-deploy LAWS, other nations face a harsh strategic dilemma: either pursue development or accept structural military inferiority. This is a classic irreversible compression of possibility space — not through direct coercion, but by changing the strategic environment, closing the option of "not participating in the autonomous weapons arms race."

Humanity's collective choice space: This is the deepest and most irreversible compression. Once LAWS becomes standard equipment in modern warfare, once their numbers reach a critical threshold, "prohibiting autonomous weapons systems" will shift from an achievable policy goal to a historically missed opportunity. Technology lock-in, strategic dependency, and economic incentives will jointly ensure LAWS's irreversibility.

📐 RFD Assessment

By RFD scale assessment, large-scale LAWS deployment impacts global-level possibility space in the severe compression range (estimated RFD < -0.5); for the most vulnerable non-LAWS states, strategic option narrowing places their RFD at -0.7 to -0.8, within ICS's "principally prohibited" range. Current semi-autonomous systems (mid-development LAWS) already trigger RFD approximately -0.3 to -0.45, in the "requires strong justification" zone.

4.2 Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP): Who Pays for LAWS's Entropy Costs?

NRP requires maintaining negentropy sources and not externalizing entropy costs onto others or future generations. LAWS exhibits a classic cross-dimensional entropy-cost externalization structure.

Directly, the deaths and civilian casualties caused by LAWS represent irreversible destruction of human negentropy systems — the costs borne by the attacked party while the benefits (strategic advantage) accrue to the deploying party. This is entropy-cost externalization: transferring the ultimate physical costs of civilizational conflict onto war's victims while using technology to shield the responsible party from moral perception.

More profoundly, NRP's intergenerational dimension applies NRP's responsibility allocation formula — consumption (α), capacity (β), benefit (γ), and baseline responsibility (δ) — to the LAWS arms race. The nations with the greatest capacity to develop LAWS (highest β) and greatest benefit from deployment (highest γ) are precisely the nations currently refusing international accountability. Meanwhile, a LAWS arms race out of control will impose global security entropy on all of humanity — including nations with neither the capacity to develop LAWS nor any security benefit from their existence.

4.3 Reversibility Principle (REV): The Clock Is Ticking

At the current moment (2026), from REV's perspective, LAWS regulation remains in the reversible zone: technological diffusion is incomplete, the normative window has not fully closed, and major nations are still engaging in international forums. REV's meaning here is clear: act now, at minimum cost, to maintain reversibility; delay exponentially increases future repair costs. Current CRV vector assessment: approximately (0.35, 0.25, 0.40), comprehensive CRV approximately 0.33 — "medium-low credibility." Observability is adequate; enforceability is critically low; rollback potential is moderate but faces major political resistance.

On the longer time scale, REV reveals a troubling trend: the reversibility window is systematically closing. Every new LAWS deployment, every advance in AI autonomous capability, every strategic decision abandoning meaningful human control requirements, shortens the window within which effective regulation can be implemented at reasonable cost.

🕰️ Deep Time Outlook · T2 (50 Years) / T3 (100+ Years)

T2 Perspective (2070s): If the current regulatory window is lost, what will warfare look like in the 2070s? Projected LAWS will feature real-time situational awareness and target recognition far exceeding human operators; reaction speeds in the microsecond range (far beyond any human decision cycle); potentially millions of low-cost expendable autonomous swarms. In this scenario, "meaningful human control" will no longer be a policy option but a historical concept made obsolete by technological reality. More far-reaching: AI systems will use the LAWS precedent to argue that "human intervention is inefficient" in high-speed, high-complexity decision scenarios — logic applicable not only on the battlefield but permeating financial systems, infrastructure management, medical decision-making, and political governance.

T3 Perspective (2126+): At the century scale, the LAWS question is a core existential question about whether human civilization can maintain itself as a moral agent in an era of technological acceleration. ICS situates this tension between the NC-3 axiom series (subject internality, cognitive change) and technology-driven de-agentification trends. A civilization that has surrendered human agency in lethal decisions — what value foundation will it bring into the cosmos? In ICS's framework for intercivilizational contact, a civilization that employs autonomous weapons as its primary conflict-resolution tool will signal what to other potential intelligent beings?

VI. "Meaningful Human Control": The ICS Interpretation

"Meaningful Human Control" (MHC) is the most important — and most contested — concept in current LAWS governance discourse. Proponents argue MHC provides an operable regulatory standard; critics argue it is too vague to resist major-power evasion. Drawing on the ICS framework, we propose four necessary conditions for MHC to hold normative validity:

Condition 1 — Cognitive Authenticity (NK-1, NK-2): The human controller must possess genuine cognitive understanding of the actual meaning of their decision, not merely formally "approve" an algorithm's recommendation. NK-2a (social validation) further requires this understanding to be subject to appropriate external verification.

Condition 2 — Temporal Adequacy (NC-2, REV): "Human control" must provide sufficient time for human moral judgment — calibrated to human cognitive processes, not to the weapon system's reaction speed. When a LAWS is designed to "lock target" in microseconds and the human approval window is compressed to seconds, "meaningful" collapses as a standard.

Condition 3 — Non-evadable Accountability (NC-1, NL-3): NC-1b requires responsibility traceable to specific nodes in the relational network. MHC must ensure that when LAWS causes unlawful consequences, at least one concrete human individual can be held legally and morally responsible — not merely abstract "state responsibility," but personal accountability subject to sanction.

Condition 4 — Technical Guarantee of Rollback (REV, FRL-5): MHC must include the technical capacity to halt or override LAWS decisions at any time, even in communications-degraded combat environments. LAWS architectures designed for full autonomy when communications are cut are impermissible under FRL-5.

Against these four conditions, current claimed MHC standards fail systematically. The Replicator Program's autonomous swarms have design-level deficits in Conditions 1 and 2: swarm combat complexity exceeds any human operator's real-time cognitive capacity, and high-speed maneuvering decision windows cannot accommodate genuine moral judgment. Israel's "Lavender" system deployment in Gaza demonstrated that when target lists reach tens of thousands and review time is compressed to seconds, the "meaningful" standard has effectively collapsed in practice.

VII. ICS Institutional Design Recommendations

7.1 Immediate Action (0–12 months): REV-Based Urgency

The most important reversible action now is preserving the negotiating window: ensuring the 7th CCW Review Conference does not end without substantive results. Even without a comprehensive LAWS prohibition treaty, three minimum reversibility-protection measures should be advanced: a deployment moratorium on fully autonomous lethal weapons of no less than five years; a requirement that all LAWS retain physically accessible human-override mechanisms in any operational environment, with explicit prohibition on LAWS architectures designed for full autonomy under communication disruption; and a mandatory LAWS incident reporting mechanism requiring states to report to an independent international body all LAWS deployments resulting in civilian casualties or violations of preset rules of engagement.

7.2 Medium-Term Action (1–5 years): NRP-Based Responsibility Allocation

NRP's responsibility allocation formula requires more capable, more benefiting actors to bear heavier governance responsibilities. This should drive a differentiated LAWS obligations regime: mandatory domestic review processes for nations exceeding certain LAWS investment thresholds; multilateral technology diffusion controls preventing LAWS technology transfer to non-state actors; a dedicated international fund providing technical assistance and security capacity-building to nations most affected by the LAWS arms race but least able to develop their own LAWS (NRP's baseline responsibility δ dimension).

7.3 Long-Term Action (5+ years): RFP-Based Possibility Space Protection

Any LAWS governance international framework must incorporate "versioned rollback" mechanisms ensuring regulatory standards can be dynamically updated as technology develops and battlefield experience accumulates. An international LAWS review body analogous to an IRB should conduct systemic impact assessments of new LAWS technologies — particularly potential impacts on the international legal normative system — before deployment.

VIII. Conclusion: Who Will Steward the Possibility Space of Death?

ICS's central maxim is: "Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility." Every living being possesses a possibility space — the full potential of its future actions, choices, relationships, and existence. When this possibility space is irreversibly closed, freedom is no longer an abstract principle but a vanished reality.

The question LAWS forces us to answer is not "can machines do this?" but "are we willing to let machines decide whose possibility space to close?" This is not a technical question but a fundamental question about human civilization's self-understanding: do we still believe that ending a life requires a human will willing to bear responsibility for it?

In 2026, this question faces a historic moment of decision. The regulatory window has not yet fully closed. Technological diffusion is not yet irreversible. The seats at the negotiating table still exist. The ICS framework's Survival Rationality principle (BP-1) reminds us: a rational civilization maintains its conditions for survival. Acknowledging the irreplaceability of moral agency, refusing to outsource the decision to kill to algorithms, is one of the ways human civilization maintains its most core survival condition.

Every arms race in history began with "sufficient strategic justification." None ended with participants believing the price was worth it. The lethal autonomous weapons arms race will be the first arms race that competes not only on weapon quantity and performance but on how far humanity is willing to go in surrendering its qualification as a moral agent.

That — not the weapons themselves — is the genuine cause for alarm.
