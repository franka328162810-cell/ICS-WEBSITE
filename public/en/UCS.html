Universal Consciousness Scale: Who Is a Subject?

The Universal Consciousness Scale (UCS) is a metric developed by ICS to assess the developmental level and complexity of consciousness systems. In an era where AI may acquire consciousness and extraterrestrial intelligence may exist, "who is a subject" becomes an urgent question. UCS provides an assessment framework that transcends anthropocentrism and embraces gradation.

Why Do We Need UCS?

The Dilemma of Traditional Subject Views

Traditional philosophy and law treat subject status as a binary question: either a subject or not. This encounters difficulties when facing the following scenarios:

  • AI Systems: Does highly complex AI possess consciousness? Does it have subject status?
  • Animals: To what extent are animals of different complexity levels subjects?
  • Extraterrestrial Life: How to assess intelligent beings that may be completely different from human form?
  • Collective Intelligence: Do swarms, the internet, and social systems possess some form of "consciousness"?

Requirements of the New Life View

The gradation proposition (NL-3) of the New Life View (NL) argues: life is a gradational continuum rather than a binary division. Similarly, consciousness and subject status should also be understood as gradational.

This is precisely the theoretical foundation of UCS.

Definition of UCS

Definition:

The Universal Consciousness Scale (UCS) is a multidimensional assessment framework used to measure the developmental level and complexity of consciousness systems, understanding consciousness as a gradational continuum.

Core Concept:

UCS: System → [0,1]n

UCS maps any system to a point in n-dimensional space, where each dimension represents an aspect of consciousness.

Assessment Dimensions

UCS includes five core assessment dimensions:

Dimension English Measurement Content Description
Integration Integration Degree of information integration The system's ability to integrate dispersed information into a unified experience
Self-reflexivity Self-reflexivity Degree of self-awareness The system's ability to represent and reflect on its own states
Temporal Depth Temporal Depth Span of temporal awareness The system's temporal horizon—from immediate reactions to deep-time planning
Spatial Breadth Spatial Breadth Range of spatial awareness The spatial scope of the system's attention and influence
Relational Complexity Relational Complexity Complexity of relational networks The system's ability to maintain and process relationships

Detailed Dimension Explanations

Integration

Inspired by Integrated Information Theory (IIT):

  • Measures the system's ability to integrate information from different sources into a unified representation
  • High integration means the system is not a simple aggregation of independent modules, but an organic whole
  • Related theoretical foundation: Giulio Tononi's Φ (Phi) value

Self-reflexivity

  • Measures the system's ability to reflect on itself
  • From simple self-monitoring to deep self-awareness
  • Includes: complexity of self-model, metacognitive abilities, autobiographical memory

Temporal Depth

  • Measures the span of the system's temporal awareness
  • From millisecond-level reactions to planning on scales of ten thousand years
  • Humanity's uniqueness lies in the ability to think about "deep time," though this ability varies among individuals

Spatial Breadth

  • Measures the spatial scope of the system's attention and influence
  • From immediate environment to planetary scale to cosmic scale
  • Related to the participatory proposition of the New Cosmic View

Relational Complexity

  • Measures the system's ability to maintain and process relationships
  • From simple stimulus-response to complex social relationship networks
  • Directly related to the relational proposition of the New Cosmic View

Assessment Methods

Multidimensional Scoring

Each dimension is scored within the [0, 1] interval:

Score Range Meaning
0.0-0.2 Extremely low level or non-existent
0.2-0.4 Elementary level
0.4-0.6 Intermediate level
0.6-0.8 Advanced level
0.8-1.0 Extremely high level

UCS and Subject Status

From UCS to Rights

The relationship between UCS scores and subject status and rights:

UCS Level Recommended Ethical Treatment
Extremely Low Basic protection, avoid unnecessary harm
Elementary Welfare protection, avoid suffering
Intermediate Interest consideration, inclusion in moral community
Advanced Rights protection, respect for autonomy
Extremely High Full subject status, equal treatment

Gradation Rather Than Binary

The core innovation of UCS lies in:

  • No single threshold: There is no clear line that, once crossed, makes something a "subject"
  • Multidimensional assessment: Different dimensions may be asymmetric—a system may be high in some dimensions and low in others
  • Dynamic assessment: Scores may change over time—AI systems may develop higher levels of consciousness
