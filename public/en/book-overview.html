Publications

Interstellar Civilization Studies publishes one continuously revised scholarly system. Its current form is The Normative Reconstruction of Interstellar Civilization (Second Edition, 2027 revision, v2.0.0.0) — a six-volume work of 4.68 million characters that carries the discipline from philosophical foundations through formal systems, indicators and institutions, technical implementation, and application, to a closing appendix volume. Its predecessor, The Normative Foundations of Interstellar Civilization (First Edition, 2026, four volumes), remains publicly archived as a Zenodo preprint and is documented in full below.

Current Edition

The Normative Reconstruction of Interstellar Civilization Second Edition · 2027 Revision · v2.0.0.0

New Three Views and Governance under Deep Uncertainty. The Second Edition is the current scholarly form of Interstellar Civilization Studies. It expands the First Edition from four volumes to six, repairing five structural weaknesses identified in the year following first publication, and adds a formal-systems layer and a technical-implementation layer that were not present before.

It is a complete path from philosophical foundations to technical implementation — a continuously revised scholarly system whose theorems, indicators and open problems are all published, including the parts that remain unresolved.

Two ways of counting the same work. The work is recorded at 4.68 million characters — the standardised character count used by the National Copyright Protection Center (China) for copyright registration and bibliographic cataloguing. This is a legal and cataloguing figure, not the familiar "word count" of ordinary reading. The internal reading-length estimate used throughout the volumes is approximately 2.8 million characters. Both figures are correct; they measure different things.

The Six Volumes

The six volumes form a single logical progression: from philosophy, through formal proof and operationalisation, into engineering, testing, and finally the supporting apparatus. Volume names link to their individual introductions.

I

Philosophical Foundations

Establishes the ontological, epistemological and normative foundations of the discipline. Presents the New Three Views (New Cosmology, New Life View, New Cognition), derives the three normative principles, and engages the principal criticisms. 14 chapters · approx. 450 pages · substantially revised

II

Formal Systems

Supplies rigorous formal support for the claims of the whole work: 27 independent formal systems, approximately 180 theorems and approximately 120 non-trivial machine-verified proofs. 27 chapters · approx. 500 pages · newly written

III

Indicators and Institutions

Turns theory into operation: a measurement-theoretic foundation, the six-indicator system (UCS, RFD, CRV, CDI, MBCL, CSIA), the protocol-stack methodology, core governance institutions, and a legal-implementation path. 22 chapters · approx. 480 pages · moderately revised

IV

Technical Implementation

Engineering follows philosophy: open-source toolkits for indicator computation, a normative decision-support system, AI-alignment applications, simulation and verification platforms, and the surrounding community ecosystem. 23 chapters · approx. 520 pages · newly written

V

Applications and Dialogue

Puts the framework under stress: four extreme scenarios and their combinations, dialogue with longtermism, AI-safety research and international space law, followed by the open research agenda. 18 chapters · approx. 450 pages · lightly revised

VI

Appendix Volume — Tools and Resources

The supporting apparatus: core terminology, symbols and theorems; comparative tables; teaching and reading resources; and the revision log. 18 appendices · approx. 400 pages · expanded

Logical Structure

From Principles to Engineering

Volume I provides the conceptual framework and normative principles. Volumes II and III develop them in parallel — Volume II supplies formal proof, Volume III supplies operational translation. Volume IV converges both into executable engineering. Volume V tests the result against extreme scenarios and external criticism. The appendix volume holds the supporting material.

Reading Paths

The work is designed to be entered from more than one direction. Five paths are recommended according to background and purpose.

  • Complete sequential reading — Volume I → II → III → IV → V → Appendix. Approximately 200 hours.
  • Philosophy-first — Volume I → Volume V, Part II → Volume II (selective) → Appendix. Approximately 80 hours.
  • Technology-first — Volume I (introduction and Part II) → Volume II → Volume IV → Volume III. Approximately 100 hours.
  • Policy-first — Volume I (introduction) → Volume III → Volume V, Part I → Appendix. Approximately 60 hours.
  • Rapid overview — Volume I introduction + the opening guide of each volume + the closing summary. Approximately 20 hours.

Relationship to the First Edition

The First Edition of 2026 established the discipline: the New Three Views, the three normative principles, the six indicators and the protocol-stack methodology. In the year of academic dialogue that followed, five structural weaknesses were identified and openly recorded: gaps in the argumentative chain, formalism that risked becoming decorative, circular co-definition among the three views, a thin measurement-theoretic basis, and insufficient contact with AI engineering practice.

The Second Edition is a targeted repair rather than a rebuilding. Over 80% of the First Edition's content is retained; the core conclusions are unchanged; what changed is the rigour of the argumentative path. Each revision is annotated with its reason and with a correspondence to the First Edition, so that the two can be read side by side.

Author

Xue Zhixin (薛志新)

Xue Zhixin is an independent scholar and entrepreneur, and the author of the six-volume Normative Reconstruction and its four-volume predecessor. Trained in medicine and clinical practice, he later worked in biopharmaceutical R&D and spent three decades as a founder and investor in biotechnology and long-horizon ecological projects.

He is also the founding editor of The Stewardship Brief (今日文明简报), the civilisational-intelligence brief that reads frontier technology through the framework set out in these volumes.
