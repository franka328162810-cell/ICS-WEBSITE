Book Overview

"Normative Foundations of Interstellar Civilization" is a groundbreaking work that fills the theoretical gap in interstellar civilization studies, proposing a complete framework from ontological reconstruction to institutional design, with direct policy implications for urgent contemporary issues such as AI governance, space governance, and intergenerational justice.

Book Introduction

Human civilization is experiencing a historic convergence of three pressures: artificial intelligence approaching the technological singularity of general intelligence, space exploration transitioning from state action to commercial competition, and global risks such as climate change approaching critical thresholds. These three pressures amplify each other, pushing humanity toward a fundamental question: under conditions of deep uncertainty, what could possibly become the norms for a world containing multiple intelligent agents, spanning interstellar scales, and affecting the deep future?

"Normative Foundations of Interstellar Civilization" directly confronts this challenge, proposing a complete framework from ontological reconstruction to institutional design.

Theoretical Innovation: New Three Views Framework
Proposes the "New Three Views"—New Cosmology, New Biology, and New Epistemology—as the theoretical core. New Cosmology argues that the universe is relational, processual, and participatory; New Biology argues that life is cross-substrate, a continuum sustained by negentropy flow; New Epistemology argues that cognition is participatory construction, "cognition is intervention." The three views mutually support each other, providing an onto-epistemological foundation for normative derivation.
Methodological Innovation: Protocol Stack Governance
Borrowing from computer science's protocol stack model, proposes a five-layer governance architecture (L₀ Physical Constraints Layer → L₁ Onto-Epistemological Layer → L₂ Normative Principles Layer → L₃ Institutional Design Layer → L₄ Operational Protocol Layer), plus a versioned rollback loop. This architecture achieves unity between "principle stability" and "operational flexibility," providing a new paradigm for long-term governance under deep uncertainty.
Tool Innovation: Indicator System
Transforms abstract principles into six measurable indicators: UCS (Universal Consciousness Scale) measures agent qualification, RFD (Recursive Freedom Degree) measures action's impact on possibility space, CRV (Commitment Reversibility & Verifiability) measures commitment verifiability, CDI (Civilization Development Index) measures civilization development level, MBCL (Memetic Biosafety Level) measures information risk, and CSIA (Cosmic System Impact Assessment) provides a comprehensive assessment framework.
Testing Innovation: Stress Tests & Simulation
Systematically tests theoretical robustness through four extreme scenarios (AGI breakthrough, First Contact, ecological tipping point, intergenerational justice extreme scenarios), and provides technical specifications for simulation frameworks, enabling theory to be tested in computer simulations.
Academic Dialogue: International Frontier Engagement
Engages in deep dialogue with international frontiers including longtermism (MacAskill, Ord), AI ethics (Russell, Christiano, Bengio), international space law (Masson-Zwaan, Tronchetti), and existential risk research (Bostrom, Hanson), clarifying the book's position in the academic landscape.

The book contains approximately 1.2 million words, 27 main chapters plus 10 technical appendices. It is a groundbreaking work that fills the theoretical gap in "Interstellar Civilization Studies," with direct policy implications for urgent contemporary issues such as AI governance, space governance, and intergenerational justice.

Relationship Between New and Old Three Views

The book explicitly demonstrates the relationship between the New Three Views and the traditional "Old Three Views" (worldview, outlook on life, values) as "dimensional expansion" rather than replacement—at the terrestrial scale and within human society, the Old Three Views remain valid; the New Three Views are their extension at interstellar scales and in multi-intelligent agent contexts.

Author Introduction

Author Xue Zhixin is an independent scholar and entrepreneur. After graduating from university, he entered clinical work as a gastroenterologist, during which he also served as a university English and pharmacology teacher. This combination of "medicine + language + pharmacology" already foreshadowed the author's later cross-disciplinary integrative thinking. After being promoted to attending physician, he transitioned to a foreign pharmaceutical company for biopharmaceutical R&D. This shift moved the author from "treating diseases" to "creating therapeutic means," expanding his perspective from individual patients to the entire biopharmaceutical industry chain.

Thirty years ago, as a principal founding partner, he established a biotechnology investment firm, engaging in angel, venture, and risk-stage investments in related high-tech projects, deeply participating in the early development of China's life sciences industry. Subsequently, the ecological construction investment company he founded was also one of China's earliest enterprises implementing long-cycle projects addressing climate change. His entrepreneurial experience not only gave the author a deep understanding of how technology, capital, and institutions interact in the real world, but this focus on long-term, systemic risks also foreshadowed his later theoretical interest in "interstellar civilization" and "deep-time responsibility."

Writing Background

The Starting Point of Life Experience

Before graduating from university, the author was diagnosed with malignant tumor and underwent resection surgery. For a young person about to enter the medical profession, this experience had a dual impact: as a patient, he faced the possibility of death; as a future doctor, he experienced the boundaries of medicine.

After recovery, the author transformed this into a lifelong starting point for inquiry. More importantly, this experience prompted him to pursue fundamental questions that transcend the scope of medicine. Thus, the author began a long journey of intellectual exploration.

The Journey of Intellectual Exploration

The author first started from his specialty in biology, attempting to find answers in the latest advances in life sciences. However, he quickly realized that pure scientific description cannot answer normative questions of "what ought to be." Thus, he turned to Eastern wisdom, beginning more than a decade of Buddhist practice. In Buddhism, he found a way of thinking that transcends binary opposition, an attitude toward life facing impermanence, and a cosmology that integrates the individual into a larger whole. This period of practice profoundly shaped the author's thinking, with its influence visible throughout the monograph—such as the emphasis on "relationality," the claim that "process" takes priority over "entity," and the preference for "continuum" thinking.

However, more than twenty years ago, the author made an important intellectual turn—abandoning Buddhism and entering the fields of philosophy of science and philosophy of mind. This turn was not a negation of Buddhism, but stemmed from his judgment of the times: humanity is becoming a trans-stellar species, breakthroughs in AI and other technologies herald the coming "singularity," and traditional wisdom traditions—whether Eastern or Western—need to be re-examined and transformed in new contexts. He realized that facing this unprecedented future requires a normative framework that can dialogue with modern science, be shared by different civilizations, and be transformed into operational institutions.

In the past decade or so, the author has continuously tracked frontier developments in physics, complex systems science, consciousness science, ethics, international law, and other fields, maintaining dialogue with the international academic community. He has integrated his background in biology, Buddhist practice, entrepreneurial experience, and investment practice into a unique perspective, attempting to answer the question he began asking in his youth—only now, the scale of this question has expanded from "individual life" to "interstellar civilization."

Manifestation of Intellectual Integration

The "New Three Views" framework proposed in the book embodies the author's unique intellectual integration:

  • The emphasis on "participatory universe" in New Cosmology has both the scientific foundation of quantum mechanics and the intellectual resonance of Buddhist "dependent origination and emptiness"
  • The discussion of "cross-substrate life" and "negentropy flow" in New Biology stems from his scientific literacy as a scholar with a biology background
  • The claim "cognition is intervention" in New Epistemology integrates his understanding of the Buddhist "mindfulness" tradition and contemporary embodied cognitive science

The "protocol stack" governance methodology embodies the practical wisdom of an entrepreneur—the author knows well that no matter how good an idea is, if it cannot be transformed into operational institutions, it can only remain on paper.

In a sense, "Normative Foundations of Interstellar Civilization" is not just an academic work, but also a "book of life." It is a cancer survivor's deep response to the meaning of life—when you have faced death, you will more seriously pursue the question: why does life exist? It is also a cross-disciplinary scholar's theoretical exploration—when you have crossed the boundaries of medicine, business, investment, Buddhism, and philosophy, you will more eagerly seek a framework that can integrate all of this. However, it is even more an entrepreneur's responsibility for the future of human civilization—when you have personally participated in climate change response practices, you will more clearly realize: the decisions and actions of our generation will affect the fate of countless future generations. As stated in the book: "Freedom is the guardianship of possibility."

The author has practiced this statement throughout his life—constantly crossing boundaries, keeping possibilities open, and transforming personal life experience into reflection on human civilization. In an era of deep uncertainty, he attempts to leave for the future a "protocol stack" that can be revised, developed, and transcended.
