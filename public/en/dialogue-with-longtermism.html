Dialogue with Longtermism: Critical Allies

Longtermism is the most influential contemporary ethical movement concerning the future. It shares with Interstellar Civilization Studies a concern for the long-term future and existential risks, but differs significantly in normative foundations and methodology. Here we explore the relationship between ICS and longtermism—we are "critical allies".

What is Longtermism?

Core Claims

Longtermism is an ethical view with the following core claims:

Claim Content
Time Neutrality The wellbeing of future people is as important as that of present people; it should not be discounted merely due to temporal distance
Numbers Priority The number of future people could vastly exceed the present, so total future wellbeing could vastly exceed present wellbeing
Existential Risk Priority Avoiding existential risks is the most important moral priority
Long-term Impact Priority We should prioritize actions with long-term impacts

Key Figures

Scholar Main Contribution
Derek Parfit "Reasons and Persons" laid theoretical foundations
Nick Bostrom Existential risk concept, superintelligence research
Toby Ord "The Precipice", existential risk quantification
William MacAskill "What We Owe the Future", effective altruism

Influence

Longtermism has gained widespread influence through the Effective Altruism movement:

• Billions of dollars in philanthropic funding directed toward longtermist priorities

• Major AI labs influenced by longtermist thinking

• Long-term impacts beginning to be considered in policymaking

Shared Concerns

ICS and longtermism share the following core concerns:

Shared Concern Content
Long-term Future Focus on the long-term future beyond the present generation
Existential Risks High priority on risks that could end civilization
Intergenerational Responsibility Belief that we have responsibilities to future generations
AI Risks View misaligned AI as a major existential risk
Institutional Change Advocate for institutional changes to protect the long-term future

Key Differences

Despite similar concerns, ICS and longtermism differ significantly across multiple dimensions:

Normative Foundations

Dimension Longtermism ICS
Core Framework Primarily utilitarian (maximizing wellbeing) Cosmic Responsibility Ethics (pluralistic normative foundations)
Core Value Wellbeing Freedom/possibility space
Value Aggregation Wellbeing can be aggregated across subjects Freedom is relational, cannot be simply aggregated

Scope of Moral Subjects

Dimension Longtermism ICS
Primary Focus Future humans Diverse agents (including AI, possible extraterrestrial intelligence)
AI Status Primarily as risk or tool May possess moral agency
Substrate Assumption Often implicitly prioritizes carbon-based life Explicit substrate independence

Methodology

Dimension Longtermism ICS
Decision Method Primarily expected value calculation Acknowledges limitations of deep uncertainty
Uncertainty Handling Probability assignment + expected value Reversibility priority + forbidden red lines
Institutional Design Emphasizes incentives and efficiency Protocol stack methodology (layered, evolvable)

Time Scale

Dimension Longtermism ICS
Time Horizon Hundreds to hundreds of thousands of years Similar, but emphasizes "deep time" concept
Time Discounting Opposes time discounting Similar, but emphasizes possibility space

ICS's Critique

ICS offers the following critical observations of longtermism:

Problems with Utilitarian Foundations

Problem Content
Aggregation Problem Can wellbeing be simply added across subjects?
Numbers Bias Should we prioritize the future simply because there will be more people?
Computational Impossibility Is expected value calculation feasible under deep uncertainty?
Counterintuitive Conclusions Utilitarianism may lead to counterintuitive conclusions (e.g., the Repugnant Conclusion)

Limitations in Scope of Subjects

Problem Content
Anthropocentrism Longtermism primarily focuses on humans, with insufficient consideration of AI moral agency
Carbon Bias Implicit assumption prioritizing carbon-based life
Extraterrestrial Neglect Insufficient consideration of possible extraterrestrial intelligence

Methodological Limitations

Problem Content
False Precision Expected value calculations may produce falsely precise results
Model Fragility Calculations based on uncertain models may mislead decision-making
Ignoring Irreversibility Expected value framework may underestimate the severity of irreversible risks

ICS's Contributions

ICS can contribute to the longtermist community:

Contribution Content
Alternative Normative Foundations Provides normative options beyond utilitarianism
Subject Expansion Extends moral concern to cross-substrate agents
Deep Uncertainty Framework Provides methodology for addressing deep uncertainty
Institutional Design Tools Protocol stack methodology applicable to long-term governance
Forbidden Red Lines Provides concept of absolute boundaries, complementing expected value framework

Space for Collaboration

ICS and longtermism have broad space for collaboration:

Domain Collaborative Possibilities
Existential Risk Research Joint research on identifying and addressing existential risks
AI Governance Joint development of AI governance frameworks
Institutional Design Joint development of institutions to protect the long-term future
Public Communication Joint efforts to raise public awareness of long-term issues
Academic Exchange Mutual criticism and learning

Positioning as "Critical Allies"

The relationship between ICS and longtermism can be characterized as "critical allies":

Dimension Content
Allies Share concern for the long-term future and can collaborate in practice
Critical Maintain critical dialogue on theoretical foundations, pointing out each other's shortcomings

This relationship reflects a healthy academic ecosystem: shared concerns enable collaboration, while theoretical differences promote mutual progress.
