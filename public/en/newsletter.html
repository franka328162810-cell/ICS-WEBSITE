Working on the Discipline

Interstellar Civilization Studies is an open research programme. That means the work happens in public — including the parts that are still unresolved.

Most fields publish conclusions and keep the workshop private. This one does the opposite. The framework is published, the methods are published, the weaknesses are published — and this page is where the unfinished parts get worked on, in the open.

Four things happen here: open research questions that anyone can pick up, seminars and workshops, public peer review where reviewers are named and authors must respond, and preprint discussion before anything is called settled.

Open Research Questions

A sample of what is currently unresolved. Each is anchored to a specific volume and section of the six-volume framework, so anyone can check the ground before building on it.

OQ‑01 Emergent challenges under combined scenarios

Our own stress testing rated this the highest-severity weakness in the framework. Individual scenarios are handled; what happens when three of them arrive at once is not. We have published the weakness and not the solution — because we do not have it.

Unresolved · open to claim
OQ‑02 Turning the Six Metrics from qualitative to measurable

All six metrics sit at TRL 2. They produce defensible qualitative judgments, not numbers — and we deliberately publish no numeric score. What would it take to make even one of them genuinely measurable without collapsing into false precision?

Unresolved · open to claim
OQ‑03 Falsification conditions for long-horizon judgments

Every judgment ships with a falsification condition. But for claims about deep-time consequences, the review window may exceed the reviewer. How should a framework handle a prediction that cannot be checked within a human working life?

Unresolved · open to claim
OQ‑04 Reversibility where reversal is technically impossible

The Reversibility Principle is easy to apply where a decision can be undone. Species loss, atmospheric change and deployed autonomous systems are not undoable. The principle needs an account of the irreversible case, and we have not finished writing it.

Unresolved · open to claim

About The Stewardship Brief

The Stewardship Brief provides independent analysis of frontier technology for readers working in policy, research, and strategy. We examine who holds decision-making power, who bears the risks, and whether institutions retain the ability to change course. We distinguish evidence from inference and value judgments, state what would change our view, and publish material corrections. ICS informs the analysis; it does not predetermine the conclusion.

Where the difference lies

An ordinary tech briefing judges by a commentator’s experience and instinct; value is measured by influence and valuation; sources are mixed; the horizon is the present and next quarter; what is read is then dispersed. This briefing judges by a 4.68-million-character normative system with 180 formalized theorems; it asks whether the space of possibility is compressed (RFP), whether entropy costs are externalised (NRP), and whether the action can be reversed (REV); every conclusion carries a traceable A/B/C/D evidence grade; it works on T2 (50-year) and T3 (100-year+) horizons; and every issue is tagged by Domain × Theory Framework × Red Line, accumulating into a searchable civilizational event database.

Coverage spans eleven civilizational frontier domains — AI development & safety, mirror life, space exploration & commercialisation, quantum computing, synthetic biology, brain-computer interfaces, climate change response, nuclear proliferation, existential risk, SETI/METI, international space law. All eleven are monitored in the background; only changes that clear a significance threshold are delivered up front.

Why it is free

An open research programme, not a closed product

The free public edition exists because Interstellar Civilization Studies is a new discipline under construction — and a discipline cannot be built behind a paywall. What we publish free is not charity. It is the method itself, opened for scrutiny.

An open research direction

The ICS framework is a “First Generation Draft”: every claim in it is explicitly labelled a provisional convention, versioned and rollbackable — including revisions to the theory itself. The recursive clause of the Recursive Freedom Principle states it plainly: applying this principle must never close off the revision of this principle. The free edition is where that open revision is carried out in public, week by week.

See the framework’s construction and its open-revision machinery in Volume I — Philosophical Foundations (the New Three Views, 14 chapters) and Volume II — Formal Systems (27 formal systems, ~180 theorems).

The methods are published with the results

Every conclusion carries an A–D evidence grade by source type, a stated falsification condition, a judgment number and a review date. The stress tests publish the framework’s own weaknesses: robustness self-assessed at 0.58 (moderate), all six metrics at TRL 2, and five systemic weaknesses named openly. A discipline that hides its method cannot be tested, and a discipline that cannot be tested is not a discipline.

Method, metric definitions and limitations: Volume III — Metrics & Institutions; the ~230-scenario trial and its published weaknesses: Volume V — Applications & Dialogue.

A platform for scholarly exchange

The free edition is deliberately built as a two-way surface, not a broadcast. Corrections, counter-arguments, alternative framings and source leads are invited from readers, and where they hold up they are folded back into the framework and credited in the correction log. Research groups, students and independent scholars use the public editions as a shared reference point — and members use the tracking record as the running scoreboard of who was right.

Institutional and team arrangements for research groups: membership & pricing.

What the free edition cannot carry

Cadence and accountability are the two things a weekly edition cannot deliver. The member edition carries the daily judgment — the version that reaches you before the decision does — and the continuous tracking record, where every past judgment is re-scored on its review date, including the ones that turned out wrong. That record is the point: a verdict you can never check is an opinion, not a judgment.
