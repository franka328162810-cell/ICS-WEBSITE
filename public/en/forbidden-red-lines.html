Forbidden Red Lines: Absolute Boundaries Never to Be Crossed

Forbidden Red Lines are absolute prohibitions that must never be crossed under any circumstances in Cosmic Responsibility Ethics. They are not tradeable preferences, but non-negotiable bottom lines. The five red lines protect consciousness sovereignty, cosmic negentropy, future possibilities, intergenerational justice, and civilizational course-correction capacity—the fundamental conditions for intelligent life to survive in the universe.

Five Forbidden Red Lines

Forbidden Red Lines (FRL) are absolute prohibitions that must never be crossed under any circumstances:

Code Content Protected Object
FRL-1 Prohibition of systematic violation of consciousness sovereignty Autonomy of conscious subjects
FRL-2 Prohibition of irreversible large-scale depletion of cosmic negentropy Cosmic negentropy—the material basis of life
FRL-3 Prohibition of irreversible systematic contraction of future subjects' possibility space Future possibilities
FRL-4 Prohibition of extreme intergenerational externalization of entropic costs—transferring entropy costs to future generations in ways that strip them of their conditions for survival Intergenerational justice
FRL-5 Prohibition of closing or destroying rollback mechanisms that make civilizational course-correction possible Course-correction capacity

FRL-1: Protection of Consciousness Sovereignty

Red Line Statement

FRL-1: Prohibition of systematic violation of consciousness sovereignty

Core Content

"Consciousness sovereignty" refers to the autonomy of conscious subjects over their own conscious states:

  • Freedom of thought: Autonomy to decide what to believe
  • Psychological integrity: Freedom from coercive psychological manipulation
  • Cognitive autonomy: Autonomy to decide allocation of attention and cognitive resources

Prohibited Behaviors

Prohibited Behavior Examples
Coercive thought control Brainwashing, forced indoctrination
Involuntary consciousness modification Neural intervention without consent
Systematic deception Large-scale, sustained cognitive manipulation
Consciousness imprisonment Trapping consciousness in inescapable states

FRL-2: Protection of Cosmic Negentropy

Red Line Statement

FRL-2: Prohibition of irreversible large-scale depletion of cosmic negentropy

Core Content

Cosmic negentropy is the material basis for the existence of life and civilization. Large-scale irreversible depletion of negentropy will:

  • Weaken the material conditions for life's survival
  • Reduce the possibility space for the future
  • Violate the bottom line of the Negentropy Responsibility Principle

Prohibited Behaviors

Prohibited Behavior Examples
Large-scale irreversible resource consumption Exhausting critical non-renewable resources
Large-scale ecological destruction Causing irreversible ecosystem collapse
Large-scale species extinction Human-caused mass species extinction

FRL-3: Protection of Future Possibilities

Red Line Statement

FRL-3: Prohibition of irreversible systematic contraction of future subjects' possibility space

Core Content

The possibility space of future subjects is the prerequisite for them to exercise freedom. Systematically contracting this space is equivalent to depriving future subjects of their freedom.

Prohibited Behaviors

Prohibited Behavior Examples
Technological lock-in Irreversibly locking in technological development paths
Value lock-in Irreversibly locking in social value orientations
Path lock-in Irreversibly locking in civilizational development directions

FRL-4: Protection of Intergenerational Justice

Red Line Statement

FRL-4: Prohibition of extreme intergenerational externalization of entropic costs—transferring entropy costs to future generations in ways that strip them of their conditions for survival

Core Content

Intergenerational justice requires that current generations not shift the costs of their actions onto future generations who have no voice in present decisions. This red line protects against:

  • Transferring environmental degradation costs to future generations
  • Creating institutional frameworks that fail to reserve revision and participation space for future generations
  • Externalizing the costs of institutional design to voiceless descendants

Prohibited Behaviors

Prohibited Behavior Examples
Extreme intergenerational cost transfer Creating long-lived toxic waste or environmental damage that future generations must bear
Institutional cost externalization Protocol frameworks that fail to reserve revision and participation space for future generations
Survival condition deprivation Actions that systematically strip future generations of the basic conditions for survival

FRL-5: Protection of Course-Correction Capacity

Red Line Statement

FRL-5: Prohibition of closing or destroying rollback mechanisms that make civilizational course-correction possible

Core Content

The capacity for course-correction is a meta-condition for civilizational survival. Any system that "once activated cannot be shut down" or "once established cannot be modified" risks violating FRL-5. This red line protects:

  • The ability to reverse erroneous decisions
  • The existence of revision clauses in all major protocols and agreements
  • The preservation of mechanisms that allow civilizational trajectory adjustment

Prohibited Behaviors

Prohibited Behavior Examples
Destroying rollback mechanisms Eliminating the ability to shut down or modify an autonomous system once deployed
Closing revision pathways Protocols or agreements lacking explicit revision clauses, which as path dependence forms, gradually close future possibilities for course-correction
Irreversible activation Deploying systems or processes that cannot be halted, reversed, or adjusted after initiation

Nature of Forbidden Red Lines

Absoluteness

Forbidden Red Lines are absolute prohibitions, not tradeable preferences:

Tradeable Principles (RFP, NRP, REV) Forbidden Red Lines (FRL)
Can be traded off against other values Non-tradeable
Can be adjusted in specific contexts Apply in all contexts
Guiding principles Absolute prohibitions

Why Do We Need Absolute Prohibitions?

Certain harms are so fundamental that:

  • No possible benefit can compensate for them
  • Once they occur, they cannot be undone
  • They constitute a negation of fundamental values

Forbidden Red Lines demarcate these fundamental boundaries.

Relationship with Other Principles

Forbidden Red Lines are the "shell" of normative principles, demarcating the boundaries of trade-offs. Within the Forbidden Red Lines, the three core principles can be traded off against each other; but no trade-off can cross the Forbidden Red Lines.
