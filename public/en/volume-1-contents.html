Normative Foundations of Interstellar Civilizations

Full online text of Volume I, "Theoretical Foundations," of the first edition (Preliminary Edition, Four Volumes). Volume I constructs a normative theoretical framework oriented toward deep time scales and diverse intelligent agents, comprising Chapter 0, the Introduction, Chapters 1-5 and Chapters 11-15. Body text, footnotes, references and appendices are published in full, chapter by chapter.

Contents

Part I  Problems and Methods

Chapter 0 Methodological Commitments and Boundary Declarations Establishes the epistemic posture of the whole work: provisional convention, fallibility, and the explicit boundary declarations that mark what this theory does not claim. Introduction Three Puzzles and a Theoretical Preview Opens the guiding puzzles of interstellar civilization studies and previews the theoretical apparatus developed across the volume.

Part II  The Three New Views

Chapter 1 The New Cosmology: Relational, Processual, Participatory Argues for a relational, processual and participatory picture of the cosmos, replacing substance-based assumptions inherited from classical metaphysics. Chapter 2 The New View of Life: Cross-Substrate, Negentropic, Continuous Redefines life as a negentropic, cross-substrate and continuous phenomenon, opening the moral community beyond carbon-based biology. Chapter 3 The New View of Cognition: Participatory, Social, Normative Reconstructs cognition as participatory and social, and shows why cognition is inseparable from normative evaluation. Chapter 4 The Unity and Interconnection of the Three Views Demonstrates the internal unity of the new cosmology, the new view of life and the new view of cognition as a single coherent framework. Chapter 5 From Description to Norm: Deriving the Bridge Principles Derives the bridge principles that carry the descriptive framework into the normative domain, resolving the is-ought gap for this setting.

Part III  Normative Principles

Chapter 11 The Recursive Freedom Principle Formulates the first normative principle: recursive freedom as a condition that agents must be able to revise their own ends. Chapter 12 The Negentropy Responsibility Principle Formulates the duty to sustain and steward negentropic order across deep time and diverse substrates. Chapter 13 The Reversibility Principle Formulates reversibility as a constraint on irreversible action under deep uncertainty. Chapter 14 Cosmic Responsibility Ethics: Integrating the Three Principles Integrates the three principles into a single ethic of cosmic responsibility. Chapter 15 Dialogue with Existing Ethical Theories Situates cosmic responsibility ethics in dialogue with consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics and contractarianism.

This volume is the full first-edition text (v1.0.0.0, 2026), published chapter by chapter. The six volumes of the second edition are introduced only; their full text is not published, for copyright reasons.
