§4.1.1 From the New Cosmology to the New View of Life

I. The Basic Structure of the Logical Connection

There is a profound logical connection between the New Cosmology (NC) and the New View of Life (NL). The New Cosmology provides the ontological basis for the New View of Life, and the New View of Life is the concrete unfolding of the New Cosmology in the domain of life.

Proposition 4.1 (The Entailment Relation from NC to NL)

The three core propositions of the New Cosmology (NC-1, NC-2, NC-3) entail the three core propositions of the New View of Life (NL-1, NL-2, NL-3). This entailment is not a strict logical deduction, but a conceptual, natural extension.

II. The Derivation from NC-1 to NL-1

NC-1 (relationality) holds that the basic unit of reality is relation, not substance. This ontological position has direct implications for the concept of life.

The path of derivation:

Step 1: NC-1 (relationality)

The basic unit of reality is relation, not independently existing substance

Step 2: Life is part of reality

Life exists within the universe, and is a form of reality

Step 3: The relational nature of life

If reality is relational, then life is relational too

Life is not an independently existing substance, but an organizational pattern in a network of relations

Step 4: A functional definition

If life is an organizational pattern in a network of relations, then the essence of life is functional

Function can be realized by different material substrates

Conclusion: NL-1 (cross-substrate life)

The essence of life is functional, and is not limited to a particular material substrate

Proposition 4.2 (From NC-1 to NL-1)

NC-1 (relationality) entails NL-1 (cross-substrate life): if the basic unit of reality is relation rather than substance, then life, as a form of reality, is essentially relational and functional rather than material. Therefore life is not limited to a particular material substrate.

NC-2 (process) holds that reality is processual: change and becoming are basic, and static existence is derivative. This ontological position has direct implications for the conditions of life's persistence.

Step 1: NC-2 (process)

Reality is processual; change and becoming are basic

Step 2: Life is a process

Life is not static existence, but a continuous process

Step 3: A process requires maintenance

The continuation of any process requires the maintenance of conditions

Step 4: Thermodynamic constraint

In the physical universe, the maintenance of a process requires an input of energy/negentropy

(This is a physical constraint at the L₀ layer.)

Step 5: The negentropy condition of life

Life, as a process, requires the maintenance of a flow of negentropy for its continuation

Conclusion: NL-2 (the negentropy condition of persistence)

Life takes a flow of negentropy as its condition of persistence

Proposition 4.3 (From NC-2 to NL-2)

NC-2 (process) entails NL-2 (the negentropy condition of persistence): if reality is processual, then life, as a process, requires the maintenance of conditions for its continuation. In the physical universe, this condition is a flow of negentropy. Therefore life takes a flow of negentropy as its condition of persistence.

IV. The Derivation from NC-3 to NL-3

NC-3 (participation) holds that the universe is participatory: the observer participates in constructing reality. This ontological position has direct implications for the understanding of consciousness.

The path of derivation:

Step 1: NC-3 (participation)

The universe is participatory; the observer participates in constructing reality

Step 2: Consciousness is the basis of participation

Participation requires some form of consciousness or perceptual capacity

Step 3: The gradualness of participation

If participation is a basic feature of the universe, then participation may be gradual

Different systems may have different degrees of participatory capacity

Step 4: The gradualness of consciousness

If participatory capacity is gradual, then consciousness, as the basis of participation, is gradual too

Conclusion: NL-3 (the continuum of consciousness)

Consciousness is a gradual, multidimensional continuum

Proposition 4.4 (From NC-3 to NL-3)

NC-3 (participation) entails NL-3 (the continuum of consciousness): if the universe is participatory, then participatory capacity may be gradual. Consciousness, as the basis of participation, is therefore gradual rather than binary.

II. The Formalized Statement of Relationality

Definition 4.1 (Relationality)

Relationality is an ontological position which holds that:

  • (1) the basic unit of reality is relation, not independently existing substance;
  • (2) the properties of a substance are relational, depending on its relations to other substances;
  • (3) the whole cannot be reduced to its parts, and relation has ontological priority.

Formalized statement:

Let be a set of entities, a set of relations, and a property function.

In the New Cosmology:

NC-1 directly holds that reality is relational. This is the ontological-level expression of relationality.

In the New View of Life:

NL-1 holds that life is functional, and is not limited to a particular substrate. Function is relational — function depends on the relation between a system and its environment.

In the New View of Cognition:

NK-2 holds that knowledge is a social achievement. Sociality is relational — society is a network of relations.

Proposition 4.17 (Relationality as a Common Basis)

Relationality is a common basis of the Three New Views: the New Cosmology holds that reality is relational (NC-1), the New View of Life holds that life is a functional organization in a network of relations (NL-1), and the New View of Cognition holds that knowledge is an achievement of social relations (NK-2). Relationality runs through the Three Views and is the first basis of their unity.

§4.5 The Limits of the Three Views and Open Problems

§4.5.1 Open Problems at the Conceptual Level

1. The Ontological Status of Relations

Open Problem 4.1 (The ontological status of relations)

The Three New Views hold that relations take priority over substances, but the ontological status of relations remains an open problem:

  • Can relations exist independently of their relata?
  • Are relations basic, or can they be further analyzed?
  • How is the exact meaning of "relations take priority over substances" to be understood?

Current position: This work adopts a moderate relationalist stance, holding that the properties of substances are relational, but not that relations can exist entirely independently of their relata.

2. The Nature of Consciousness

Open Problem 4.2 (The nature of consciousness)

The Three New Views hold that consciousness is a degree-based multidimensional continuum (NL-3), but the nature of consciousness remains an open problem:

  • How is the "hard problem" of consciousness (how subjective experience arises from physical processes) to be solved?
  • Do the five dimensions of UCS exhaust all aspects of consciousness?
  • How is the threshold of consciousness to be determined?

Current position: This work does not attempt to solve the "hard problem" of consciousness; instead, it takes a functionalist approach, attending to the functional dimensions of consciousness. UCS is a revisable framework that can be adjusted in light of new research.

3. The Source of Normativity

Open Problem 4.3 (The source of normativity)

The Three New Views hold that cognition has a normative dimension (NK-3), but the source of normativity remains an open problem:

  • Can normativity be derived from natural facts?
  • Is normativity basic, or can it be further analyzed?
  • How is the naturalistic fallacy to be avoided?

Current position: This work handles the derivation from "is" to "ought" through the bridge principle (Chapter 5), but acknowledges that the validity of this derivation remains contestable.

§4.5.2 Open Problems at the Level of Argument

1. The Strength of the Entailment Relations

Open Problem 4.4 (The strength of the entailment relations)

This chapter has argued for the entailment relations among the Three Views, but the strength of these entailment relations is an open problem:

  • Are the entailment relations logically necessary, or merely natural conceptual extensions?
  • Are there other possible paths of derivation?
  • If one of the views is revised, what effect does this have on the other two?

Current position: This work holds that the entailment relations are natural conceptual extensions, not logical necessities. This means that the entailment relations can be questioned and revised.

2. The Sufficiency of Coherence

Open Problem 4.5 (The sufficiency of coherence)

This chapter has argued for the coherence of the Three Views, but whether coherence is sufficient to justify them is an open problem:

  • Is coherence a sufficient condition for justification?
  • Might there be other equally coherent but different frameworks?
  • How is a choice to be made among coherent frameworks?

Current position: This work acknowledges that coherence is not a sufficient condition for justification, but holds that it is a necessary condition. The justification of the Three Views also depends on their consistency with experience, their explanatory power, and their practical utility.

3. The Validity of Retrospective Support

Open Problem 4.6 (The validity of retrospective support)

This chapter has argued for the retrospective support from NK to NC, but the validity of this retrospective support is an open problem:

  • Is retrospective support circular reasoning?
  • How does retrospective support enhance the credibility of the Three Views?
  • Can retrospective support be weakened?

Current position: This work holds that retrospective support is not circular reasoning but mutual coherence. Retrospective support enhances the overall credibility of the Three Views, but it can be questioned and revised.

§4.5.3 Open Problems at the Level of Application

1. Cross-Civilizational Applicability

Open Problem 4.7 (Cross-civilizational applicability)

Whether the Three New Views apply to all possible civilizations is an open problem:

  • Might an extraterrestrial civilization have a fundamentally different ontology and epistemology?
  • Are the Three New Views anthropocentric?
  • How is the cross-civilizational applicability of the Three New Views to be verified?

Current position: This work holds that the Three New Views are designed for the interstellar-civilization situation, but acknowledges that their cross-civilizational applicability requires further verification. NK-4 (negotiable standards) provides a framework for cross-civilizational negotiation.

2. Difficulties of Operationalization

Open Problem 4.8 (Difficulties of operationalization)

The operationalization of the Three New Views faces difficulties:

  • How is the degree of consciousness (UCS) to be measured?
  • How is the compression of the space of possibilities (RFD) to be measured?
  • How are negotiable standards (NK-4) to be applied in practice?

Current position: This work provides operational frameworks (UCS, RFD, CRV, etc.), but acknowledges that these frameworks require further development and verification.

3. Compatibility with Existing Institutions

Open Problem 4.9 (Compatibility with existing institutions)

The compatibility of the Three New Views with existing institutions is an open problem:

  • How are the Three New Views to be made compatible with existing legal systems?
  • How are the Three New Views to be made compatible with existing frameworks of international governance?
  • How are the Three New Views to be integrated into existing institutions gradually?

Current position: This work holds that the relation of the Three New Views to existing institutions is one of "dimensional upgrade and expansion" rather than "replacement." At the everyday scale, existing institutions still apply; at the interstellar-civilization scale, a new institutional framework based on the Three New Views must be developed.
