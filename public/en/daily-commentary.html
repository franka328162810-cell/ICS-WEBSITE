The 100 Signals That Waited 21 Years — Who Decides How Humanity Responds?

Source: Live Science / SETI Institute | January 2026 (FAST verification ongoing)

The SETI@Home distributed computing project, launched in 1999, used twenty-one years of data from the Arecibo Observatory to mobilize millions of volunteers worldwide, collectively identifying more than 12 billion "signal candidates." After Arecibo's catastrophic cable collapse in 2020, the project entered its final phase. Scientists have now compressed the candidate list to 100 priority targets, handing them over to China's FAST — the world's largest single-dish radio telescope — for systematic follow-up verification (Source: Live Science, January 2026). This is the largest-scale follow-up investigation of SETI signal candidates in history.

🔍ICS Framework Analysis

📡Dimension One: The Absolute Priority of REV in First Contact

On the surface, this is a story about methodological progress in astronomy: a bigger telescope, a more rigorous filter, an answer inching closer. ICS framework analysis indicates this event is actually a cross-section of something else entirely — a countdown to a governance crisis over the ownership of an irreversible decision.

ICS's three core principles carry a clear priority order under deep uncertainty: REV (Reversibility Principle) > RFP (Recursive Freedom Principle) > NRP (Negentropic Responsibility Principle). First Contact represents the most archetypal deep-uncertainty scenario in human history. We don't know the other party's intentions. We don't know whether their value system is commensurable with ours. Under these conditions, the reversibility of any response outweighs every other consideration.

Yet no binding international legal framework currently governs who has the authority to transmit a response signal upon receiving a suspected extraterrestrial contact, or what review process must precede such a transmission. Neither the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) nor the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA) has a binding METI protocol. Any capable nation could transmit unilaterally, without international consensus. Once a signal is sent, it travels at the speed of light toward its destination. There is no recall button.

⚠ FRL-5 Risk Threshold Alert

ICS Forbidden Red Line 5 (FRL-5) states: Prohibit closing or destroying the rollback mechanisms that make civilizational course-correction possible. Actively transmitting to a civilization of unknown intent structurally closes the option of "choosing not to contact" — precisely the boundary FRL-5 warns against.

🏛️Dimension Two: Who Speaks for Humanity? — The Absence of MIT

Suppose FAST confirms a signal tomorrow. Then what? The honest answer is: no procedure exists. No institution has been authorized by the international community to say, "Yes, this represents humanity's collective will."

ICS's institutional design module proposes the concept of a Multi-Intelligence Tribunal (MIT) — a decision-making body that accommodates diverse actors and transcends the boundaries of traditional international law. The most paradigmatic use case for MIT is exactly this: First Contact. MIT does not exist in reality. More critically, its functional equivalent — a binding international METI protocol — also does not exist. We are confronting a 21st-century decision that could determine the fate of our entire species using 20th-century governance tools.

🤖Dimension Three: AI as First Interpreter — A New Source of Uncertainty

The verification pipeline from SETI@Home to FAST relies increasingly on machine-learning algorithms to filter and classify signals. This is not inherently problematic. But it introduces a new structural tension: AI systems will be the first "interpreters" standing between humanity and any potential extraterrestrial signal. The AI determines whether a signal is "worth investigating" or "noise" — and human insight into that judgment process may be severely limited.

ICS FRL-1 applies directly here: Prohibit deploying systems whose decision mechanisms humans cannot understand in high-risk domains. SETI signal classification is unambiguously high-risk — a genuine signal misclassified as "noise" will be buried in databases permanently; a falsely flagged noise pattern could trigger an erroneous chain of downstream decisions.

⏳Deep-Time Perspective: Century & Millennium Impact

[T2 Level — Next 10 to 50 Years]: Will the METI governance vacuum be filled during the FAST verification window? Or will it be perpetually deferred with the justification that "no signal has been confirmed yet"? Path dependency is forming — every year we delay building international protocols, the cost of filling that gap increases. Meanwhile, asymmetries in radio-transmission capabilities across nations are growing, widening the window for unilateral action.

[T3 Level — Century Scale]: If First Contact does occur, how will today's governance vacuum determine the character of that contact? Will humanity present itself as a unified, deliberate civilization — or as whichever entity responds fastest? This is not merely a diplomatic question. It is the design of civilization's first business card.

The ICS framework classifies this type of situation as a high-irreversibility path-selection node. Before actual contact occurs, we still inhabit a window in which governance norms can be proactively established. That window will not remain open indefinitely.

💡Key Concepts

REV — Reversibility Principle

Under conditions of deep uncertainty, prioritize reversible actions. When an irreversible action is unavoidable, the burden of proof lies with the actor, who must rigorously justify its necessity. In First Contact scenarios, REV takes absolute precedence over all other ICS principles.

FRL-5 — Rollback Mechanism Prohibition

One of ICS's five Forbidden Red Lines: prohibit closing or destroying any mechanism that makes civilizational course-correction possible. Any system that "once activated cannot be shut down" or "once established cannot be modified" risks violating FRL-5.

MIT — Multi-Intelligence Tribunal

An ICS-proposed institutional design concept: a decision-making body that accommodates diverse actors — including AI system proxies and representatives of future generations — transcending the boundaries of traditional international law. Its most paradigmatic application is the governance of First Contact scenarios.

🎯Recommendations & Outlook

1
For international institutions (UNOOSA / ITU): Use the FAST verification window to initiate negotiations toward a binding international METI protocol. The core clause need only be one: any active transmission toward extraterrestrial targets must undergo review by a multi-nation authorization committee, with a mandatory waiting period before any confirmed signal triggers a response. "No signal confirmed yet" is not a reason to delay this discussion — it is precisely the right moment for it.
2
For the SETI scientific community: Mandate interpretability audits for AI signal-classification systems — not just accuracy metrics, but documentation of which signal features are systematically underweighted or overweighted, along with human review checkpoints at critical decision nodes. When an AI says "this is not a signal," we need to know why.

Core Recommendation: Governance frameworks must precede signal confirmation — not be improvised in its aftermath. The FAST verification phase represents humanity's last low-pressure window to establish First Contact norms.
