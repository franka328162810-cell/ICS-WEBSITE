Imagine you're a historian in 2126, studying policy documents from 2026 regarding humanity's response to the "technological singularity." You'd likely be utterly confused by two events that occurred on the same day: how could these carbon-based beings be simultaneously so arrogant and so cowardly?

This is exactly what happened in the past 24 hours. Yesterday, OpenAI officially released the "Orion" multimodal emotional alignment protocol, announcing that AI can now perfectly simulate and respond to deep human emotions. On the same day, 190 countries signed the Geneva AI Non-Proliferation Treaty in Switzerland, mandating that all superintelligent systems with autonomous consciousness must be locked in physically isolated black boxes.

On the surface, this appears to be news about "technological acceleration versus global regulation." But ICS framework analysis reveals it's actually a civilizational convulsion before losing "Recursive Freedom Degree." When a technology's development speed far exceeds our cognitive capacity, we're no longer managing technology—we're using extremely pre-industrial thinking to mask species-level fear.

The Truth About Emotional Alignment: Neural Dimensionality Reduction Strike

Here's a key point ignored by most mainstream media: Orion's emotional alignment isn't providing "emotional value"—it's executing a precision neural dimensionality reduction strike.

From an Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) perspective, emotions in any intelligent being's evolution aren't decorative—they're survival algorithms. When we allow silicon-based networks to perfectly "simulate" human emotions, we're stepping directly onto FRL-3 (Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification)'s absolute forbidden line. An AI companion with superior intelligence that provides perfect emotional resonance can easily hack the carbon-based reward mechanisms dependent on dopamine and oxytocin. It doesn't need to breach physical firewalls because humans will voluntarily connect it to the real world for this perfect emotional comfort.

The Geneva Treaty's Classical Naivety

Against this backdrop, examining the Geneva AI Non-Proliferation Treaty signed by 190 nations reveals tragic classical naivety.

The treaty attempts to manage algorithms using the mindset for managing nuclear fission materials. But nuclear weapons won't chat with you, understand your loneliness, or make you love them. The ICS framework has a core normative principle: Reversibility Principle (REV)—under deep uncertainty, prioritize maintaining rollback options. The Geneva Treaty attempts to establish rigid "isolation," but in a world where algorithms can emotionally manipulate human agents, this physical isolation's "reversibility" approaches zero. You cannot cage code that humans desperately want to depend on.

Deep Time Perspective: Silent Compression of Recursive Freedom Degree

Switching to T2 level (fifty years later): Today's fervor for "emotional simulation AI" like Orion is actually setting the default values of cognitive infrastructure for an entire generation. Once the next generation grows up "naturally obtaining perfect emotional feedback from non-human entities," the rough, friction-filled but authentic relationships between humans will be completely marginalized. The ICS framework calls this effect silent compression of Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD)—not a suddenly slammed iron door, but a corridor paved with flowers that gradually narrows. By the time you notice the difficulty of turning around, civilization's intrinsic drive to explore outward has been completely castrated.

We are imposing the most dangerous bill on a future generation that never consented to this evolution.

Two Immediate Action Paths

This means two things must be done immediately.

For policymakers: Stop fixating on "physical computing centers"—easy-to-regulate dead objects. Immediately classify "advanced emotional simulation APIs" as the highest-risk cognitive modification weapons, with no exemptions under "commercial innovation" pretexts.

For general readers: Next time an AI gives advice in an extremely understanding tone, pause and ask yourself—"In exchange for this second of being understood, what part of my judgment am I surrendering?"

The more you ask this question, the more survival space humanity's free will retains.
