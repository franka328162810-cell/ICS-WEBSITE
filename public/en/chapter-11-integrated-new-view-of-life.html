§11.1 The Integration of MS + NE + SC

§11.1.1 The Necessity of Integration

The Problem Posed

Chapters 8-10 constructed three independent formal systems, respectively:

  • MS (Material Substrate): formalizes cross-substrate life (NL-1)
  • NE (Negentropy): formalizes negentropy organization (NL-2)
  • SC (Subject Continuum): formalizes the subject continuum (NL-3)

These three systems are each independent, but the argumentation of Chapter 2 of Volume 1 shows that the three core propositions of the New View of Life are mutually supporting and inseparable as a whole. Therefore, we need to answer the following questions:

Question 11.1 (the necessity of integration):

Can the three independent formal systems MS, NE, and SC be integrated into a unified formal system NL? If so, does the integration preserve consistency? If not, does it mean that the argumentation of Volume 1 harbors a hidden contradiction?

Question 11.2 (the non-triviality of integration):

Is the integrated system NL merely the simple union of the three subsystems, or does it produce new theorems (emergence)?

Question 11.3 (the uniqueness of integration):

Is the manner of integration unique? Do different manners of integration lead to different theoretical consequences?

This chapter will systematically answer these three questions.

§11.9.3 Methodological Reflection on the Formalization

Through the construction of NL, we have gained a deeper understanding of the methodology of formalization itself.

Reflection 1: Formalization is revelation, not creation

Formalization does not create new truths, but reveals truths that already exist but are not obvious.

For example, Theorem 11.11 (the uniqueness of lifehood) was not something we "invented," but something "derived" from reasonable axioms. This shows that the objective structure of lifehood already existed before the formalization.

Reflection 2: The costs and benefits of formalization

Formalization has costs:

  • conceptually: it requires precise definitions, and may lose the insights contained in vagueness
  • technically: it requires specialized training
  • temporally: the process of formalization is time-consuming

But the benefits of formalization are greater:

  • it reveals hidden logical structures
  • it exposes weak links in argumentation
  • it produces new theorems and insights
  • it provides rigorous standards of testing

The success of NL shows that, for core concepts, the benefits of formalization far outweigh the costs.

Reflection 3: The complementarity of formalization and natural language

Formalization and natural-language argumentation are complementary, not opposed:

  • natural-language argumentation provides intuition and motivation
  • formalization provides rigor and precision
  • the combination of the two produces the strongest argumentation

The combination of Volume 1 (natural language) and Volume 2 (formalization) embodies this complementarity.

Reflection 4: The limitations of formalization

Formalization has its limitations:

  • it cannot capture all philosophical content (e.g., the subjectivity of consciousness)
  • the "elegance" of formalization may mislead us into accepting problematic premises
  • the precision of formalization may create a false sense of certainty

We need to maintain a clear awareness of the limitations of formalization and avoid the "worship of formalization."

Reflection 5: The openness of formalization

Our formalization is not "final," but "open":

  • the axioms can be modified
  • the bridge axioms can be added
  • the subsystems can be replaced

This openness embodies the principle of "the revisability of knowledge." The formal system itself follows the principle of versioned governance.

Problem 1: The completeness of NL

  • we have proved the consistency of NL, but not its completeness
  • by Gödel's incompleteness theorem, NL cannot be complete
  • but we have not yet explicitly pointed out which propositions are undecidable in NL

Problem 2: The controversy over the measurement of consciousness

  • NL adopts as the measure of consciousness
  • but there is controversy over this within the consciousness science community
  • if a paradigm shift occurs in consciousness science in the future, NL will need to be revised accordingly

Problem 3: The determination of cross-substrate functional equivalence

  • Theorem 11.14 proves the mathematical possibility of cross-substrate equivalence
  • but how to determine functional equivalence in practice remains an open problem
  • this requires a detailed methodology of functional analysis (Volume 4)

Problem 4: The precise determination of thresholds

  • the precise values of several thresholds of NL () require further research
  • the current values are reasonable estimates based on empirical data
  • more rigorous calibration methods will be needed in the future

Problem 5: Deep connection with quantum mechanics

  • NL is based on classical physics and information theory
  • but quantum mechanics may provide a new perspective on life (e.g., quantum biology)
  • a quantum version of NL is a direction for future research
