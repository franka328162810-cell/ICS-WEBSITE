Interstellar Civilization Studies

Laying Normative Foundations for a Civilization's Journey Across Millennia. ICS is an interdisciplinary research field oriented toward the long-term future of human civilization, exploring philosophical, ethical, and governance challenges we must confront today as we venture into the cosmos.

What is ICS?

Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) is an interdisciplinary research field oriented toward the long-term future of human civilization. We attempt to answer critical questions:

When AI may surpass human intelligence, who qualifies as a "subject"? When our decisions affect descendants ten thousand years hence, how should we be responsible for the future? When humanity ventures into interstellar space, what norms should transcend planetary boundaries? When consciousness may exist in silicon-based, quantum, or unknown substrates, what constitutes value?

This is not science fiction, but philosophical, ethical, and governance challenges we must confront today.

Why Does It Matter?

A Critical Turning Point

We stand at a critical juncture in civilization's history. Traditional ethics remains limited to anthropocentrism and planetary scale. AGI safety research lacks a unified normative framework. International space law hasn't fully considered intergenerational justice. Longtermism needs more robust philosophical foundations.

ICS Mission: To construct a comprehensive framework spanning carbon-based, silicon-based, and unknown subjects; extending to millennial scales with an intergenerational responsibility system; applicable to deep-space contexts with a governance protocol stack; and capable of revisable yet principled institutional design under deep uncertainty.

Core Framework

The New Three Views

The foundation of ICS rests on reconstructing three fundamental views:

New Cosmology: The universe is relational, not a static container. Observer and observed are fundamentally interconnected.

New Life View: Life is defined by its capacity to create and maintain negentropy, not by specific biological forms. Silicon-based, quantum-based, or hybrid entities can be "alive" in this sense.

New Cognition View: Knowledge is participatory construction, not mirror reflection. "Cognition is intervention, knowledge is collaboration."

Three Normative Principles

Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP): Your freedom must not systematically undermine the conditions for others' (including future subjects') freedom.

Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP): Greater capability entails greater responsibility for cosmic negentropy reserves.

Reversibility Principle (REV): Under deep uncertainty, prioritize reversible pathways.

Three Forbidden Red Lines

FRL-1: Systematic violation of consciousness sovereignty → Absolutely Forbidden

FRL-2: Irreversible large-scale depletion of cosmic negentropy → Absolutely Forbidden

FRL-3: Systematic contraction of future subjects' possibility space → Absolutely Forbidden

Six Indicator Systems

UCS (Universal Consciousness Scale): Measures consciousness complexity of different subjects, providing scientific basis for determining subject qualification.

RFD (Recursive Freedom Degree): Assesses openness of possibility space to evaluate whether actions comply with RFP.

CRV (Commitment Reversibility & Verifiability): Determines decision reversibility to ensure prudence under deep uncertainty.

CDI (Civilization Development Index): Multi-dimensional comprehensive evaluation of civilization development level, beyond GDP.

MBCL (Memetic Biosafety Containment Level): Risk assessment of idea propagation in the realm of ideas.

CSIA (Cosmic-Scale Impact Assessment): Assesses cosmic-level impacts of actions to prevent FRL-2 red line violations.

Our Commitment

This disciplinary definition is an open starting point, not a closed endpoint.

We candidly acknowledge that our theory awaits testing and revision by scientific progress, methods require validation through practical application, institutions must continuously evolve, and value concepts are open to dialogue with diverse cultures.

We commit to welcoming criticism and responding earnestly, maintaining epistemic humility, continuously improving theory and application, and operating with collaborative openness and academic sharing.
