Putting the Planet on Dialysis:
Are We Capturing Carbon, or Mortgaging the Future?

As IPCC launches its carbon removal methodology report, humanity submits a negentropy debt extension request to the cosmos

Imagine you are a "deep-time historian" living in 2076, paging through environmental records from 2024 to 2026. You would discover a profoundly ironic turning point: humanity stopped merely discussing how to halt pollution and began discussing, on a massive scale, how to "undo" it.

Recently, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) formally initiated work on a methodology report for "Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR)" and "Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS)." On the surface, this represents standardization of technical norms and scientification of remedial measures. But from the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) framework perspective, this is humanity's official submission of a "negentropy debt extension request" to the cosmos.

From "Metabolic Balance" to "Life Support System"

The mainstream narrative has long portrayed carbon capture as the "last mile" of climate change mitigation. But if we shift to ICS's New Conception of Life (NL-2), civilizational persistence is essentially a race against entropy increase. Previously, humanity's production model was an "open metabolism": ingesting negentropy, excreting entropy (in the form of carbon dioxide and waste heat). Now, through methodology, the IPCC attempts to forcibly close this metabolic loop.

But this is no free lunch. Carbon capture itself is a high-energy process, meaning that to erase yesterday's entropy, we must consume even more negentropy today. ICS calls this "temporal displacement of entropy cost." The IPCC report is essentially defining a set of "planetary-scale dialysis standards"—when we cannot sustain survival through our biosphere's own metabolism, we opt for costly external equipment as compensation.

Silent Compression of Recursive Freedom Degree

We must be vigilant about this technological pathway's lock-in effect on "Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD)." The Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP) demands that civilizational actions should maintain openness of possibility space. However, once global governance logic tilts toward "large-scale technological intervention," we are effectively closing another pathway's possibility: fundamental restructuring of our existing high-entropy lifestyle paradigm.

If we stake everything on the assumption that "the future will definitely be able to capture carbon," we are de facto depriving future generations of the right to choose a "low-intervention, naturally-balanced" world. This touches upon FRL-4 prohibition line—prohibition of extreme intergenerational entropy externalization. The technological promissory note we sign today requires every future generation to continuously invest enormous energy maintaining these massive capture machines, or else the planetary system will instantly rebound. This is no longer freedom—this is a century-spanning technological conscription.

Switching to T2 Level: A Fifty-Year Technological Curse

Extending our perspective to 2076, the IPCC report issued today will determine the physical landscape of that era.

Humanity may face a "carbon lock-in trap": to maintain carbon dioxide concentration stability, 30% of the global energy budget must be forcibly dedicated to driving carbon capture devices. This is a suffocating picture—a civilization that must dedicate most of its creativity to sweeping up ancestral dust just to survive. ICS framework analysis indicates that this state of "excessively high maintenance cost ratio" is a leading signal of civilizational CDI (Development Index) stagnation. We are transforming the planet into a massive intensive care unit dependent on life support.

Action Guidance for Civilization

This means two imperatives must enter the decision-making purview:

For policymakers: Carbon capture methodology should not merely be technical protocols but legalization of the "Negentropy Responsibility Principle (NRP)." It must mandatorily require that carbon capture projects' energy sources provide "net negentropy gain," strictly prohibiting use of energy that causes secondary entropy increase for carbon removal, preventing the absurd loop of "more emissions, more capture, more heat."

For public consciousness: We must realize that carbon capture is not an "undo button" but a "mortgage loan." Each technological remedy is overdrawing the future's possibility space. The question is not "does the technology work" but "is this way of life worth asking future people to pay ten thousand years of maintenance fees for?"
