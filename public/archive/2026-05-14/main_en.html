I. The Precise Shape of the Event: Don't Be Misled by "Lawful Purpose"

Grasping what happened requires clarity on details that mainstream coverage has largely glossed over.

First, what are IL6 and IL7 networks? IL6 refers to U.S. military networks operating at the "Secret" classification level. IL7 is an informal designation for systems handling Top Secret information and Special Access Programs (SAPs). An AI model granted access to these networks could, in principle, interact with battlefield targeting data, intelligence assessments, and potentially nuclear command-and-control protocols.

Second, what does "any lawful purpose" actually mean in practice? OpenAI's contract reportedly preserves the company's "full discretion" over safety mechanisms. Google's agreement, according to The Information, includes Google consenting to adjust safety filters at government request 鈥?with no legally binding constraints. In effect, Google surrendered its veto over how Gemini is applied in military contexts. Google DeepMind researcher Alex Turner stated plainly on X: "Google has confirmed it cannot exercise a veto and relies on promises that carry no legal weight. It's disgraceful."

Third, why was Anthropic excluded? The Pentagon suggested Anthropic was unwilling to "cooperate." The specifics are more revealing: Anthropic refused unrestricted access to its unreleased model Mythos 鈥?a system described internally as capable of identifying advanced cybersecurity vulnerabilities at a level that could enable offensive cyber operations. Anthropic's position was that without an adequate governance framework, AI systems cannot be permitted to participate in decisions that could cost civilian lives autonomously. The Pentagon's answer was to remove the company from the entire military supply chain.

The signal here is worth reading carefully: when a government discovers that an AI company is attempting to establish ethical limits, its first response is to eliminate that company through market force.

VII. What Does Anthropic's Choice Actually Mean?

A reductive narrative deserves to be rejected: reading Anthropic's refusal as moral posturing or business strategy. Both interpretations diminish what is actually at stake.

What Anthropic refused was not defense sector participation. It refused unrestricted access 鈥?that is, it refused to allow the military to deploy its most advanced AI for "any lawful purpose" without any operationalizable governance constraints or verifiable safety review mechanisms. The substance of this stance is the insistence that AI systems in high-stakes applications must have clear, enforceable safety boundaries, rather than relying on contractual language that essentially says "we trust you will use it well."

From the perspective of the ICS Reversibility Principle (REV), Anthropic's position is: under conditions of deep uncertainty, do not deploy unrecallable systems.
