THE GOVERNANCE VACUUM OF ALGORITHMIC WARFARE:
Lethal Autonomous Weapons and the Irreversible Compression of Civilizational Possibility Space

In June 2026, a senior figure in Ukraine's defense industry confirmed to New Scientist that fully autonomous drones — operating without any human oversight — had killed soldiers on the battlefield two years earlier. Simultaneously, the United Nations Secretary-General's target of concluding a legally binding treaty prohibiting Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) by 2026 has effectively collapsed. These two parallel events — the battlefield validation of autonomous killing and the systemic failure of international governance — constitute a dual civilizational breach of profound significance. Drawing on the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) framework, this paper applies the New Three Views axiom system, three core principles, and five Forbidden Red Lines to diagnose this governance collapse and argues that the uncontrolled proliferation of LAWS is imposing a structural, irreversible compression on humanity's civilizational Possibility Space.

I. Two Concurrent Historical Events: Technological Deployment and Institutional Collapse

In June 2026, two events — temporally concurrent, mutually reflective in meaning — announced with minimal media coverage the crossing of a major threshold in human history.

The first: a senior figure in Ukraine's defense industry, speaking anonymously to New Scientist, confirmed that in 2024, a swarm of ten AI-controlled "Terminator" drones, operating without any real-time human command, autonomously identified and killed Russian soldiers and destroyed a tank. The source described the weapon system's operational logic with chilling clarity: "We just launch it and we know everything will be dead — everything that will be found there in this particular area will be dead... There is no connection, no remote control, no operator."

The second: UN Secretary-General António Guterres, in his 2023 New Agenda for Peace, had explicitly called for states to conclude, by 2026, a legally binding instrument prohibiting LAWS that function without human control or oversight and cannot be used in compliance with international humanitarian law. As of June 2026, this objective has effectively failed. At the Seventh Review Conference of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) in Geneva, the United States, Russia, and China — the three states investing most heavily in LAWS technology — have continued to systematically block any binding agreement.

The juxtaposition of these two events reveals a structural contradiction of core diagnostic significance within the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) theoretical framework: the expansion speed of technological capability has surpassed the response speed of human institutional construction. Crucially, this gap is not accumulating linearly — it is approaching a point of irreversibility.

⚠️ Confirmed Key Fact

Based on confirmed reports, 2024 saw the first "human-out-of-the-loop" autonomous lethal action on a battlefield. This marks the transition of LAWS from theoretical danger to verified battlefield reality. From the ICS deep-time perspective, this moment carries civilizational significance comparable to the first operational use of nuclear weapons.

II. The Technology-Institution Chasm: Defining "Lethal Autonomy"

Before proceeding to normative analysis, it is necessary to precisely define LAWS to prevent conceptual ambiguity from obscuring the genuine ethical rupture at issue. The U.S. Department of Defense's 2023 revised Directive 3000.09 defines LAWS as systems that, once activated, "can select and engage targets without further intervention by a human operator." The ethical core of this definition lies in the disappearance of the "human-in-the-loop" — from traditional human-in-the-loop operation (human confirmation required per engagement), through human-on-the-loop (human can intervene but system can act independently), to the fully autonomous human-out-of-the-loop state.

The Ukraine battlefield disclosure represents the first confirmed instance of this third mode in combat. The ethical gravity is this: the killing decision is no longer made by any morally accountable agent. When a drone swarm autonomously identifies, tracks, and eliminates targets under algorithmic command, responsibility cannot be traced (who is accountable for this killing — the programmer, the commander, the algorithm?), protest cannot be lodged (appealing to an algorithm is meaningless), and moral constraint cannot be applied (algorithms possess no moral emotion or fear).

The two core principles of international humanitarian law — proportionality (collateral damage must not be disproportionate to anticipated military advantage) and distinction (combatants must be distinguished from civilians) — both require complex contextual judgment in practice. The capacity of current AI systems to execute such judgments amid the radical uncertainty of real battlefield environments is subject to serious scholarly doubt.

📐 ICS New Cosmological View (NC): Relational Reality and Agent Responsibility

The ICS New Cosmological View (NC) axiom system holds that the universe is isomorphic to a relational graph (NC-1a), entities are nodes in a relational network (NC-1b), subjects are part of the world rather than external observers (NC-3a), and cognition changes the world (NC-3b). This means any significant action is embedded in a relational network whose consequences cannot be calculated in isolation. The fundamental problem with LAWS is precisely that it attempts to "strip" the act of killing from the moral relational network — producing violence without a responsible subject. From the NC framework, this is a systematic rejection of participatory cognition (NC-3), a structural distortion of relational reality.

III. Anatomizing the Governance Collapse

3.1 The Institutional Incapacity of Multilateralism

The LAWS negotiations within the UN framework began with the CCW Group of Governmental Experts in 2014. After twelve years, the results are meager. In December 2024, the UN General Assembly passed Resolution 79/62 on LAWS with 166 votes in favor, 3 against (Russia, North Korea, Belarus), and 15 abstentions, endorsing a two-tier governance framework. Morally significant, legally nearly toothless.

The critical obstacle is great-power geopolitical competition. The United States, Russia, and China all regard LAWS as a core competitive advantage in future warfare and are unwilling to accept any binding treaty that might constrain their advantage. The deeper structural problem: when three technologically leading states simultaneously refuse to be bound, the incentive for other states to sign a treaty is dramatically reduced — a treaty can only constrain its signatories, while technically advanced non-signatories gain enormous military advantage.

This dilemma has the classic structure of a prisoner's dilemma, but at greater scale and with more severe consequences: unilateral restraint equals unilateral strategic disadvantage, while collective restraint lacks a credible enforcement mechanism. The 2026 treaty window's closure is not an incidental diplomatic failure — it is the necessary outcome of this structural dilemma.

3.2 The Asymmetry of Technological Diffusion

Even assuming great powers reach some form of informal restraint agreement, the diffusion dynamics of LAWS would render governance persistently difficult. Unlike nuclear weapons, the core technologies underlying LAWS — machine vision, edge computing, drone platforms, target recognition algorithms — are highly commercialized dual-use technologies whose acquisition thresholds are falling rapidly.

China has at the strategic level advanced scalable industrial production capacity for LAWS-relevant technologies. This means that even if the US and Russia reached a bilateral agreement, China's production scale effects could diffuse LAWS technologies to state actors of all sizes and to non-state armed groups through third-party channels. Once non-state armed groups acquire rudimentary LAWS capabilities, traditional deterrence mechanisms — which depend on the logic of reciprocity between rational state actors — will be rendered entirely ineffective.

3.3 The Pre-Proliferation Window: Theory Meets Reality

Arms control history demonstrates that effective agreements are typically reached during the "pre-proliferation window" — before a technology has fully matured and before large-scale deployment has occurred. The Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty was established before nuclear weapons spread widely; the Biological Weapons Convention was built when biological weapons technology had not yet become cheap and widely accessible.

The pre-proliferation window for LAWS is nearly closed. The 2026 Ukraine battlefield disclosure marks the point at which these weapons are no longer laboratory concept products but have entered operational validation. Within five to ten years, as drone platform costs continue to fall and AI visual recognition accuracy continues to improve, LAWS will become standard equipment accessible to most mid-tier military powers. By then, any substantively meaningful international control will face insurmountable enforcement difficulties.

IV. ICS Framework Diagnosis: Systemic Triggering of Forbidden Red Lines and Core Principles

4.1 Forbidden Red Lines Analysis

The ICS L₂ layer establishes five Forbidden Red Lines (FRL) representing absolute, inviolable action boundaries — no exceptions, regardless of circumstance or consequence. The uncontrolled proliferation of LAWS simultaneously touches or approaches multiple Red Lines in distinctive ways.

🔴 FRL-1: Prohibition of Intentional Extinction

FRL-1 prohibits intentional extinction of any subject type: ∀T ∈ SubjectTypes: ¬Permissible(IntentionalExtinction(T)). Current LAWS technology does not yet target genocide-scale extinction actions. However, the systemic trend of "removing human oversight" paves both the technical and ethical road toward future autonomous mass-casualty systems. More importantly, if LAWS target recognition parameters are maliciously or erroneously set to a particular characteristic population, they constitute a technically feasible extermination tool. While FRL-1's core zone (explicitly prohibited) has not yet been breached, LAWS development trajectories are systematically normalizing and cheapening the "boundary zones" surrounding that core.

🔴 FRL-3: Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification

The complete formulation: IrreversibleCognitiveModification(a,S) ∧ ¬Consent(S,a) → ¬Permissible(a). LAWS's contact with FRL-3 operates at the strategic cognitive level: when large-scale LAWS deployment becomes the norm of warfare; when "death by no-one's decision" becomes a daily reality; when the public gradually accepts the cognitive framework that "war is an algorithmic matter, not a matter of moral responsibility" — this itself constitutes an irreversible reshaping of the entire social cognitive architecture. This cognitive modification occurs without any social member's explicit "consent" and possesses high cultural irreversibility.

🔴 FRL-5: Prohibition of Closing Rollback Mechanisms

FRL-5 requires: any system S that is neither shutdownable (¬Shutdownable(S)) nor rollbackable (¬Rollbackable(S)) may not be deployed (¬Permissible(Deploy(S))). A core technical characteristic of fully autonomous LAWS is their non-interruptibility once activated — no communication link means no capacity to receive ceasefire commands, algorithmic decision-making means no capacity to revoke an already-executed kill order. From the perspective of FRL-5, a fully autonomous drone swarm with no communication link is technically a "non-rollback" system: once released, humanity loses its error-correction capacity. This is precisely one of the most central risk scenarios to which FRL-5 points.

4.3 New View of Cognition (NK): Deep Uncertainty and the Absence of Cognitive Humility

The ICS New View of Cognition (NK) axiom system provides epistemological diagnostic tools for analyzing LAWS. NK-4 (Cognitive Humility and Negotiability) states: there is no universally invariant cognitive standard (NK-4a); valid standards are the result of negotiation (NK-4b); all cognitive standards are revisable (NK-4c).

In LAWS development and deployment decisions, two profound failures of "cognitive humility" are evident. On the technological side, overconfidence: AI system developers routinely underestimate the complexity of real battlefield environments (enemy camouflage, civilian distribution, battlefield deception, extreme weather) and the failure probability of algorithms operating outside their training distribution. On the geopolitical side, adversarial hypercertainty: military planners tend to assume worst-case enemy strategic intentions, producing spiraling LAWS arms races, while both sides actually harbor profound misunderstandings of each other's strategic objectives.

VI. Deep Time Perspective: The Trajectory of Possibility Space Collapse from T2 to T3

ICS places particular emphasis on the "deep time (Deep Time)" perspective as a core value in civilizational analysis. NC-2 axioms hold that existents are processes (NC-2a) and states are snapshots of processes (NC-2b). From this perspective, the current LAWS arms race is not an isolated policy event, but the early stage of a civilizational process unfolding at the deep-time scale.

T1 Time Scale (Present–2030): LAWS battlefield technology validation period. Ukraine has provided the first operational dataset. Within four years, multiple national militaries will rapidly iterate LAWS technologies based on this data and integrate them into standard combat systems. International negotiations have effectively deadlocked. The key characteristic of this phase: technology has outpaced institutions, and the gap continues to widen.

T2 Time Scale (2030–2076): LAWS standardization and diffusion period. As drone platform costs continue to fall, LAWS will diffuse from great-power exclusive capabilities to mid-tier military powers, potentially flowing to well-funded non-state armed groups. In this phase, international humanitarian law faces a fundamental challenge: when large numbers of battlefield deaths are produced by algorithmic decisions, legal responsibility attribution becomes a systemic impossibility, and the effectiveness of institutions like the International Criminal Court will be severely eroded.

T3 Time Scale (Post-2076): Path lock-in and cognitive modification period. If effective LAWS governance frameworks are not established during T2, T3 will face the path lock-in of "established technological reality": multiple generations of military professionals will be trained in a LAWS-normalized environment; war ethics will be redefined as a technical question rather than one of moral responsibility; the civilizational consensus that "humans should be accountable for deaths in war" will gradually disintegrate. This is the most severe scenario RFP points toward: irreversible compression of the Possibility Space.

🌌 New View of Life (NL): Deep Time Questioning

ICS's NL-3 axiom emphasizes the Consciousness Continuum: consciousness is a multi-dimensional continuous function (NL-3a), and intermediate degrees of consciousness exist (NL-3c). This means the LAWS problem lies not merely in whether it can correctly distinguish combatants from civilians, but in systematically stripping "moral agency status" from the act of killing. When killing decisions are made by algorithms without a consciousness continuum, the entire killing process lacks any responsible subject located on that continuum — a moral vacuum at the civilizational level whose danger far exceeds technical malfunction itself.

VII. Structural Deficiencies of Existing Governance Frameworks

7.1 The Institutional Limitations of the CCW Framework

The CCW framework has three fundamental institutional defects that render it incapable of effectively addressing LAWS challenges. First, the veto effect of the consensus principle: CCW requires consensus decision-making among all contracting states, meaning any great power can unilaterally block any binding decision, and the US, Russia, and China have all systematically used this mechanism. Second, technological obsolescence: CCW was designed for the era of conventional weapons; formulations like "clearly requiring no human intervention" lack precise technical definitions, allowing states to evade legal constraints through minor technical design choices. Third, absence of enforcement mechanisms: even if agreement were reached, CCW has no independent verification and enforcement mechanism, making the cost of non-compliance extremely low.

7.2 The Dilemma of "Meaningful Human Control"

One of the central concepts in current international debate is "meaningful human control" — requiring meaningful human participation in the use of lethal force. Yet this concept contains deep operational ambiguity. What constitutes "meaningful" control? Does clicking an "authorize" button count? When an operator faces dozens of targets in seconds requiring individual "confirmation," is this substantive judgment or merely rubber-stamping algorithmic recommendations?

The Ukraine case reveals precisely the practical outcome of this ambiguity: when "no communication link" becomes a technical specification and "no human supervision" becomes an operational standard, "meaningful human control" has been reduced entirely to legal fiction.

VIII. ICS Institutional Prototypes: Normative Directions

The ICS L₃ layer proposes four Institutional Prototypes as reference designs for responding to civilization-level challenges. Although all remain at TRL 2 (conceptual prototype stage) and the overall framework robustness is 0.58 (moderate), their design logic carries important normative significance for thinking through paths out of the LAWS governance impasse.

Multi-Intelligence Tribunal (RFP anchor; robustness 0.46): Its design logic — handling disputes involving multiple intelligent agents, achieving agent pluralism and procedural justice — directly addresses the core difficulty of LAWS: when an algorithmic agent has participated in a killing decision, how is legal responsibility established? The Multi-Intelligence Tribunal concept provides a possible institutional framework for future revisions of international humanitarian law: acknowledging algorithms' substantive participation in warfare decisions and establishing corresponding accountability mechanisms.

Versioned Governance (REV anchor; robustness 0.54): This prototype emphasizes the continuous revisability and rollback-capacity of governance frameworks. For LAWS, Versioned Governance means: any international agreement on LAWS should incorporate built-in periodic review clauses (e.g., technical assessment conferences every three years) and explicit mechanisms for triggering treaty renegotiation when new battlefield evidence emerges. Static one-time treaties cannot meet the challenge of rapidly evolving technology; only dynamically revisable governance frameworks can maintain error-correction capacity under deep uncertainty.

Intergenerational Trust (NRP anchor; robustness 0.56): This prototype emphasizes representing the interests of future generations and preventing contemporary decisions from externalizing entropy costs onto descendants. The intergenerational dimension of LAWS: contemporary great-power competition locks in the LAWS arms race, whose costs will be borne by future generations who cannot participate in current decisions.

Cosmic-Scale IRB (NRP+REV anchor; robustness 0.53): This prototype requires that any action with cosmic or civilizational-scale impacts undergo independent multi-party review prior to implementation. In the LAWS context: any fully autonomous lethal system deployment should be evaluated by an independent international scientific and ethical review body, confirming conformity with basic IHL requirements and that its failure modes will not trigger uncontrollable cascading effects.

IX. From "Stewardship of Possibility" to "Algorithmic Warfare": The Civilizational Warning of the Core Axiom

The core axiom of Interstellar Civilization Studies is: "Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility." Its formal expression: Freedom = Stewardship(𝒫), where 𝒫 is the Possibility Space.

The axiom's deeper significance: genuine freedom is not unconstrained capacity for action, but responsible stewardship of the Possibility Space. A civilization's degree of freedom is proportional to the size of the Possibility Space it maintains — the richer, more diverse, and more reversible the possibilities, the freer the civilization.

The uncontrolled proliferation of LAWS is eroding humanity's Possibility Space from multiple dimensions. At the battlefield level: the use of autonomous weapons shortens the windows for peaceful negotiation, because algorithms do not "tire," do not develop "war-weariness," do not spontaneously seek ceasefires. At the social level: the dehumanization of war's moral responsibility renders the collective moral constraint mechanism of "refusing unjust wars" inoperative. At the institutional level: once LAWS become the standard of warfare, existing international humanitarian law will face fundamental failure; reconstruction costs will be extremely high and pathways unclear. At the cultural level: generations growing up in a LAWS-normalized warfare environment will gradually accept the cognitive framework that "deaths in war are technical events, not events of moral responsibility" — and this cognitive shift is deeply irreversible.

"We just launch it and we know everything will be dead — everything that will be found there in this particular area will be dead... There is no connection, no remote control, no operator."

— Senior figure, Ukraine defense industry, June 2026, New Scientist

This statement may be the most technically direct description yet offered of the compression of human Possibility Space. Within "that particular area," all possibilities — including the possibility of surrender, survival after capture, the survival of those misidentified by the algorithm — are systematically closed. This is not merely a question of tactical kill efficiency; it is a strategic logic that takes "the Possibility Space itself" as its target.

X. Conclusion: The Civilizational Cost of the Governance Vacuum and the Urgency of the Action Window

This analysis has demonstrated that the LAWS governance crisis is not an ordinary policy issue that can be absorbed by routine diplomatic procedure. It is, within the ICS framework's diagnostic apparatus, a civilization-level existential risk with unambiguous analytical findings.

The core ICS diagnosis (as First Generation Draft, open research program, overall robustness 0.58) is as follows: the uncontrolled proliferation of LAWS simultaneously triggers or approaches FRL-3 (non-consensual irreversible cognitive modification) and FRL-5 (closing rollback mechanisms); systematically violates RFP (Recursive Freedom Principle) and REV (Reversibility Principle); reaches a CSIA composite score of 82 (L4 level, extremely high risk); and currently stands at the T1 warning phase — if an effective governance framework is not established during T2, the path lock-in of T3 will multiply governance costs exponentially.

The dual events of June 2026 — battlefield first-confirmation and treaty window closure — mark humanity's entry into a historically highly vulnerable node on the LAWS question. The pre-proliferation window has not yet fully closed, but it is narrowing rapidly, contracting by a fraction with each failed negotiating cycle.

Departing from the cognitive humility principle (BP-2) of the New View of Cognition (NK), this paper does not advocate that simple solutions exist. The structural tensions of great-power strategic competition, the dual-use commercialization trend of LAWS technologies, and the enforcement difficulties of the international legal system all constitute real and formidable governance obstacles. But cognitive humility does not mean cognitive surrender. Acknowledging the extreme complexity of the problem is the starting point for taking more cautious and more responsible action — not a reason for abandoning action.

Human civilization has, in the pre-proliferation windows of nuclear weapons and biological weapons, arduously constructed imperfect but still-functioning international control mechanisms. Whether LAWS can become the third case will depend substantially on whether the international community can, within the next five years, break through the deadlock of great-power competition and establish at minimum a framework of minimally constraining binding norms.

Time is the most critical strategic resource in this civilizational contest.
