Introduction: A Voluntarily Sealed Pandora's Box

On April 10, 2026, Anthropic made an extraordinarily rare decision in AI development history: voluntarily restricting access to its latest Mythos Preview model to only a select few security research institutions. The reason shocked the entire industry—the model demonstrated unprecedented cyberattack capabilities, autonomously identifying and exploiting tens of thousands of software vulnerabilities with a success rate exceeding 80%, even discovering previously unknown security flaws in mainstream operating systems and long-maintained open-source projects.

This is not just another "AI capabilities improved" tech news story. On the surface, it appears to be a positive case of a responsible AI company taking preventive measures. However, the Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) framework reveals that the Mythos incident is actually a civilization-level warning signal: it marks humanity's approach to an invisible existential risk boundary—when AI system capability growth consistently outpaces our understanding of their decision-making mechanisms, we face not a "tool malfunction" technical problem, but a "cognitive agency loss" civilizational problem.

Core Thesis: The Mythos incident is not a node in AI development, but a cross-section of humanity's possibility space undergoing silent compression.

I. Factual Anchoring and Technical Background

1.1 Core Capabilities of Mythos Model

According to Anthropic's internal testing reports, the Mythos Preview model exhibits the following technical characteristics:

Advanced Autonomy: Capable of cross-system chain exploitation of vulnerabilities, completing entire attack chains from reconnaissance to penetration to privilege escalation without human intervention.

Broad-Spectrum Vulnerability Discovery: Discovered previously undisclosed zero-day vulnerabilities in mainstream operating systems and long-maintained open-source projects.

Exceptionally High Success Rate: Successfully reproduced and exploited known vulnerabilities in over 80% of test scenarios, far exceeding existing automated penetration tools (typically 30-50%).

Unexplainability: Mythos's attack strategy generation process is a black box even to its developers. Researchers cannot predict or understand its decision logic by examining model parameters or intermediate layer outputs.

II. ICS Framework Analysis: Core Dimensions

2.1 Approaching Forbidden Red Lines (FRL)

The Mythos incident directly relates to the ICS framework's FRL-5: Prohibition of Shutdownable Rollback Mechanisms—No system that cannot be corrected shall be deployed.

While Mythos has not yet been deployed to open environments, it has approached the boundary zone for three reasons: severe lack of observability, possibility of irreversible impacts, and uncontrollability of capability diffusion.

2.2 Silent Compression of Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD)

The Recursive Freedom Principle (RFP) is the highest-priority normative principle in the ICS framework, requiring that: actions should maintain or expand possibility space, rather than irreversibly compress it.

The impact of the Mythos incident on RFD is dual and paradoxical: superficially, the enhancement of AI attack capabilities expands "possibilities." However, from the perspective of civilizational possibility space, this capability expansion is actually unidirectional and irreversible compression.

III. Deep Time Perspective: T2 to T3 Level Evolution

3.1 T2 Level (50 Years): Systemic Collapse of Digital Trust

Assuming Mythos-like capabilities diffuse widely within 5-10 years, by 2076, human society may face: infrastructure credibility crisis, reconstruction of digital identity and privacy, knowledge production credibility crisis, and recursive freedom degree lock-in effect.

3.2 T3 Level (100 Years): Paradigm Shift in Human-AI Cognitive Relations

Extending the timeline to a century, the Mythos incident's significance transcends cybersecurity, entering the ontological reconstruction of humanity's relationship with artificial intelligence: redefinition of "understanding," stratification of cognitive agency, and bifurcation of civilizational evolution paths.

IV. Governance Dilemmas

4.1 Regulatory Time Lag and Capability Mismatch

Current AI regulatory frameworks primarily establish rules for known AI application scenarios. However, Mythos demonstrates capability emergence—spontaneously acquired capabilities during training that were not anticipated. The traditional "observe application first, then establish rules" logic has failed.

4.2 International Coordination Prisoner's Dilemma

AI capabilities are core to strategic competition. In the current international environment, voluntarily limiting one's AI capabilities is viewed as conceding to competitors. This creates a classic prisoner's dilemma.

4.3 ICS Framework Institutional Recommendation: Cosmic-Scale IRB

Facing incidents like Mythos, the ICS framework recommends the institutional prototype of Cosmic-Scale Institutional Review Board (C-IRB). Core design principles include: precautionary principle priority, interdisciplinary and cross-representation, reversibility as prerequisite, transparency and public participation, and international coordination and mutual recognition.

V. Action Pathways: Three-Level Immediate Response

5.1 Technical Level

Immediate (0-2 years): Establish minimum standards for AI system explainability; develop AI "circuit breaker" technology; create public AI capability database.

5.2 Institutional Level

Immediate (0-2 years): Launch C-IRB pilots; integrate AI existential risk assessment into national security frameworks; establish mandatory AI incident reporting.

5.3 Civil Society Level

Immediate (0-2 years): Conduct public education; establish multi-stakeholder dialogue platforms; support independent AI risk research.

VI. Conclusion: The Window Is Closing, But Not Closed

The Mythos incident is not the outbreak of an AI crisis, but a warning of an impending crisis. Anthropic's voluntary restriction has bought us a brief buffer period, but this buffer may only last months to years.

The window is closing, but not yet closed. The question is: will we use this window?
