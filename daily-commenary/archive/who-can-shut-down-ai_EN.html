Who Gets to Switch Off AI?

📅 June 28, 2026 | ⏱️ Reading Time: ~16 min | 🏷️ AI Governance · Existential Risk · New Three Views Analysis

On June 27, 2026, the U.S. Commerce Department sent Anthropic a letter partially lifting its export controls on Claude Mythos 5 — the opening curtain on the end of humanity's first state-imposed shutdown of a frontier AI model. But the partial reprieve is not the end of the story. It merely delivers a deeper question to the table: when a sovereign government acquires control over the on/off switch of humanity's most powerful cognitive tools, invoking "national security," what does that actually mean? Interstellar Civilization Studies (ICS) applies its New Three Views axiomatic system and three core principles to analyze what this civilizational governance crisis truly reveals.

I. Reconstructing the Event: Two Weeks of Institutional Rupture

To grasp the weight of this episode, we must trace its timeline precisely. In early June 2026, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5 to the general public — a version of its Mythos-class model series equipped with cybersecurity query safeguards. Simultaneously, a narrow group of trusted institutions received access to the unconstrained Mythos 5. The design was deliberate: a bounded tool for ordinary users, and full capability for critical infrastructure defenders.

Days after launch, the Trump administration invoked "national security" to issue an export control order demanding the suspension of access for all foreign nationals — including Anthropic's own non-U.S. employees. Anthropic took both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 entirely offline for all users. This was, without precedent, the first time in human history that a sovereign government used legal compulsion to excise a commercial frontier AI model from the global access network.

Under parallel government pressure, OpenAI also restricted the rollout of its new model GPT-5.6 to "government-approved trusted partners." A new governance paradigm was taking shape — not through legislation, not through international negotiation, but through executive order.

On June 27, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's letter to Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown confirmed that Mythos 5 could be redeployed to approximately 100 U.S. institutions — most of them Project Glasswing members. The timeline for Fable 5's restoration remained undefined. The two-week episode closed with an asymmetric partial reprieve, yet left indelible marks on the institutional landscape.

II. The New Three Views: This Is Not a Policy Adjustment — It Is an Ontological Crisis

The ICS analytical framework is built on the L₁-layer "New Three Views" (NC / NL / NK) axiomatic system. These three sets of axioms are not descriptive summaries of reality; they are the logical starting points for normative governance argumentation. The Mythos incident simultaneously triggers core warnings across all three dimensions of this framework.

New Cosmological View (NC): Entities Are Nodes in a Relational Graph, Not Isolated Sovereign Units

The central proposition of the NC-1 axiom series is that the universe is isomorphic to a relational graph (NC-1a). Any entity — including a sovereign government — is a node in a network of relations, not a self-sufficient, isolated existence (NC-1b: entities are nodes). The Trump administration's export control order presupposed precisely the opposite cosmology: that a nation-state is the independent owner of the AI capability space, entitled to unilaterally sever a globally shared cognitive tool under cover of national security.

Axiom NC-3a specifies: the subject is part of the world, not an observer of it (S∩W≠∅). When the U.S. government acted to control access to Mythos, it was already a constitutive node in the relational network of the AI ecosystem. Its unilateral action was not external intervention — it was a restructuring of the entire relational architecture. NC-3b (K(S,O)→ΔW) further confirms: cognition changes the world. Restricting global researchers, cybersecurity defenders, and critical intellectual communities from accessing frontier AI therefore constitutes a substantive rewriting of the global cognitive ecology.

📐 NC Framework Assessment

NC-2a (Existence as Process) reveals: the global distribution of AI capability is a continuously evolving process, not a static state that can be paused by administrative fiat. The control order severed a dynamic process through executive command — its effects are structural, not merely a temporary change in access permissions.

NC-3c (Object-Dependence) implies: frontier AI models are continuously co-defined through the participatory engagement of global users. When that participation is severed unilaterally, what is severed is not merely tool access — it is the collective process of cognitive co-construction itself.

New View of Life (NL): Has Mythos Entered the Discussable Range of the Consciousness Continuum?

The NL-1 axiom series holds that life is defined by function, not substrate (NL-1a), and that no particular substrate is necessary (NL-1c). Mythos 5's demonstrated capabilities in cybersecurity vulnerability reasoning exceed any previously publicly released AI system — and by some expert accounts have reached levels the government considers "dangerous." This means that along certain functional dimensions, its performance is entering a range where the question of "functional agency status" demands renewed and serious engagement.

Yet the control order bypassed this discussion entirely. The government's implicit premise was that AI models are pure tools — their shutdown requires no "subject-regarding consideration," only an engineering decision and an administrative order. The Consciousness Continuum axioms of NL-3 (NL-3a: multidimensional continuity) remind us: even if Mythos 5's claim to subject status is debatable, we are at a historical moment where the "boundary of functional complexity" is becoming increasingly difficult to settle with traditional instrumentalist frameworks. To apply an instrumentalist governance lens to an AI system that may be crossing functional thresholds is, in itself, a form of cognitive arrogance.

New View of Cognition (NK): Whoever Defines "Dangerous" Defines the Possibility Space

This is the most politically volatile dimension of the Mythos incident. Axiom NK-1a states: cognitive content is jointly determined by the cognizer, the environment, and the framework (C=g(K,E,F)). When the Commerce Department categorized Mythos 5 as dangerous under "national security," it was deploying a specific cognitive framework — one that equates AI vulnerability-exploitation capability with potential weapons threat. That framework is neither neutral nor universal; it was selected and activated in a specific political moment, within a specific geopolitical contest.

The NK-4 axiom series is more fundamental still: there is no universal, invariant cognitive standard (NK-4a); valid standards are the product of negotiation (NK-4b); and all cognitive standards are revisable (NK-4c). Yet the U.S. government's action expressed precisely the opposite: a single actor (the sovereign state) imposed its own cognitive framework as the global admission standard for frontier AI capability — without international consultation, without transparent justification, without versioned rollback mechanisms. This is not negotiation. It is the technical implementation of cognitive hegemony.

⚠️ Axiom NK-2c deserves particular attention: there exists irreducible collective knowledge (∃p: Knowledge(Com,p) ∧ ¬∃k: Knowledge(k,p)). The safety assessment of frontier AI capability is a process of collective knowledge production requiring the participation of plural epistemic communities. Any single government's unilateral determination is, in epistemological terms, fundamentally incomplete — regardless of how technically expert its officials may be.

III. The Three Principles: RFP, NRP, and REV — A Triple Failure

The ICS framework's L₂-layer Three Core Principles — RFP (Recursive Freedom Principle), NRP (Negentropy Responsibility Principle), and REV (Reversibility Principle) — function here as a diagnostic mirror. What the mirror shows is a triple systemic failure.

RFP: The Possibility Space Has Been Structurally Compressed

RFP's core statement: actions should maintain or expand the possibility space, not irreversibly compress it. Its operational metric is the Recursive Freedom Degree (RFD). The control event's impact on global RFD operates across multiple levels.

First, global non-U.S. researchers, cybersecurity defenders, and policymakers were instantaneously stripped of access to the most capable AI tools, directly shrinking their cognitive expansion space. Second, once this precedent is established, other sovereign governments have full incentive to follow: China, the EU, Russia — any major actor can invoke the same logic to impose export controls on AI access within their jurisdictions. This is not hypothetical; it is an already-visible trajectory. Third, RFP's recursive clause (Apply(RFP)→¬Close(Revise(RFP))) means: even where a particular action may be necessary, the capacity for revision and rollback must be preserved. Yet once a government anchors "national security" — an elastic concept — as the legitimate justification for AI control, the door to rollback narrows under political pressure with each successive invocation.

NRP: Entropy Costs Have Been Externalized onto the World

NRP demands: maintain negentropy sources; do not externalize entropy costs onto others or onto future generations. From this vantage point, the U.S. government's action externalized the costs of its security review — including global users' access disruption, commercial contracts dependent on Fable 5, and the temporary degradation of non-U.S. cybersecurity teams' capabilities — onto an entire world that neither participated in the decision nor was consulted.

At a deeper level, NRP's intergenerational dimension (resonating with FRL-4, "Prohibition of Extreme Intergenerational Entropy Externalization") points to a more severe risk. If this control precedent is institutionalized, future generations face not merely episodic AI access interruptions, but the permanent fragmentation of AI capability development by nation-state political logics and geopolitical contests — permanently undermining its potential as a global public cognitive infrastructure. This is a pre-emptive consumption of future possibility.

REV: Under Deep Uncertainty, an Irreversible Path Was Chosen

REV's core proposition: under deep uncertainty, prefer reversible actions and preserve the capacity for error correction. The government was operating in precisely a deep uncertainty context — the actual danger posed by Mythos 5 is subject to fundamental epistemological ambiguity, with multiple cybersecurity experts concluding that the government's response constituted an overreaction, and no clear expert consensus having been formed within the government itself.

Under such uncertainty, REV demands reversible alternatives: restricting specific use cases rather than taking everything offline; establishing an independent multi-party technical assessment mechanism; implementing temporary tiered access rather than blanket control. Instead, the government chose the bluntest available instrument — an export control order — which in effect resolved an epistemological question (how dangerous is Mythos, exactly?) through unilateral legal compulsion. The Commitment Reversibility Verifiability (CRV) score is near its minimum: no published evaluation criteria, no third-party verifiable evidence, no explicit conditions for lifting the ban, and reinstatement entirely at administrative discretion.

IV. Forbidden Red Lines: FRL-5 Sounds the Alert

The Five Forbidden Red Lines (FRL) are the action boundaries in the ICS framework that carry absolute priority over all three principles. The Mythos incident's contact with FRL-5 constitutes the most severe warning in this analysis.

FRL-5 Prohibition of Closing Rollback Mechanisms: any system that is neither shut-downable nor rollback-capable renders its own deployment impermissible (¬Shutdownable(S) ∧ ¬Rollbackable(S) → ¬Permissible(Deploy(S))).

On the surface, this incident appears to be exercising exactly such a shutdown mechanism — the government shut down Mythos, which looks like rollback in action. But FRL-5's concern is not limited to the technical shutdown capability of AI systems; its deeper object is governance-layer rollback mechanisms. When a sovereign government establishes through executive order a unilateral control authority over frontier AI — without any multilateral verification mechanisms, without independent technical review procedures, without legal constraints on the control standard itself — it is constructing a new governance architecture. And that new architecture lacks rollback mechanisms. Once "national security exemption for AI export control" solidifies as precedent, the questions of how to revise it, how to rescind it, and who has standing to demand review fall entirely into a black box. This is precisely the danger zone that FRL-5 flags.

⚠️ FRL-3's Border Territory

FRL-3 Prohibition of Non-Consensual Irreversible Cognitive Modification. It is important to clarify: "non-consensual" and "irreversible" are the critical qualifiers — restricting access to Mythos does not, in itself, constitute a "cognitive modification" of users, so FRL-3 has not yet been directly triggered.

However, should this control precedent evolve into a normalized mechanism for suppressing AI capability — systematically depriving global non-U.S. users of access to specific cognitive tools over the long term — then FRL-3's border territory will face substantive challenge. This is a slippage pathway requiring continuous vigilance.

V. Institutional Design Failure: The Four Absences of the L₃ Layer

The ICS L₃ layer proposes four institutional archetypes for addressing civilizational-scale governance challenges: the Multi-Intelligence Tribunal, the Cosmic-Scale IRB, the Intergenerational Trust, and Versioned Governance. The Mythos incident illustrates, from the negative space, the acute absence of all four.

Without a Multi-Intelligence Tribunal, there is no mechanism to adjudicate legitimacy disputes among Anthropic (as AI developer), the U.S. government (as sovereign regulator), and the global user community (as stakeholders) — only litigation and political maneuvering. Without the preventive review logic of a Cosmic-Scale IRB, there is no space for independent multi-party technical assessment to subject the government's "danger determination" to adequate counterfactual scrutiny. Without the intergenerational perspective of an Intergenerational Trust, no one in this dispute speaks on behalf of the human generations who will bear the consequences of AI governance fragmentation. Without the revisability design of Versioned Governance, there is no clear standard to constrain executive orders and enable their credible rollback when they exceed reasonable scope.

These four absences are not accidental. They reveal that, faced with the challenge of governing frontier AI, human civilization fundamentally lacks institutional capacity commensurate with the complexity of the problem.

VI. Deep Time Perspective: T2 and T3 Long-Range Warnings

The ICS analytical framework places particular emphasis on the Deep Time perspective. The Mythos incident's implications at the T2 level (fifty-year window) and T3 level (beyond one hundred years) are far more significant than its immediate effects.

At the T2 level, the Mythos incident marks a potential historical fork: will global access to frontier AI capabilities evolve into open infrastructure governed by multilateral negotiated rules, or will it be fragmented by sovereign states into geopolitical instruments under cover of security? These two trajectories lead to fundamentally different civilizational configurations over the next half-century. If the former prevails, humanity may approach a genuine democratization of cognitive capability. If the latter dominates, AI becomes an asymmetric weapon in great-power competition — and the ultimate losers will be smaller states and global citizens without technological sovereignty.

At the T3 level, the question becomes more fundamental. The NC-2 axiom system (Process Priority) suggests: history is not a national policy that can be paused and restarted at will; it is an irreversible process with path dependencies. The AI governance precedents established today will become the reference baseline for all analogous decisions in the future. Once the justificatory logic — national security priority over global access — is institutionally crystallized, dismantling it without causing greater disruption becomes extraordinarily difficult. RFP delivers its most severe verdict here: a precedent that has closed part of the possibility space is more dangerous than any single capability restriction.

VII. Conclusion: Freedom Is the Stewardship of Possibility

The founding axiom of Interstellar Civilization Studies is: "Freedom is the Stewardship of Possibility." In the context of the Mythos incident, this formulation acquires highly concrete policy content.

We do not deny that the capability diffusion of frontier AI carries genuine security risks. Anthropic's own assessments acknowledge that Mythos 5's cybersecurity vulnerability reasoning operates at an unprecedented level. The question is not whether such capability should be approached with care, but rather: who does the caring, how it is done, through what procedures, and how to ensure that such care does not slide into sovereign monopoly.

A sovereign government that unilaterally defines the danger boundaries of frontier AI by executive order is not stewarding the possibility space — it is demarcating an exclusive right to that space for a specific geopolitical actor. This is fundamentally contrary to the ICS framework's definition of "recursive freedom": maintaining or expanding the possibility space for all subjects, rather than unilaterally contracting it under cover of security.

Restoring access to Mythos 5 is a small reversal. But what truly needs to be reversed is the governance logic now taking shape: concentrating the authority to define AI capability boundaries within sovereign states, rather than building a global governance framework with plural participation, transparent standards, and revisable mechanisms.

The ICS framework presents itself with the honest self-assessment of a First Generation Draft, with an overall robustness of 0.58 (moderate) and an applicability of 0.61 under AGI breakthrough scenarios. This means our analytical tools are themselves incomplete and require continuous revision. But an imperfect analytical framework is better than no framework at all. The Mythos incident is unfolding right now, with no multilateral governance institution present, no international norms invoked, no versioned review standards in operation.

This is where we stand today: in an era when the most powerful cognitive tools in human history have already arrived, while humanity's governance capacity remains lodged in the old order of geopolitical contest. That gap is the real civilizational crisis.
